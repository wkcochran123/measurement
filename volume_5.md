## Audience

Readers who do not want the theorem first, the physics first, the
compiler first, or the trace first, but can follow necessity when it
arrives as language. This is the book for readers who understand an
argument by hearing the names become unavoidable.

A reader who has met `DISTINGUISHABLE` in a checklist before should
hear in the rhetorical procession the same obligation that
checklist already trusts. A reader who has never opened a Lean file
should still feel the pressure of each name as it arrives.

## Claim

The class names in the Lean code already form a public rhetoric. They
are not implementation labels. They are verdicts. Each name marks a
condition the argument had to satisfy before the next move was
admissible. A reader who hears the names in order hears the argument
in its load-bearing public form.

The book proceeds through names. Not definitions first. Not proofs
first. Names first.

## What This Volume Is

Volumes 1-4 ask the reader to enter through a discipline:

- Volume 1 asks for mathematical patience.
- Volume 2 asks for physical intuition.
- Volume 3 asks for algorithmic imagination.
- Volume 4 asks for trust in a transcript.

Volume 5 asks for ordinary language.

This volume is not a glossary. A glossary explains words one by one.
This book lets the words force one another. The reader should feel
the sequence: "of course it has to be called that, because the
previous name made the next obligation visible."

## Why This Is A Gauge

A rhetorical gauge measures what the argument can make public.

The mathematical gauge proves. The physical gauge measures. The
algorithmic gauge computes. The reference trace gauge witnesses. The
rhetorical gauge persuades by necessity of naming. The experimental
history gauge (Volume 6) shows what the history of experiment taught
the record to trust.

This volume does not soften the formal project. It performs a
different calibration. It asks whether the public name of each formal
condition carries the right pressure. A bad name hides an
obligation. A good name exposes it.

## Section Kernel

Volume 5 should not invent a new section genre. Volume 6 uses the
`phenom` form from the instrument book because it carries historical
named effects. Volume 5 carries class names, not historical effects,
so its kernel is different: the six-slot rhetorical pattern.

Every Chapter 2 section uses the same six-slot kernel:

```text
1. A public scene of pressure.        (the human reader's foothold)
2. The class name.                    (code font, allowed to sit alone)
3. Why the name had to appear.        (the obligation; not a definition dump)
4. The used-car or flight example.    (one new particular per section)
5. An independent domain example.     (one or two sentences; not cars)
6. The next pressure.                 (handoff as causal pressure)
```

Load-bearing refusal anchors carry a seventh slot:

```text
6a. Refusal sketch.                   (one or two sentences; what the gauge denies)
6.  Next pressure.
```

The kernel prevents three failure modes:

- **Glossary drift.** A definition that arrives before pressure
  becomes a glossary entry; the reader does not hear necessity. Slot
  1 (pressure) before slot 3 (why the name appears) forces necessity
  before definition.
- **Anecdote drift.** A running example that becomes a story rather
  than a particular under the name. Slot 4 (used-car or flight)
  imposes "one new particular per section" -- it accumulates rather
  than narrates.
- **Domain capture.** A name that only works inside the cars or only
  inside the flight. Slot 5 (independent example) proves the name
  carries beyond either thread.

## House Rule

Do not define too soon.

Each section begins with a human pressure, then names the class that
answers it. The definition may come later, but the reader should
first feel why the word had to appear. The section should not say
"in Lean, this is a typeclass" until the name has already done its
rhetorical work.

For the running example, begin at the driveway, the title envelope,
the shop bay, the scan tool, the lift, the test drive, the pricing
sheet, or the purchase table before turning toward code. The
practical pressure makes the name audible without importing a fight
that belongs to another domain.

## Example Architecture

Three kinds of examples.

**Opening gate example: a pre-flight checklist.** Before takeoff,
the crew names aircraft, runway, fuel, flaps, trim, clearance,
weather, and final readiness. This is a named gate inside a larger
trusted apparatus. It shows that public names already carry
obligations in ordinary life. The checklist is not a one-shot prop.
The plane it gates is held in reserve through Movement IV and
returns at the Movement V promotion below.

**Running example, Movements I-IV: a used-car inspection before
purchase.** A buyer wants to know whether to buy, walk away, or
renegotiate. The mechanic checks the VIN, title, service records,
OBD-II codes, frame, tires, fluids, compression, brakes, test
drive, comparable prices, and seller claims. The final sentence is
not "the car's Truth is possessed." It is "under this inspection,
the buyer may infer that the car is worth buying at this price."

**Promoted thread, Movements V-VI: the same checklist's airplane,
now in flight.** The names after `ACOLYTE` ask for transport across
distance, contact with the real, locality, universality, halting,
and compilation into a record. A car inspection cannot carry that
weight without straining; a flight can. Cockpit voice recorder,
contact with the runway on landing, the specific airport, the
airspace rules, the wheels-down moment, the flight data recorder,
the post-flight report -- these scale to the cosmic register
Movements V-VI demand. Same instrument family, same crew
discipline, same closed question -- now lifted into the air.

The running question, in two registers:

```text
May the buyer infer that this car is worth buying at the offered price?
May the controller infer that this flight may be cleared, flown, and closed?
```

Each class section also carries an independent example from a third
domain (medicine, kitchens, archives, courts, music, shops,
libraries, weather, farms, construction). The running thread gives
continuity. The independent example proves the name is not a car or
a flight metaphor.

## A Note On Satirical Names

Do not apologize for the satirical names. Do not sand them smooth.
Show why the system kept them.

`GUNGAN`, `FINITE_ELEPHANT`, `BULLSHIT`, `PROPAGANDA`, `ACOLYTE`,
and `BigRedDogProcess` are not polished academic labels. That is
part of their power. They came out of the writing in the way the
manga records -- frustration, misfire, second draft, retained
because the first label kept being right.

`BULLSHIT` is especially important. It is not merely a joke. It
marks the moment when overhead becomes measurable. Once an argument
needs a meter for its own inflation, the name is doing honest work.

Of these six, `BigRedDogProcess` is a process, not a class, and
rides as a sidecar inside the `LOCAL` section rather than getting
its own section. The other five each receive a section in Chapter 2.

public. Class headers are tagged with a Bullshit meter reading.

pending current-Lean confirmation):

```text

BULLSHIT          2733
ACOLYTE           7699

```

The remaining 32 readings must be filled from the current Lean
source before drafting commits margin annotations. The drafter
should not interpolate or invent readings: a fabricated meter
breaks the book's own honesty principle. If a class does not yet
have a verified reading, the section is drafted without the margin
annotation and the annotation is added in the second pass.

The comment on `class BULLSHIT` declares the quantity *strictly*
conserved -- a conservation law in the same grammatical form as
energy or momentum. This is the joke that ties the satirical names
to the rest of the project: the formalization counted its own
bullshit, accumulated it, and made the accumulation public through
the comments.

Format: a small right-margin annotation at each section header,
like `[meter ~ 2733]`. No commentary on the number. The number is
the gag. The accumulating climb from 9 to 23964 is the rhetorical
book's most efficient second argument.

## Three-Chapter Shape

This volume does not keep the eleven-chapter technical skeleton.
The public rhetorical form is simpler:

1. **Scope.** What kind of claim can a name carry?
2. **The Argument.** Each class name is a section, in order.
3. **The Absence Of Truth In The Presence Of The Question.** What
   remains when all the necessary names have been spoken?

The second chapter is the book's body. The first chapter licenses
the method. The third chapter refuses the false comfort that naming
has captured Truth itself.

Use the pre-flight checklist as the chapter's first public prop.
The checklist is brief, named, disciplined, and nested inside a
process whose stakes the reader already understands. The chapter
should teach the reader how to hear class names: not as labels
pasted onto code, but as obligations the code accepts.

The canonical case for "Name As Obligation" is calibration. The
class `LOGICAL` carries `ekg : Calibration.EKG`. That field matters
rhetorically before it matters technically. Logic is not
free-floating cleverness. It carries a scale.

The "Name As Public Argument" section should walk the first three
names in the procession -- `DISTINGUISHABLE`, then `ADMISSIBLE`,
then `COUNTABLE` -- and show that the sequence is already an
argument the public can hear. Each step is forced by the one
before it.

  not carry it.

## Chapter 3 - The Absence Of Truth In The Presence Of The Question

Closed question: what remains when all the necessary names have been

This chapter must hand the public reader the project's deepest
refusal without making them open Book 3. The selection rule that
decides which admissible history is the one actually carried
forward requires the kind of information measurement (Kolmogorov
complexity) that no general-purpose machine can compute. The
compiler that built this volume's formalization cannot perform that
selection either. The procession's quiet final line is therefore
not "we have arrived at Truth" but "even the instrument that wrote
this book cannot own the rule it just named." The buyer's
walking-away is the homely echo of that cosmological refusal.

The car and the flight should both remain visible. The buyer does
not own the Truth of the vehicle forever. The controller does not
own the Truth of the flight forever. Each can only answer the
current practical question under the current record.

Drafting note for `TRUTH` and `WITNESSED` in Chapter 2 and for 3.1
in Chapter 3: do not flatten institutional contingency into regime
labels. The same discipline that lets a buyer walk away from a
particular car lets honest inference survive across very different
institutional conditions; do not pretend the institutions are
identical. (Volume 6 Chapter 4 carries the Lavoisier/Lysenko load
of this point; Volume 5 should not duplicate it but should not

## Source Policy

Volume 5 is the public-facing volume. What it may cite is constrained.

**May cite (sparingly, in code font):**

- Class names from the Lean source. These ARE the book's subject.
- Class field names like `ekg : Calibration.EKG` when the field
  names matter rhetorically.
- The Bullshit meter readings, only those verified against current
  Lean source.

**May cite (rarely, in body prose):**

- The instrument book by chapter or `phenom` label, when a reader
  who wants the formal version should know where to look.
- Volume 6 episodes by name, when a historical example is exactly
  the thing the rhetorical section is about.

**Must not cite:**

- Lean proof bodies. Quote class headers; do not quote proofs.
- Mathlib names. The book is for readers who have never opened a
  proof assistant.
- BibTeX citations from the instrument book. Volume 5 inherits
  through Volume 2; primary-source discipline belongs to Volume 6.

**Must not fabricate:**

- Bullshit meter readings. If the reading is not yet in the source,
  the margin annotation is omitted in the first pass and added in
  the second.
- Class field names. If a class does not yet have a `ekg`
  calibration field, the section is drafted without that reference.
- Apparatus details. The car and the flight are the apparatus
  examples; do not invent technical apparatus.

## Structural Risks

1. **Glossary drift.** A reader who hears the definition before the
   pressure has read a glossary entry, not the necessary name. The
   Section Kernel slot order (pressure before name before why)
   prevents this if the drafter respects the order.
2. **Anecdote drift in the running examples.** The used-car and
   the flight are running threads, not stories. Each section adds
   one new particular and stops. If a section starts to retell
   the whole car or the whole flight, the drafter has lost the
   thread.
3. **Voice mismatch with technical volumes.** Volume 5's voice is
   the most plainspoken in the project. If a section starts to
   sound like Volume 1 or Volume 3, the rhetorical book has
   imported a register that does not fit. Test: would a reader
   who has never read Volume 1 follow this sentence without
   external context? If no, rewrite.
4. **Marginalia fabrication.** The Bullshit meter must not carry
   invented numbers. A fabricated meter breaks the book's own
   honesty principle in the form the book chose for itself. If a
   number is not verified, leave the annotation blank in pass one.
5. **Satirical-name apology.** The satirical names are the book's
   sharpest move. Apologizing for them sands them smooth. Show why
   the system kept them; do not soften them.
6. **Krylov violation.** Do not draft Chapter 2 sections out of
   procession order. Do not draft Chapter 3 before Chapter 2 is
   in place. Do not draft the closing anchor `INFERRED` before
   the working sections that lead to it. The procession is the
   iteration; the iteration cannot run out of order.

## Drafting Method

For each chapter, in order (Ch 1 -> Ch 2 -> Ch 3):

1. **Draft sections in order against the Section Kernel.** Before
   drafting each section, restate the section's tier (Working
   ~250, Anchor ~400, Closing anchor ~450) and the slot
   allocations. The kernel is the operational target.
2. **For anchor sections, restate the concept sub-budget out
   loud before drafting.** Pressure 60 / Why 140 / Running 100 /
   Refusal 40 / Independent 40 / Next 20. The sub-budget is the
   anti-sprawl discipline.
3. **Concatenate sections into the chapter file in procession
   order.** Each section drops into its slot; no later section is
   started until the current one is in place.
4. **Movement landings drafted after the surrounding sections
   exist.** The landing is a hinge between completed material; do
   not draft a landing into a vacuum.
5. **Chapter opening drafted last.** The opening should not
   promise a neat story; it should name the obligation the chapter
   actually performed.
6. **Move to the next chapter.** Do not revise the previous
   chapter beyond typo fixes; revisiting later breaks the budget
   discipline.

## Drafting Order

Per `three_gauges.md` "Drafting Order: The Krylov Constraint,"
chapters and procession sections are drafted in forward order.

1. **Draft Chapter 1 first.** Three sections (`Name as Instrument`,
   `Name as Obligation`, `Name as Public Argument`), opening, and
   handoff. The chapter licenses the method that Chapter 2 will
   walk.
2. **Draft Chapter 2 in procession order.** Section 2.1 (`Fact`)
   first, then 2.2 (`DISTINGUISHABLE`), then in order through
   2.37 (`INFERRED`). Movement landings drafted between movements
   only after both surrounding movements are in place.
3. **Draft Chapter 3 last.** The chapter must inherit the
   calibration the procession has established; drafting it earlier
   forces the closing move to guess at what Chapter 2 will earn.
4. **Front matter drafted last.** The preface, the reader's note,
   the bullshit-meter note, the satirical-names note -- all
   drafted after the body so they refer to actual material.

Within Chapter 2: section 2.k cannot be drafted before section
2.(k-1). The procession is the Krylov iteration. The hardest
sections (`BULLSHIT`, `PROPAGANDA`, `ACOLYTE`, `INFERRED`) are
hardest precisely because they depend on the most prior basis
vectors; that is exactly why they must come last in their
dependency chain.

The restart pass: after Chapter 2 is in place end-to-end, a second
forward pass Ch 2 sections 2.1 -> 2.37 with the full-procession
voice calibrated. The Movements act as eigenvalue clusters;
revising within Movement IV (Overhead) after Movement V (Truth
Transport) has stabilized is the restart-strategy move.
