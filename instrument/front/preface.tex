\chapter*{Preface}

This book is written in two registers. One register is ordinary prose. The
other is a formal device written in Lean. The prose is used to explain,
motivate, and connect the ideas. The Lean is used to measure the compiled
process by which those ideas are introduced, constrained, and carried forward.
It is not included as ornament, and it is not present merely to reassure the
reader that the definitions could be made precise later. It is itself part of
the method of the book.

The role of the Lean device is simple. It records what must be primitive, what
may be derived, and what follows from what. In that sense it measures the
logical shape of the work. When a term is introduced in prose, the Lean is
meant to show what that term commits us to. When an inference is drawn in the
text, the Lean is meant to expose the structure that makes the inference
defensible. The code does not replace the argument, but it prevents the
argument from drifting.

For that reason, the central terms of the book are treated in three ways at
once. Each important term may be given a connotative sense, a denotative
sense, and a precise sense. The connotative sense tells the reader how the
term lives in ordinary language. The denotative sense tells the reader what
the term points to operationally. The precise sense tells the reader how the
term is fixed within the formal system. By placing these three senses beside
the Lean definition itself, the book aims to keep its vocabulary readable,
operational, and exact.  In the prose, we indicate our precision using
a \lean{monospace] font to indicate to the reader that the word they are looking
at has, simultaneously, a colloquial meaning, a mathematical meaning, and
a series of logical predicates constructed by the argument evaluated as a
truth about rigor.

This arrangement is especially important in a philosophy of science. Such a
book must speak in language broad enough to address meaning, evidence, law,
history, and explanation. At the same time, it must avoid the familiar danger
that its most important words become suggestive long before they become
disciplined. The Lean device is built to resist that danger. It is a measuring
instrument for the compiled process of definition and inference. It tells us
not only what can be said, but when it may be said, and on what prior
structure it depends.

The reader should not think of the Lean as a parallel text detached from the
book's claims. It is better understood as a device internal to the work. It
measures the passage from witnessed difference to admissible continuation, from
admissible continuation to induction, from induction to countable structure,
and from there to the larger constructions that the prose will later discuss.
In this way, the formal system does not merely accompany the philosophy. It
helps determine the order in which the philosophy may responsibly proceed.

The hope, then, is not to replace philosophy with formalism, nor to reduce
scientific thought to syntax. The hope is narrower and more practical. By
using Lean to measure the compiled process of the argument, this book seeks to
make its definitions unusually precise and its conclusions unusually
defensible. If the prose succeeds, the reader will find the argument clear. If
the device succeeds, the reader will also find that the clarity is earned.
