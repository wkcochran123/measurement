## The one decision

A compiler is a single decidable decision over its source: compiles (accept) or throws
(reject). The device's core is `different?` / `dec_distinct : DecidablePred different?` —
decidable distinguishing. The new frame makes **the reader the source**. Reading the book
runs the book's one decision on the reader's assent.

- **Believe** → `different?` = false → reader and proof fall into ONE class → **funge /
  covariant / the world's count**. The needle threaded once, holds. The fact is not the
  reader's to reject; it is counted.
- **Do not** → `different?` = true → reader **tanges** out, holds the proof as a label at
  arm's length → **contravariant / ours**. The branch that reopens the loop, taken.

## The unification (why this is THE metaphor, not a metaphor)

It is the same distinction as **the-naming** (Meanwhile24, in build): finiteness forbids the
infinite line → the tower wraps → the electron named by counting.

- Reader who **believes** = the reader for whom the tower **wraps** (finite, the loop closes,
  the identity forced). Belief = accepting the pigeonhole collapse.
- Reader who **does not** = the reader holding out for the **continuum** (the infinite line,
  arbitrarily fine distinctions, never forced to identify, the naming never made). Disbelief =
  demanding the continuum's infinite resolution.

Every device landmark is this one decision applied to the reader:
- the summit **collapse** (true = false made one) = the moment the believing reader stops
  distinguishing the book's truth from their own;
- the **needle close** (III.1) = "does the reader's belief differ from the proof?" — for the
  believer the difference is carried but never taken;
- the **measured agreement** (the pulse, V.3) = the reader's compiled verdict, read not proved.

believe. Believed → the tower wrapped, the loop closed, the electron named by counting, the fact
