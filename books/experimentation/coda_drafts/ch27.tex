\noindent The case turns now on the very stage it has been standing on, and finds there is no stage. Time and
space, which every trial seems to play out within, are not a backdrop the case was set against; they are read
off the records themselves --- the order the marks already carry, the geometry the coordinated events already
make. The instrument never stood on a floor of time and space; it laid down its counts in order, and the order
was the time, and the coordination was the space.

Watch each of them come off the record rather than out from under it. Time is nothing but the order of the
counts --- one event before another exactly when its mark was entered earlier; ``before'' and ``after'' the
shape the sequence already has, not a container it sits inside. Kant took time for an a priori form, given
before any experience and presupposed by all of them; recast here, that form is nothing grander than the
record's own order, the shape the marks already carry. That order costs nothing to assert --- to write
one mark after another is grammar, a move in the language of recording that adds nothing to the world's count
--- but everything to keep: once written after, no later refinement may set it before; the order may be
extended, never bent or reversed. And the order is directed --- no reading is admitted without a prior cause to
trigger it, a registration with nothing before it to license it rejected outright, not entered as doubtful but
not entered at all. Space, too, is a summary and not an arena: a point is no primitive atom of it but the limit of a nest of
overlapping regions, and the regions are built from which events overlap which --- relations first, and the
dimension the count of how many independent ways the events can be coordinated, the smooth manifold a shadow of
that finite web. The marks are ours to lay; the
order and the geometry they carry are the world's.

And beneath all of it, one sentence returns --- the very first the book asked, come back now from the far side
of everything measured since. At the opening the instrument asked of a single mark: which of its features are
the world's, and which are only the hand's bookkeeping? The answer, offered then as a premise, stands here as a
foundation: the physical content of a record is exactly what survives relabeling. Rename the events, slide the
origin, boost to a moving frame --- handle the account however one likes --- and whatever is left unmoved is the
physics, and whatever shifted was ours from the start. Galileo's relativity, that no frame is privileged and
one physics holds in every one, meets the older algebra's rule, that the real structure of a thing is just what
its transformations cannot touch --- one statement said in two tongues. The discipline the book has kept in every chapter, drawing
the one line between the world's part and the hand's, was never a habit it picked up along the way; it was the
ground it stood on the whole time. The first claim held because the last records obey it --- the spine
recovered, and found to have been the foundation all along.

And the \emph{charge} is that invariant, seen for what it always was. From the first turning of the wheel the
charge was a count --- and a count is precisely what survives relabeling: rename the marks, permute the indices,
rewrite the ledger in another alphabet, and how many there are does not move. The charge is the part of the
record no relabeling touches, the world's residue beneath every naming; it was the invariant from the first
count, and the book has spent its length showing that the invariant is what a measurement is of. Whatever comes
to be owed is owed on that residue --- the count that survives every retelling, unchanged from the first mark to
the last. And the trace is the order that residue is read in: the record's own sequence, kept monotone, never
bent --- the time the counts themselves carry. Charge and trace are the invariant and its order, the two things
no relabeling can take away. It is the plainest form of the one line the book has drawn everywhere: the labels
are the hand's, and the count beneath them the world's, held fast through every change of alphabet the record
has ever worn.

And the three readings the case will bring share exactly this ground. Each keeps its counts in the same
records-borne order; each reads its geometry off the same coordinations; each answers to the same invariant
beneath its labels. That common ground is what will let them be compared at all --- three accounts in three
hands, but all of them counts in one order, all answerable to what survives relabeling. The three are corrected
and ready, standing now on a foundation examined from beneath; the number they must meet on is still not in
hand. The ground is firm; the meeting is not yet made.

One question of the foundations remains. Having found time and geometry in the records' own order, the
instrument asks next what relations the records build among themselves --- how they combine, compare, and
compose; the logic and the algebra a record grows from within. The next chapter turns to those relations. The
case is still open, its ground recovered as a foundation and its three readings not yet agreed; the court does
not sit until the record is complete.
