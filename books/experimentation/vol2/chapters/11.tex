\chapter{The Apparatus Weighs Its Own Act}

\section{The cost of a configuration is a reading}

The last chapter forced the residue to show on benches of matter. This one turns the bench on itself. Every reading the
apparatus takes requires the apparatus to \emph{set something up} --- to establish the field, the loop, the configuration in
which the residue can show --- and that setting-up has a cost, a definite energy the arrangement carries. This chapter reads
that cost as a quantity in its own right. The action of a configuration, evaluated on the configuration the apparatus
actually realizes, is a reading; and the reading the apparatus takes of its own setup is the self-energy the whole climax
will turn on.

The mathematics is the action evaluated on-shell. A field configuration is scored by an action $S[\phi] = \int
\mathcal{L}(\phi, \partial\phi)\,\mathrm{d}^4x$, and the configuration the apparatus realizes is the one that extremizes it,
$\delta S = 0$. But the extremal value $S[\phi_{\text{cl}}]$ --- the action \emph{evaluated on} the realized configuration ---
is not zero and not nothing; it is a number, the cost of that configuration, and it is a reading the apparatus can take. For
the electromagnetic field the on-shell action is built from the field energy, $W = \tfrac12\int (\epsilon_0 E^2 +
B^2/\mu_0)\,\mathrm{d}V$, and this is precisely the price of establishing the field the measurement needs. The cost of a
configuration is a reading because the on-shell action is a definite quantity, floored and bracketed like any other the
instrument takes.

What makes this cost the apparatus's \emph{own} is that it is the price the apparatus pays to make any reading possible at
all. To read the residue on a bench, the bench must first establish the configuration --- energize the field, tension the
fiber, set the orbit --- and the energy of that establishment is charged whether or not the residue is ever read. It is the
overhead of measurement, the standing cost of having an apparatus at all. The instrument, weighing this overhead, is weighing
the price of its own readiness to measure. This is a genuinely new object: not the residue the bench reads, but the cost the
bench incurs to be a bench. And it is readable by exactly the same means --- a loop, an alteration, a bracket --- so the
apparatus can turn its measuring on its own overhead and get a number back.

There is an honesty to mark before the chapter proceeds, because the on-shell action of a point source is famously ill. For a
point charge the field energy $W = \tfrac12\int \epsilon_0 E^2\,\mathrm{d}V$ diverges as the volume shrinks onto the point,
$W \sim \int_0^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^2} \to \infty$. The apparatus's own cost, read naively, is infinite --- which is
the residue of Chapter 3 in its most stubborn form, the leftover that will not integrate away. The instrument does not
subtract this infinity and pretend it was never there; it carries it, floors it at the resolution of Chapter 6, and reads the
cost as a bracket at that floor. The divergence is not a flaw to be deleted but the residue reasserting that a finite
instrument cannot resolve a point --- and the floored bracket is the honest reading of a cost that has no finite point value.

In the two verbs, the cost of a configuration is a funge the apparatus takes of its own setup. The residue was the world's
funge; this cost is the apparatus's own --- the on-shell action bagged as a single number, the price of the whole
arrangement gathered into one reading. To weigh the setup is to funge the configuration into its cost, and to read that cost
is the same act of bracketing the instrument has performed all along, now pointed inward. The next section presses the
inward turn to its sharpest form: the apparatus's own field acts back on the apparatus, and the self-reference of a bench
that weighs its own act becomes a physical force.

\section{The apparatus in its own field}

The apparatus has a cost, and the cost is a reading. This section makes the inward turn physical: the apparatus's own field
does not sit inertly waiting to be weighed --- it acts \emph{back} on the apparatus that established it. The measuring
arrangement is immersed in the field it created, and that field exerts a force on its own source. Self-reference, in the
physical register, is this back-reaction: the bench weighs its own act because its own act pushes on it. The self-energy is
not a bookkeeping total but a physical self-force, and reading it is reading the apparatus in the grip of its own field.

The physics is radiation reaction. A charge that is disturbed radiates, and the field it radiates carries energy and
momentum away, so the field must exert a reaction force back on the charge to balance the books --- the Abraham--Lorentz
self-force, $F_{\text{self}} = \frac{\mu_0 e^2}{6\pi c}\,\dot{a}$, proportional to the rate of change of the charge's
acceleration~\cite{lorentz1904}. The source and the field are not two separate systems; the source sits inside the field it
sources, and the field pushes back. This is self-reference made into dynamics: the charge's own field is a term in the
charge's own equation, the apparatus is an input to the measurement it performs. The bench that weighs its own act does so
because its own act is one of the forces acting on it.

The circularity here is real and is not a defect --- it is the exact structure the instrument was built to read. An ordinary
measurement reads a residue external to the apparatus; a self-measurement reads a residue the apparatus itself generates and
is then subject to. The loop, in this case, closes through the apparatus: the field goes out from the source, and comes back
to act on the source, and the alteration read at the end is the source's response to its own field. This is the tightest loop
the book has drawn, and its holonomy is the self-energy --- the residue of a circuit that begins and ends at the apparatus.
The instrument weighing itself is a loop through its own field, and the reading is what the loop hands back.

The reading is marked, as always, for what is derived and what is glossed. That the apparatus's field acts back on the
apparatus --- that a source sits in its own field and is forced by it --- is the physics, exact and classical. That this
self-force \emph{is} the self-energy the coupling will be read as --- that the cost of the bench weighing its own act is the
quantity the world knows as the electron's self-energy --- is the reading laid over the self-force, and marked as a reading.
The instrument derives a back-reaction; the identification of the back-reaction's cost with the self-energy is the
interpretation the climax will lean on, and the book flags it here so the climax does not have to pretend it was proved. A
self-force, derived; a self-energy, read.

In the two verbs, the apparatus in its own field is a funge that closes through the selfsame thing that made it. The residue
is funged --- the field bagged as the apparatus's own --- and the tange that reads it is the apparatus's response to that
very bag: the source selecting, out of all the forces on it, the one its own field exerts. Self-reference is the funge and
the tange sharing a subject: the apparatus is both what is bagged and what selects. So the bench weighs its own act by
standing in its own field and reading the push --- and the last section names the currency the push is weighed in, which is
the apparatus's own, with no external standard anywhere in it.

\section{The pulse in its own currency}

The apparatus weighs its own act by standing in its own field. This closing section names the units the weighing is done in,
and the answer completes the chapter's turn inward: the self-energy is denominated in the apparatus's own currency, its own
natural pulse, with no external standard anywhere in the account. The instrument does not weigh its self-cost against a meter
kept elsewhere; it weighs it against itself, in the beat the whole construction has run on. The self-energy is the pulse of
the apparatus, counted in the apparatus's own units, and that self-containment is what will let the climax ask the coupling
without reaching outside for a single number.

The physics of ``its own currency'' is natural units. The apparatus carries its own fundamental constants --- the quantum of
action $\hbar$, the speed $c$, its own charge $e$ --- and the self-energy is naturally expressed in the scales these build,
$e^2 / 4\pi\epsilon_0$ over a length the apparatus itself sets. There is no external ruler in this: the self-energy is a ratio
of the apparatus's own quantities, a pure number once the apparatus's own scales are used to measure it. The pulse is the
apparatus's characteristic frequency, its own oscillation, and the self-energy counted in pulses is the self-energy weighed
against the apparatus's own clock. Every quantity in the self-weight is the instrument's own; none is fetched from a standard
kept outside.

This matters because it is the anti-crank structure in its most refined form. An apparatus that weighed its self-cost against
an external standard could tune the standard to make the cost come out however it wished. An apparatus that weighs its
self-cost against itself has no such freedom: the currency is fixed by the same construction that generates the cost, so the
reading cannot be adjusted by any external dial. The self-energy is what it is because the apparatus's own scales say so, and
the apparatus does not get to choose its own scales after the fact. The self-containment is not elegance for its own sake; it
is the guarantee that the self-weight is a reading and not a setting --- that the number, when the climax asks for it, is
imposed by the construction and not selected to please.

And here, at the currency, the chapter does what every chapter has done at the edge of the number: it names the kind and
withholds the size. It has said \emph{what} the self-weight is --- an on-shell cost, a back-reaction, a self-energy --- and
\emph{in what} it is denominated --- the apparatus's own pulse, its own constants, no external standard. It has not said, and
will not say here, \emph{how much}. The magnitude is the next part's, and even there it will not arrive as a value weighed
off this bench; it will arrive as a bracket, floored, and beside it an external number the instrument proves it cannot
derive. The self-energy's units are the apparatus's own; the self-energy's size is not this chapter's to hand over, and it
does not.

In the two verbs, the pulse in its own currency is a funge the apparatus takes entirely within itself. The self-cost is
bagged --- funged into a single self-energy --- and the units it is bagged in are the apparatus's own beats, a tange it takes
from its own construction rather than from any external scale. There is no outside standard to tange against; the selection
and the bag are both the instrument's. So the apparatus weighs its own act in its own coin, and hands the climax a fully
self-contained quantity: a self-energy, denominated in the instrument's own pulse, its kind named and its magnitude withheld.
Part IV closes with the apparatus having weighed itself --- and the field, next, closes around this self-weight as the
curvature whose coupling the last part will finally ask.
