\chapter*{Preface: Two Indices and a Curvature}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface: Two Indices and a Curvature}

This is the physical reading of a single instrument. Four volumes walk the same fifteen chapters; each is written in the
language where the instrument's face comes out exact, and English is the fallback, never the medium. Here the medium is
geometry and the field --- differential geometry where the object is smooth, quantum field theory where it is quantized ---
and the reader should expect equations to carry the argument and prose to say what the equations mean. The claim of this
volume is not that physics decorates the instrument. It is that the instrument, read on this face, \emph{is} physics: the
residue it carries is a curvature, the number it reaches for is a coupling, and the honest thing it hands back at the end is
a bracket with the dimensions of an inverse fine-structure constant and the width of the machine's own resolution.

Two words recur on nearly every page, and it is fairer to set them at the door than to let the reader decode them for a
hundred pages. To \emph{tange} is to select by a characteristic --- to pick out \emph{this} against \emph{that} by the one
property that parts them. To \emph{funge} is the opposite gesture: to gather into one heap everything that is, in the way
that matters, the same. In this volume the two verbs are not loose metaphors; they are the two ways an index sits on a
tensor. A quantity that transforms \emph{with} the change of frame --- a covariant index, one that is summed against the
world and counts what the world does --- is a funge: it bags together, under one component, everything the chosen basis
cannot tell apart. A quantity that transforms \emph{against} the change of frame --- a contravariant index, the label we
lay on the world to read it --- is a tange: it is our selection, our coordinate, the pin we put in to name a point. Funge is
covariant and counts; tange is contravariant and labels. The metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ is precisely the machine that trades one
for the other, $V_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}V^\nu$, raising and lowering, turning a label into a count and back. To measure, in this
book, is to tange a quantity out of the world with a contravariant label and then funge it, through the metric, into a
covariant reading the world agrees on. Every experiment in these pages is those two acts and the tensor that joins them.

There is a place where \emph{tange} stops being an ordinary act and becomes the deepest fence in the subject. To select
one point from one neighborhood is nothing; the chart does it. To select one point from each of finitely many
neighborhoods is still nothing; you do it in turn and finish. But to select one point from each of a continuum of
neighborhoods, all at once, with no smooth rule to select by --- that is a tange promoted to the continuum, and it cannot be
taken for granted. In the foundations it has a name, the axiom of choice, and its history is a standing warning: Zermelo
posited it~\cite{zermelo1904}, G\"odel showed it consistent~\cite{godel1938}, and Cohen showed it independent by
\emph{forcing}~\cite{cohen1963} --- approaching an ungraspable object through an ascending tower of finite conditions, each
one writable, converging on a limit no single condition reaches. That tower is the shape of this whole construction, and
this volume takes the finite side of it without apology. The instrument at the center of these pages tanges and funges
without pause, but it only ever tanges \emph{finitely}, off a resolution it can actually resolve. It never selects across a
continuum by a rule it cannot exhibit. The consequence is the reason the book can end where it does: an instrument granted
the continuum for free could posit any coupling it pleased and dress the wish as a measurement. This one cannot. When it
runs out of distinctions it stops, and hands back not a point but an interval, the width of the interval the exact measure
of what its finite selections could not resolve.

That discipline shows up, on this face, as the oldest honesty in physics: report the bound your apparatus can repeat, not
the digit you would like it to read. The instrument here measures a coupling, and it measures it the way a bench measures
anything it cannot resolve to a point --- it brackets. It drives a lower bound and an upper bound toward each other until it
reaches the floor of its own resolution, and there it comes to rest, because past that floor the convergence is no longer resolving
a quantity but ringing on an artifact of the search. The bracket is wide by choice, not by failure. To close it to a point
would be to claim a precision the floor forbids; to leave it open is to tell the truth to scale. The reader will meet the
external, laboratory value of this coupling exactly once, near the end, and it will be named for what it is --- a number the
world's benches measure and this machine \emph{proves it cannot derive} --- and it will never be set beside the machine's own
bracket in a gesture of ``look how close.'' There is no look-how-close in an honest measurement. There is the reading, and
the floor, and the width between them.

The volume is built in five parts. \emph{A Difference} lays the ground: a nonzero separation, before any metric, becomes
a countable index and then a residue that will not integrate away. \emph{The Turn} walks the tower back down, finds the loop,
and reads its failure to close as a curvature --- the slip that becomes a number, bracketed and floored. \emph{The Faces}
splits that number into the readings a single tensor carries under a change of frame: charge, mass, phase, spin, and the
named electron that sits stable among them. \emph{The Closing} brings the residue to the physical bench, lets the apparatus
weigh its own act as a self-energy, and closes the field. \emph{The Bound} asks the coupling, answers it as a bracket, and
draws, at the last, the jar: the trace it can derive and the shape it cannot, the near field the local matter fixes and the
free field it does not, set side by side and never confused. The climaxes of this volume are the two chapters where the
field closes and the coupling is asked --- and even there, most of all there, the field-closure only asks; the bracket
answers. The ending is the climax, not a disappointment. It is the boundary of derivation, drawn to scale, in the one
language where that boundary is a curvature you can see.
