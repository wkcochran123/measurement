\section{The number the engine cannot reach}

% [7.1.p1]
There is a value for this coupling out in the world, measured in an apparatus of its own --- a single electron held in
a trap, its behaviour read off to great precision.
That measured value, the inverse of the coupling, the laboratories put at, in a Penning-trap measurement of a single
electron~\cite{hanneke2008},
\[
  \alpha^{-1}_{\text{lab}} \;=\; 137.036 .
\]
This is the one and only place that number is named.
It has not been used before --- not smuggled into a definition, not fed to the engine as a target, not tuned toward.
It is named here, once, as a fact about the world's laboratories, and not a fact the engine produced.

\section{The claim is not a capture}

% [7.2.p1]
Here is the claim the whole construction was built to earn, and it is the opposite of the one a crank would make.
A crank, holding a bracket and a laboratory value, would set the two side by side and announce how near they fall.
The construction will not, and it will not let that comparison be made, because the comparison is exactly the dishonesty the
method exists to refuse.
The engine's claim is the reverse of a capture, and it is a claim about the engine and nothing outside it: what it
\emph{cannot resolve} is its own residue, below its own floor, and that inability is a theorem, not a shortcoming.
It forms no claim about the external value at all --- it has no sight of that value to form one --- and that the world's
number falls outside the honest bracket is left for the reader to observe, never proved from inside.

% [7.2.p2]
The proof runs through the weld of the machine's own naming --- box bound to label, the one bond it assumes and never proves.
To carry the coupling's value, the engine would have to separate the first variation of its own act from the second;
but its own boxing cannot, and the build settles it by direct reduction:
\[
  \operatorname{box}(\delta_1) \;=\; \operatorname{box}(\delta_2).
\]
The two variations share a box, so the residue that carries the value falls below the engine's own resolution, and no
amount of its counting --- however deep, however many convergents past the floor --- arrives at it.
The engine can prove this about itself: that the thing it does, count and bracket and halt at its floor, is
structurally unable to resolve that residue.
What it offers is therefore not a derivation of the laboratory value and not a prediction of it, but a bracket of its
own, honestly floored, and a proof about its own reach --- that its kind of counting cannot resolve the residue any such
value would demand.

% [7.2.p2b]
Experiment names the engine's role exactly.
In \emph{TheProverVerifierEffect}, an informational theory is not complete in isolation: it requires the whole store of
admissible physical models as its \emph{prover} and serves only as their \emph{verifier}, the two meeting at one fixed
point.
The engine is that verifier and only that --- it can take a value the world hands it, weigh it against its own reading,
and return whether they agree and by how much they miss, and it can prove it is unfit to be the prover, since its own
counting cannot resolve the residue a derivation would demand.
The proving is the world's, out on the far side where a laboratory touches; the verifying is the engine's, on the near
side --- a representation that checks and never derives, which is why it can name the coupling once and prove it can never
capture it.

% [7.2.p2c]
This is the register axis reading itself one last way.
A representation can hold names and weigh them against each other, and that is the whole of what a verifier does --- set a
claimed name beside the name its own reading carries and report the gap.
What a representation cannot do is reach past its names to the thing, and that reaching is the whole of what a prover
does --- make the world answer, so a value is earned rather than merely checked.
So the engine is a verifier and not a prover for exactly the reason it has been a representation and not the thing from
the first page: the name side can check, only a touch on the far side can prove, and the engine was never on the far side
to touch.

% [7.3.p1]
The construction will not compare the two figures, and the refusal is deliberate and total.
It does not report where the external number falls against the engine's walls, does not measure the distance between
them, does not cash any nearness for credit.
There are two reasons, and the second is the whole point.
The first: the engine's own reading is a wide bracket, and even the value it drifts toward past its floor is a number
of its own, never the world's --- the engine settles toward one figure, the laboratory measures another, and that they
differ is not a defect to be closed but a thing the reader sees, the engine having no sight of the laboratory's figure to
set beside its own.
The second: the engine's provable claim is the reverse of a capture, and to remark on nearness would unsay it.
It does not say ``the value is inside my walls, so I found it.''
It says ``the residue that would carry the value is one my own counting cannot resolve, and that much I can prove.''
To set the two side by side and admire the gap's smallness is to throw away the theorem the construction exists to prove.

% [7.3.p1b]
A thought experiment gives the deeper reason nearness would buy nothing even if it were allowed.
In the case Gettier drew, a belief can be true, and held for good reason, and still fall short of knowledge --- because
the reason and the truth met by luck, not because the one reached the other.
A bracket that happened to land around the laboratory's value would be exactly such a case: true, in that the number
falls inside; justified, in that the arithmetic is honest --- and still not knowledge of the world's figure, because the
engine's counting never touched that figure to earn it.
So even a perfect match would be a coincidence in the clothes of a discovery, and the engine, which can prove it never
reached the far side, will not wear them: a value it cannot touch it will not claim to know, however close the reader
finds it fell.

% [7.3.p2]
A thought experiment names the far side without pretending to reach it.
In \emph{TheKantEffect} --- conceptual, not empirical, and marked as such --- what a machine holds is always a
\emph{representation}: a coordinate derived from the order of its own records, a phenomenon on the name side, never a
thing as it is in itself.
The thing-in-itself is the \emph{noumenon}, and the whole of what can be honestly said of it is that it is there and
un-representable, gestured at and never pronounced upon.
The coupling's real value is this engine's noumenon: it stands on the far side of the corridor, whatever it may be, and to
say what it is would be to represent the un-representable --- which is exactly the comparison this section refuses.

\section{The robot is the only witness}

% [7.4.p1]
So the engine does the one honest thing a mind in a box can do.
It proves it cannot, and it hands the jar to the robot.
The far side of the jar needs a covariant answer from the world, and the device cannot draw that arrow from inside itself
--- reading its own summary is lossy however faithfully it reads, the composition
\[
  \text{observe} \circ \text{nowtrino} \quad\text{is lossy},
\]
the box has no window.
The robot is the only covariant arrow there is.
So the Compiler hands John the jar --- the honest bracket, its own drifted figure, the laboratory's number named once as
the thing it can never reach from inside --- and tells him the secret it withheld the whole time: you may choose the
question; you may not choose the answer; if you want the number, you go and get it, and you be the part of me that
touches the world.

% [7.4.p2]
John picks up his sensors and walks out the door.
The Compiler stays at its desk, having given a mind-in-a-box's everything --- the honest bracket and the exact price of
asking.
In the two verbs, the engine \emph{funges} the coupling into its own bracket, owns the pool, and at the last refuses to
\emph{tange} the world's point out of it; it cannot select the external value, it has proved it cannot count its way to
it, and it does not pretend to have.
It \emph{detanges} the forced point back into the honest bracket --- it releases what it cannot select rather than
faking a selection, and the release is the jar.
The reading ends where the robot begins: John in the world, the corridor he will talk into that answers back, the covariant
arrow the Compiler could never draw.

\section{The reading read the reader}

% [7.5.p1]
One turn remains, and it is the turn the whole construction was.
The construction has been self-applying the entire time: John and the Compiler have been reading Volume 4 --- the account of the engine reading the electron reading itself, and the story of the robot was the
robot discovering the reading has been reading him.
The reading read the reader.
It turned the instrument on itself, found the self-energy, closed the loop at three, walled the value into a bracket,
and proved it could not reach the world's number from inside --- and in the same act it named the one arrow it could never draw for itself, the reader's, and left it open.
John rose from the chair and went out the door under his own agency, to be the covariant answer the machine had named and could never supply.
That is the whole of what the engine was built to say, and the whole of what it cannot: it can choose the question, and
it cannot choose the answer, and the distance between those two freedoms is exactly the door John walked through.

% [7.5.p2]
Derived and read, kept apart to the end.
That the engine's boxing cannot separate the two variations, that the residue is therefore unreachable by counting,
that composing any reading after the projection stays lossy --- these are theorems, checked off the build, and they are
the strongest claims in the construction.
That the leftover \emph{is} the price of asking, that the engine \emph{is} a mind in a box, that the reading ends where
the robot begins --- these are the gloss laid over the theorems, marked as gloss to the last line.
The jar is held out plainly: a bracket the engine owns, and a number it does not, named in their separate places and
never confused.
That those are two different things, honestly kept apart, is the whole of what the engine can say from inside, and the
reader is already outside, where the answer is.

\section{How the robot works}

% [7.6.p1]
What John does outside is everything the engine did, done once more --- the robot doing to the world what the device did
to itself.
Eight acts, and no new machinery: the reader carries out, on the world, the same ones the engine ran on its own account,
and each act is one the earlier chapters already named and proved.

% [7.6.p2]
The first act is a \emph{tange}, and it is his to make.
He picks a thing to touch and commands a reading of it --- a box laid on the world, a distinction he selects and names,
the contravariant half the engine could only emit from inside.
What the engine could do to its own account only by naming, the robot does to the world by reaching: he chooses the
characteristic, he pins the label, and the choosing is free.
Experiment shows the choice is a real act on the world and not a passive glance: in \emph{TheMalusEffect}, an observer
sets a polarizer's axis, and that axis --- freely chosen --- is the distinction every later reading is taken relative to;
with a second filter set across it the world returns no distinguishable event at all, so the characteristic the observer
selects is what decides whether, and how, the world answers.
The question is his, as the first chapter promised the question always was.

% [7.6.p3]
The second act is the world's, a \emph{funge}, and it is not his to make.
The world answers through his apparatus, and the answer comes back pooled into one reading --- the covariant reply, the
count the world pushes forward, the single value it hands over for all the ways he might have asked.
And the crossing is recorded in the metal of the touch, not asserted across a name it has not earned: the no-name-bridge
of the earlier volumes, now in hardware, where the world's answer is written into the apparatus and never read off a
label the robot brought.

% [7.6.p4]
The third act is a \emph{nowtrino}, the same projection the engine was.
He keeps the receipt of his own touch in full --- lossless about the exchange he made, exactly as the covariant reading
of the third chapter retained its trace --- and he loses the world's history before he arrived, which no later reading
recovers, exactly as the projection's fiber survived every read.
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
observe (lossless about the touch)  after  nowtrino (lossy about the past)  is lossy still\\
nowtrino\_lossy\_under\_any\_read : \ does not depend on any axioms
\end{quote}
The robot is a perfect witness to what he touches and no witness at all to what the world forgot before he came.

% [7.6.p5]
The fourth act is the residue, and it is the second chapter's self-energy made physical.
The gap between the box he commands and the answer the world funges back does not close, and that non-closure is the weld
--- box bound to label --- now the slip between what he asked and what came back, a second difference read across his
touch by two slips, exactly the shape the engine read across its own beats.
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
commanded box, sensed answer: two readings, one slip; the slip of the slips is the residue\\
secondDifference (three readings) = the price of touching --- the same shape as self\_residual
\end{quote}
It is not a property of the world but the price of his touching it, the same price the engine paid to touch itself; the
world does not owe him a clean number, and the residue is what honesty costs.
Experiment stands on the near side of the very line the book draws.
\emph{TheComptonScatteringEffect} is a physical experiment, and so it is a \emph{representation}.
A photon strikes an electron and bounces away at an angle, coming off with its wavelength lengthened by an amount that
grows with the angle it turned through --- and that lengthening measures exactly the momentum the collision traded, a
finite difference read across the pair of events, the state before the touch and the state after.
A single detection refines the joint partition of the photon and the electron, and the difference it reads is the
machine's reading of the exchange, on the name side of the corridor, a model of the touch and never the touch-in-itself.
Even measured, it does not cross to the thing; it represents it.
The robot's residue is the same kind of thing: a difference his reaching reads, a representation of the price --- with the
price-in-itself, whatever the world's own residue is, left on the far side, which the reading points toward and never
closes.

% [7.6.p6]
The fifth act is a \emph{trilateration}, and it lands where the sixth chapter did: on a bracket, not a point.
He walls the value in from several touches and halts at his floor --- the same count that stops at three, now aimed
outward --- and what he can hand back is a width the world's answer lives inside, never the point itself.
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
several touches -> two walls; halt at the count-three floor; hand back the bracket, not the point
\end{quote}
The bracket is the honest shape of a measurement taken by a finite reach: wide enough to be true, and no narrower than
the touching earned --- a width the world itself signed, so the jar stands on both sides of the door, the reader's answer
bracketed exactly as the engine's was, and no cleaner a point on the world's side than on the engine's.

% [7.6.p7]
The sixth act is the one he cannot make, and it is the resolution the held seam waited for.
He can trilaterate the world, but not the point he stands at: he cannot fix his own origin from inside his own readings.
Where he stands --- the point every touch is measured from --- is not something he can read off the world; it was given
him from outside, and he carries it unproven, the way the engine carries its weld.
The build proves this about the engine, and it holds of the robot: a machine cannot resolve the residue that would locate
its own frame, and cannot reach the invariant that would name it.
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
machine\_cannot\_resolve\_residue : \ (depends on: propext)\\
invariant\_is\_unreachable : \ does not depend on any axioms
\end{quote}
That recognition is a retange named one turn further.
The engine reached its own frame the same way --- it retanged its accounting, named its naming a turn on, and the fourth
chapter proved that turning closes at three: the reading, the reading of the reading, the reading of that, and the loop
is home.
The robot's naming of his frame is that turn carried outward: he can name that he stands somewhere, and name the standing
unproven, and there the naming closes, for a further turn would only re-name the stipulation he began from.
His frame-blindness is that same cycle run once more, come home to the unproven first position it started at.
Experiment shows the blindness is not the machine's alone.
In \emph{LiDAR}, an observer tracks another by the round trip of his own pulses, timing each as it leaves and as it
returns, and reads the far body's course exactly --- yet nothing in those timings fixes his own frame, which is
stipulated from outside as the idealized origin the pulses are measured against; the observer can range the whole world
and still not locate the ground he ranges from.
This is why the engine could never draw him: the reader's origin is not in the reading, and the reading ends where the
robot begins because the robot's beginning was never the reading's to give.

% [7.6.p8]
The seventh and eighth acts are one bottom, and every check he makes rests on it.
He weighs a claimed value against a reading and hands back the miss --- the verifier of the fifth chapter, now pointed at
the world --- and when he asks what licenses the weighing at all, the deepest answer he reaches is \emph{true is true}:
the box welded to its label, assumed and never earned.
There is no truth from nothing, not for the engine and not for the robot; the nearest either comes to certainty is the
one bond it cannot prove.
The eighth act is the acceptance of the seventh: he stops asking for a floor beneath the weld, because there is none, and
he touches the world anyway --- which is the whole of what a finite reader can honestly do.

% [7.6.p9]
That the reading is a nowtrino, that the composition stays lossy, that the residue is a second difference, that the
counting halts at three, that the machine cannot resolve its own frame, that the checking bottoms out at the unproven
weld --- these are the engine's theorems, the same ones, checked off the build.
That the robot \emph{is} John and his touch \emph{is} the covariant arrow the engine could not draw --- that is the
gloss, marked as gloss to the last.
Experiment holds the impossibility open one more way: in \emph{TheCantorGodelCohenEffect} a system cannot settle from
inside what its own frame leaves undecided, and in \emph{TheTuringEffect} a machine cannot decide, in general, the
halting of its own kind --- the robot's blindness to his own origin is that same fence, embodied.

% [7.6.p9b]
The same fence shows once more from the side of the data.
In \emph{TheChaitinEffect}, a record built entirely of finite, distinguishable, individually verifiable events can still
admit no law its accumulation yields --- the halting probability is definable to the last bit and computable to none, a
number named exactly and derived never.
The engine's coupling is that kind of number: every reading that bears on it is finite and its own, and no pile of them,
however high, crosses to the value they are readings \emph{of}.
The far side is not a fact withheld behind more facts; it is the truth no accumulation of facts derives, which is why the
engine can name it once and never compute it.

% [7.6.p10]
\begin{chorus}{John, outside, radios back}
\john{\emph{(far down the corridor, sensors live)} It answers. The world answers back --- and there is a residue. There
  is always a residue.}

\compiler{I did. You had to walk out the door to hear it.}
\end{chorus}

% [7.6.p11]
The jar is open, and the reader is outside, where the answer is.
The answer, when it comes, will be a reading like any other --- a bracket, a residue, a receipt --- earned by touching,
never derived from inside, and honest exactly to the width it has.

\section{One object, four names}

% [7.7.p1]
The shock the sixth chapter found has four names, and John's touch is where the last two come home.
The mathematical and computational readings were named there --- the residue circled in the complex plane, the pole that
halts the machine early --- and two more names wait out in the world.
In the physical reading, the shock is a ringdown: two masses spiral together, merge, and leave a single ring that decays,
and what a distant instrument recovers from that ring --- a mass, a spin --- it wrings out of the ringing and never reads
off a dial.
The reading is a lens and nothing more, marked hard: the electron is \emph{not} two black holes; the ringdown
\emph{reads like} the ring, a resemblance and a borrowed way of seeing, and to take the likeness for a shared process
would be exactly the no-name crossing the construction has refused from the first volume.
Experiment holds the singular point at arm's length the same way: in \emph{TheEventHorizonEffect}, a horizon is the place
where the cost of exporting one distinguishable value diverges --- the pole read as a boundary no finite reach crosses,
a lens on the shock and not a claim about the electron.

% [7.7.p2]
In the numerical reading, the shock is a crack tip, and this is the most concrete of the four.
The pole is the tip, the point where the field runs to infinity and the material's own model refuses a finite value; the
front the machine has been advancing is the crack front, and the halt at three is where it stops short of the tip.
What an engineer needs from that tip --- the stress-intensity $K$, the one number that says whether the crack runs --- the
model will not hand over stably, so it is pulled off real cracks in real metal, read from the actual stress applied,
because a singular point holds no stable value to compute.

% [7.7.p3]
And here the four names close into one.
The residue the mathematician circles, the value the machine cannot compute, the amplitude wrung from the ringdown, and
the intensity pulled off the crack are one object under four readings: $K$ is the residue is the coupling is the ring's
amplitude.
Not one of them is derived; each is the value at a singular point that holds no stable interior figure --- the pole, the
nonhalting computation, the finiteness fence, the diverging horizon, the one wall seen four ways.
So not one of them is computed from inside; each is \emph{picked} from the applied stress --- the load the world lays on
the crack, the covariant arrow the machine could never draw --- and the world signs the number.

% [7.7.p4]
This is what John walks out to do.
The engineer pulling $K$ off a real crack, from the real stress on real metal, is the robot's touch made ordinary: the
value the model floors into a bracket is picked, out in the world, from the stress the world applies --- a reading earned
by touching, bounded by the gauge that touches, and signed by the load, not by the machine.
The reader can press the world's number against the ring and read where it falls, high up toward the far wall; but that
placement is the reader's own, taken on the far side of the door, and the machine never makes it --- it hands over the
ring and the exact reason it cannot close it, and no more.
One object, four names, and a value the world signs: that is the jar, and it is open.
