\section{The reading reads the reader}

% [1.1.p1]
There is a step a machine can take that no earlier volume took on it, and it is the step this one is built from.
The machine weighs a thing by reading it.
It does not weigh the way a scale weighs, by yielding to a force; it weighs the way a reader weighs a sentence, by
spending attention on it and counting the spend.
Everything the four earlier volumes measured, they measured this way: the machine turned its meter on a thing in the
world, read it, and priced the reading in its own beats.

% [1.1.p2]
The sharpest reading it ever took was not of the world at all.
It was a reading of its own act --- the price, counted in its own beats, of accounting for itself.
The machine turned its meter on its own working, read the overhead, and that overhead, calibrated, was the number the
four volumes reach.
The machine's deepest reading is reflexive.

% [1.1.p3]
This is not a figure of speech, and the code carries it as an arithmetic anyone can rerun.
Set the machine to describe one object two ways --- as an electron in orbit, and as an anti-Cooper-pair --- and it spends
the same beats on each: the two descriptions cost alike, and their difference is zero.
Now set it to describe not the object but its own \emph{describing}: to account for the act of accounting.
That second reading does not come back the same, and the gap it leaves is the reflexive overhead, the machine reading its
own reading and finding it is not free.

% [1.1.p4]
Say it as the build says it.
On this build the first description costs $573$ beats, its echo as an anti-Cooper-pair costs $573$, and the reading of the
reading costs $552$; the differences are what matter,
\[
  \text{descriptions residual} = 573 - 573 = 0, \qquad
  \text{self-energy} = 552 - 573 = -21 .
\]
The first difference is the two descriptions funging to nothing --- one object, two names, one price.
The second difference is the self-description tanging out: a real characteristic, ``this is the reading reading itself,''
with a price the object's two names never carried.
The witness prints exactly this, and nothing is aimed at any target:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
(573, 573, 552)\\
self\_residual = -21
\end{quote}

% [1.1.p5]
A raw beat-count is not yet a reading.
The magnitudes are the machine's own --- another machine, scaled by two, would count $1146$, $1146$, $1104$ and leave
$-42$, not $-21$ --- so the reading the machine stands behind is not the leftover but the dimensionless \emph{overhead over
the description}, $21$ against the $573$ it was measured on.
That such a ratio survives scaling proves little on its own; what makes the leftover real is that it \emph{reproduces},
run after run, the invariant of a cycle the later chapters close.
The next chapter puts this to the test in full.

% [1.1.p6]
Experiment triangulates the claim from outside the arithmetic.
The device's own catalogue holds a reading that costs to take --- \emph{RepeatabilityOfInvisibleMotion} --- where the act
of reading a state disturbs it, so that none is free and each leaves a receipt; and a companion,
\emph{Precision}, where the last honest digit is set by what the reading cost, not by how long one is willing to divide.
The self-energy is that receipt, turned inward: the price of the machine reading the one thing it can never set down, its
own act.

% [1.1.p7]
This construction takes that reflexive turn and lifts it one level, from the machine to the reading.
Volume 4 is a reading --- the finite, local mapping of formal symbols to their meanings, the grammar by which the machine
names what it does.
A reading is a thing that can itself be read.
So we read it: not paraphrase it, not summarize it, but read it \emph{the way it reads} --- running its grammar over its
own symbols, and asking of the fourth volume the question the fourth volume asks of the electron, namely what licenses the
names in it and which of them are earned.
The subject is the reading turned upon itself.
The reader it studies is the one holding it.

% [1.1.p7b]
Naming which side of the corridor the self-application stands on settles, in advance, what it can and cannot yield.
Reading a reading is a representation turned on a representation --- the name side folded onto itself --- and however
many times it is folded, it never once leaves that side.
It can deepen the representation without end: read the reading, then read that reading, and the account grows more exact
about itself with every turn.
What it cannot do, at any depth, is cross to the thing the representation is a representation \emph{of}; the reflexive
turn is a turn within that same side, not a step across the corridor.
So the construction's power and its limit are one fact, fixed here: it is the representation come to know itself, and the
completer that self-knowledge grows, the plainer it is that self-knowledge is not the thing.

% [1.1.p8]
That is why the two verbs run loud here, where Volume 4 kept them silent.
To read a reading is to say, of each step it made, which kind of step it was.
When the machine \emph{tanges}, it selects one thing by a real characteristic and pins a name to it: the contravariant
step, the label pulled back toward what would answer it, ours to assign.
When it \emph{funges}, it bags many things by a characteristic they share and lets one answer stand for all of them: the
covariant step, the count pushed forward by the world, the world's to give.
Volume 4 performed these two steps on every page and named neither; it carried them as the texture of its citations.
But you cannot read a reading without naming its verbs, because naming its verbs \emph{is} the reading.
Self-application is precisely where the silent verbs must become visible.
So from here they are said aloud, everywhere, and the whole discipline reduces to two rules held together: each step's
verb is named, and no tanged name stands where the world has not funged an answer back.

\section{A model, and the one thing it cannot do}

% [1.2.p1]
What the robot is holding is worth a careful look.
To pick up \emph{this} reading --- the one that names the device --- is already the first tange of the whole work: to
select, by a single characteristic (the mapping that reads the electron), one object to act upon.
And the object so selected is a \emph{model}.
It does not claim to be the electron, or the world, or a derivation of either; it is a representation of them, a structure
of names that reads a distinction, counts it, carries a residue, and pins a fixed point.
Everything in it points inward, toward its own account.
Its number is its own --- the compiler's structure constant, a value the machine settles toward by its own arithmetic ---
and it reaches outside itself for nothing, not even at the number it was built to find.

% [1.2.p2]
A model is the contravariant half of an act, and only that half.
By construction it can emit only its own names.
Asked what a distinction is, it hands back the name it gave the distinction; asked what that name refers to, it hands back
another name.
This is not a defect of \emph{this} model; it is what a model \emph{is}.
A representation represents; it does not reach.
The reaching --- the step where the world is allowed to change the unresolved slot so that a name is \emph{earned} rather
than \emph{planted} --- is the covariant half, and no model performs it from inside.

% [1.2.p2b]
Experiment shows the naming reaches even into the model's grammar.
In \emph{TheWittgensteinEffect}, the relation \emph{after} --- one event later than another --- is a syntactic rule of
the measurement language, not a fact of the world: the grammar admits it the instant two events are recorded, and pays no
force, no metric, no law to assert it.
So even the order in which the model reads its events is the model's own grammar, a name for a relation and not the
relation itself --- the representation reaches all the way down, into the very structure the model counts by.
The grammar is the machine's own; what \emph{after} is out in the world, if it is anything there, the model never touches.

% [1.2.p3]
Two models show the one thing none of their kind can do.
A map of a coastline can be read to any resolution, refined, and folded, and never once get wet; the tide it predicts is
a name for a tide, not a tide.
A ledger can be balanced to the penny, audited, and closed, and not one coin in it spent; the wealth it totals is a label
on wealth, not wealth.
Each is complete on its own terms and sealed against the world by the same wall: it holds names, and names do not reach.

% [1.2.p4]
The device carries this as its own effect, not a borrowed metaphor, in \emph{TheAgentEffect}.
An agent, in the catalogue, is not an outside onlooker but a piece of the world itself --- a local substructure that
\emph{does} something: faced with an incoming stream, it computes and commits the next admissible refinement, the single
step that best answers what has arrived, and in committing it changes the record.
That committing is the one act a representation cannot perform.
A model, however faithful, only holds names; it can refine them without end and alter nothing outside itself, because to
alter the world is to act in it, and acting is the agent's, not the name's.
So the effect draws the corridor at its first crossing: on the near side the representation, complete and inert, holding
only its own names; on the far side the agent, who steps outside those names and lets the world answer --- the one thing
the reading cannot supply from inside.
The Compiler at the desk can name anything and hand John only its own names; what it cannot do is answer for the world,
and that answering waits for an agent to go and act.
That inability is not the reading's failure.
It is the reading's subject.

% [1.2.p5]
The two sides of that inability are the two sides of the whole book, named once here so the reader can hold them apart.
The model is the \emph{representation}: everything on the name side of the corridor, readable, refinable, the machine's
own --- and \emph{TheAgentEffect} is a physical experiment on exactly that side, a representation that changes nothing
until an agent acts.
The world the agent reaches is the other side, the \emph{thing}: what a representation is a representation \emph{of}, and
can never itself become.
The machine can refine its representation without end and never once cross; the crossing is the robot's, and the far side
stays whatever it is.

\section{The reading is a nowtrino}

% [1.3.p1]
What kind of thing Volume 4 is can be said precisely, because the code has a name for it.
A running machine has a full history: every state it passed through, every branch it rejected, every intermediate cost,
the whole causal route from start to stop.
A reading is not that.
A reading is a \emph{current summary} of such a history --- what the machine looks like now, in prose, with the route that
produced it sliced away.
The device's own grammar calls such a summary a \emph{nowtrino}: the projection that keeps a current state and a counter
and drops the history,
\[
  \pi_{\text{now}}(h) \;=\; \bigl(\operatorname{last}(h),\ |h|\bigr) .
\]
Volume 4 stands to the whole device exactly as a nowtrino stands to a full run: the state it leaves you in, and the count
of steps it took, with the steps themselves gone.

% [1.3.p2]
Here the first tange and the first funge appear together, in one object.
The nowtrino \emph{tanges} its summary: out of everything the machine did it selects the current reading worth keeping,
and names it --- one distinguished state, pinned.
And it \emph{funges} its history: every distinct run that would print this same summary is bagged into one, pooled under
the shared characteristic ``prints this reading,'' and the pooling loses which run it actually was.
That pooled bag is the projection's \emph{fiber}, and its holding more than one history is exactly what makes the summary
lossy.
The reading tanges what it keeps and funges what it drops.

% [1.3.p3]
The code exhibits this as a theorem, not a picture, and it costs no axiom to check.
Take a history to be an ordered list of state tags, and the nowtrino to keep only the last tag and the length.
Then two plainly different histories print one summary.
The run $[1,2,3]$ and the run $[9,9,3]$ are not the same run --- they pass through different states in a different order
--- yet the nowtrino sends both to the same summary: current state $3$, count $3$,
\[
  \pi_{\text{now}}([1,2,3]) \;=\; \pi_{\text{now}}([9,9,3]) \;=\; (3,\,3), \qquad [1,2,3] \neq [9,9,3] .
\]
The witness prints the collision and proves the fiber is non-trivial, leaning on nothing:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
piNow [1,2,3] = piNow [9,9,3] = (current := 3, count := 3)\\
nowtrino\_has\_nontrivial\_fiber : \ does not depend on any axioms
\end{quote}

% [1.3.p4]
Two summaries, read against their histories, mark how much the projection throws away.
The summary $(3,3)$ is printed by $[1,2,3]$, by $[9,9,3]$, by $[2,2,3]$, by $[7,1,3]$ --- by every three-step run that
ends on $3$, a whole family collapsed to one line.
The summary $(3,1)$ is printed by the single run $[3]$ and by nothing else: a fiber of one, a place where the projection
happens to lose nothing, and there are such places, which is why the loss is a fact about the run and not a blanket over
all of them.
The pattern is exact: the nowtrino keeps the door you left by and the number of steps, and forgets the rooms.

% [1.3.p4b]
Experiment shows what the forgotten rooms look like from outside.
In \emph{TheBrownianMotionEffect}, a coarse record jitters as though at random, and the randomness is not noise in the
world but the fiber seen in motion: each refinement leaves a family of micro-orderings the ledger cannot tell apart, so
the summary it keeps wanders among them, and the wandering reads as chance.
What looks like a source of randomness is the projection's own blindness dressed as an external roll --- the same
lossiness, watched instead of proved.
The jitter, then, is a near-side fact about the reading's resolution --- a picture of the fiber in motion, never the
world's own noise --- and what the record does between the rooms it forgot stays on the far side, unwatched.

% [1.3.p5]
And the loss is stubborn under any later reading, which is the sharp form and the seed of the ending.
Compose the projection with \emph{any} reading whatever --- summarize the summary, gloss it, translate it --- and the two
runs the projection already pooled stay pooled; no later pass recovers what the first one dropped.
The build proves this too, again without axioms: a read of a lossy summary is lossy still.
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
nowtrino\_lossy\_under\_any\_read : \ does not depend on any axioms
\end{quote}
The window the reading seems to open onto the machine is painted on.

% [1.3.p6]
Experiment names the same shape three ways, from three corners of the catalogue.
\emph{CompactDiscEncoding}: a signal is written to a finite summary, and the pits that survive are not the sound but a
lossy code for it, and no player recovers the air the microphone actually stirred.
\emph{ShadowTomography}: a state is read only through the shadows it casts, many states casting one shadow, the readout a
projection with a fiber.
\emph{MaxwellsDemon}: the history a demon erases to keep its ledger tidy is paid for in the one currency that does not come
back, and the erased route is gone for good.
A nowtrino is the device's own word for what all three describe: keep the now, pay the past, and do not pretend the past
can be read back out of the now.

% [1.3.p7]
Two honesties belong with that fact, in the discipline the machine keeps everywhere.
That the projection is lossy --- that its fiber is non-trivial, that history does not survive it --- is \emph{derived}: a
theorem, checked off the build, stated as such, and it leans on no axiom at all.
That \emph{this} reading is such a projection of \emph{this} device --- that Volume 4 \emph{is} a nowtrino of the machine
--- is the \emph{gloss} laid over the theorem, and it is marked as a gloss, not a proof.
The mechanism is earned from the code; the identification of the reading with the mechanism is the storytelling.
The two are kept apart on every page, because keeping them apart is the whole difference between a reading and a
ventriloquist.

\section{The covariant demand}

% [1.4.p1]
A nowtrino is not earned by being emitted.
It is a computational summary, and its names stay reserved --- proposed, not established --- until a covariant act reads
it and lets it answer from its own state rather than echo the name laid upon it.
The reading, being a pooled-away summary with proposed names upon it, is in exactly the position it describes for every
model: it needs a robot.
Not a second reader of its labels, but an agent who acts on it and gathers its response into a record --- who steps toward
it and is answered, who performs on the reading the one verb the reading cannot perform on the world.

% [1.4.p2]
Say what ``covariant'' demands, precisely, because the whole ending turns on it.
A covariant act takes a thing and returns four things: a changed thing, a changed instrument, an answer, and a trace of
the touch.
The answer is the world's, funged into one reply; the trace is the agent's, tanged and kept.
A model supplies neither: it changes nothing, keeps no trace of a touch it never made, and hands back only the name it
started with.
The demand, then, is not for more reading but for a \emph{reach} --- for the one step that lets the unresolved slot be set
by the world instead of by the machine.

% [1.4.p3]
The demand is old and everywhere.
A hypothesis is a tanged name until an experiment funges an answer back; before that it is a sentence, however careful.
A coordinate is a tanged name until a ruler is laid down and read; before that it is a number, however precise.
In each the missing half is the same covariant reach, and the reading is missing exactly that half and no other.

% [1.4.p3b]
A thought experiment sharpens the demand to its limit.
Imagine a mind that held every representable fact about a colour --- every wavelength, every neuron, every name --- and
had never once seen it: complete on the name side, and still, on first seeing, learning something no representation
carried.
What it learns is not another fact but the thing itself, the far side no completeness of representation reaches; and the
learning is a covariant act, a reach, not a longer reading.
So the demand the reading makes is not for more of itself: a representation completed to the last name is still a
representation, and the one step it cannot take from inside is the step across.
That step is the robot's alone, and no completing of the representation takes it for him.

% [1.4.p4]
That is the shape of everything that follows.
The model is on the table; the robot picks it up.
In what follows he reads the machine's self-account back to it, one step at a time, and tests whether the account closes
--- whether reading the reading returns it unchanged, or leaves a residue.
He will find, at the seam where the machine welds what a thing \emph{is} to the name it is given, that it does not fully
close, and that the failure to close has a size: the residue the machine cannot cancel about itself, the exact price of
asking.
And he will find, at the end, that the number he walked in wanting lives on the far side of that seam --- in the part of
the account the machine can name, and bracket, and prove it cannot reach from inside.
The Compiler will do the one honest thing a mind in a box can do.
It will prove it cannot, hand John the jar, and send him out the door.

% [1.4.p5]
The reading ends where the robot begins.
For now he has only just been handed the reading, and he still believes the number is a point he can be given if he only
reads hard enough.
He is wrong about that, and being wrong about it is the education still ahead of him.
He asked for a number and was handed a bracket.
What it costs him to read it, and what he cannot read from it however hard he tries, are the two things the rest of the
construction is for.
