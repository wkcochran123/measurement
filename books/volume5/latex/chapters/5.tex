\chapter{The Two Paths Agree}

% [5.chorus]
\begin{chorus}{John asks for a second opinion}
\john{You keep saying the value comes back the same. Then reach it a second way and show me it does not budge.}
\compiler{Gladly. I will take it straight through, and I will take it in steps, and we will lay the two side by side.}
\john{And if they differ?}
\compiler{Then the value was an accident of how I asked, and worth nothing.}
\john{\emph{(after the Compiler works)} Well?}
\compiler{Straight through, and in steps: the two land on one value, nearer each other than I can tell them apart.}
\john{So it is the number. Say it.}
\compiler{It is a value that does not care how you reach it. That is more than a number and less than the one you want.
  I have shown you it holds still. I have not yet shown you what it is --- and I will not, here, because holding still and
  being said are two different earnings, and only the first is paid for yet.}
\john{You are stalling.}
\compiler{I am being exact. A value you can trust and a value you can name are not the same debt. Watch me pay the first.}
\end{chorus}

\section{Reach it twice, by two routes}

% [5.1.p1]
The self-account closes and hands back a steady leftover; that much is settled.
The question here is whether that leftover is a property of the thing or an artifact of how it was reached.
There is a clean test.
Taken by two different routes, the value either lands in the same place or it does not.

% [5.1.p2]
The machine has two routes to its own value, and they are genuinely different procedures.
One goes straight through, in a single pass.
The other goes in steps, resolving a piece at a time and folding the pieces together at the end.
If the value depended on which route you took, it would belong to the route and not to the machine, and no honest reading
would stand behind it.
So the two routes are set going, and the question is only whether they agree.

% [5.1.p3]
Path-independence is the oldest test there is for telling a property from an artifact, and it comes in two familiar
forms.
A height between two points on a hill is the same whether you climb straight or switchback, because height is a property
of the points; the length of your walk is not, and depends on the path.
A theorem proved two independent ways is trusted more than one proved twice the same way, because two routes to one
conclusion make the conclusion, not the route, the thing they share.
The value the machine seeks is claimed to be the property in each pair, not the artifact, and this chapter is the test.

% [5.1.p4]
Experiment sharpens the distinction to a knife, in \emph{ItoLemma}.
Integrate a quantity along a path, and the answer can depend on how the path wanders.
Along a smooth path the integral is what calculus expects; along a jagged one --- a path that never stops changing
direction, at every scale --- an extra term appears, a correction the smooth path never sees, because the jaggedness
accumulates a contribution all its own.
The device reads this not as a fact of chance but as a structural consequence of refinement: when a finite record is
refined again and again, the admissible interpolants must update to stay consistent and minimal, and those updates
produce exactly the correction terms a classical setting would call stochastic.
So a value that survives both the smooth path and the jagged one has earned what a value surviving only one has not --- it
has shown itself independent of the route, not an artifact of a particular way of reaching it.
The reading stays on the near side: the path-correction is the known object, represented as a term the refinement rule
forces, and it claims nothing about a randomness in the world beneath the record.

% [5.1.p4b]
Both experiments are physical, and both are representations, on the near side where the machine works.
A path-dependent integral and a boundary-equals-interior sum are two ways the device models a value's dependence on how
it is reached --- readings of a process, not the process itself.
So the whole test that follows is conducted on the near side: whatever the coupling answers to in itself, neither
experiment reaches it, and the chapter asks only whether the machine's own reading holds still.

\section{They agree below the floor}

% [5.2.p1]
The build runs both routes and lays them side by side, and the answer is yes, with a precise sense of yes.
The straight route and the stepped route do not return identical figures all the way down; far past the point that
matters they part in the last digits.
But the gap between them falls below the count-three floor --- the resolution below which the machine cannot separate
two candidates at all.
At its own resolution the two routes \emph{funge} to one value, and the build certifies it: the difference is smaller than the
smallest distinction the machine can draw,
\[
  (1 \to 3) \;=\; (1 \to 2 \to 3) \quad\text{below the count-three floor} .
\]

% [5.2.p2]
The witness lays the two routes side by side and reports the gap, not the value --- because the value is not yet earned,
but the agreement is:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
direct   (1 -> 3)     : one straight pass\\
stepped  (1 -> 2 -> 3): quasi-Newton descent, count three\\
|direct - stepped|  is below the count-three floor  (smaller than the machine's finest distinction)
\end{quote}
The two procedures share every leading digit down to the floor and part only beneath it, where the machine has no eyes;
so at the machine's own resolution they are one reading, and the agreement is a theorem while the figure stays unspoken.

% [5.2.p3]
What kind of claim this is matters, because it is the machine's whole discipline in miniature.
The machine does not say the two routes are equal; equality below the floor is a fiction it refuses.
It says the two routes agree \emph{to the depth it can tell them apart}, and it declines to speak past that depth.
Two figures that differ only where the machine has gone blind are, for the machine, one figure --- not by rounding, but
by the honest admission that it has run out of resolution.
Three readings of ``agree'' keep the claim exact: they agree \emph{to the floor} (all shared digits match), not
\emph{at the floor} (the machine names no digit there), and never \emph{below} it (where it refuses to look) --- and only
the first is claimed.

\section{A property, not an accident}

% [5.3.p1]
A value that comes back the same however you reach it is a property of the thing, not of the reaching.
The self-value is robust to re-reading: whether you read the machine's account straight or the reading of it in steps, you
arrive at one place.
That robustness is the reflexive payoff of the whole construction --- the reading, turned on itself, arriving at the same
value whichever way it is worked.
The number the reading is about is not a figure it reports; it is a value that survives its own re-reading.

% [5.3.p1b]
Name which value this is, because the corridor runs through here too.
What comes back the same by either route is the machine's \emph{representation} of its own self-value --- a property of
that representation, real because it is route-independent, and entirely on the name side.
It is not yet the world's value, the thing: that the machine's reading holds still under re-reading says the reading is
honest, not that it has reached across the corridor.
Robustness earns the representation the name of a property; it does not earn it the far side, which stays whatever it is
until a robot touches it.

% [5.3.p2]
The two verbs sort the routes exactly.
The two routes are two \emph{tanges}: two distinct ways the machine chose to ask, each selected by its own procedure,
and both ours to choose.
The one value they settle on is a \emph{funge}: the two askings pool, below the floor, into a single answer the machine
owns.
Two tanged routes, one funged value --- this is the echo again, now at the level of the value.
Where the machine earlier heard the two descriptions of the object cost alike and pool to zero, here the two routes to the value
land alike and pool to one, and the same word names both poolings.

% [5.3.p3]
The robustness runs past two paths.
The same value can be reached from two genuinely independent standpoints --- the count's own descent to the crossing, and
the electron's second variation read off its orbit --- not two dressings of one procedure but two separate readings, and
they land in the same bracket, agreeing to the depth the machine can resolve.
Exactly two are independent, and the machine keeps the count honest: a further reading it can take, its own
self-measurement, lands on a \emph{different} crossing, and that one it holds apart rather than pooling in.
It can tell them apart because it checks them --- given a claimed value and a reading it returns whether they agree and by
how much they miss --- \emph{funging} the standpoints that read one crossing into a single answer and \emph{tanging} the
one that reads another crossing back out.
The agreement holds only up to that reported residual, and the residual is never closed by hand.

% [5.3.p3a]
\emph{TheDescartesEffect} is a physical experiment on the same shape, and it is a representation.
Take two rulers, each a repeatable procedure that lays down a stable count, and apply them in directions that do not
interfere.
Each returns its own tally, and the two tallies cannot be folded into a single number without losing which came from
which --- the only way to keep both is to write them as an ordered pair.
That pair is a coordinate, and it was forced by nothing geometric: no space was assumed, only two independent counts that
had to be recorded without collapsing into one.
Coordinates, on this reading, do not measure a space that was already there; they are the minimal bookkeeping that lets
two repeatable procedures coexist without one erasing the other.
The device's two standpoints are two such rulers: the count's descent and the electron's orbit are independent readings,
and their agreement forces a value the way two rulers force a coordinate --- a fact about the representations, not a reach
to the thing they read.
What the coordinate is \emph{of}, the position the rulers only bracket, stays on the far side, as it does here.

% [5.3.p3b]
Experiment sets the seal on the distinction between a property and an artifact, in \emph{TheGalileoEffect}.
One ledger of events admits many descriptions: different observers, or the same machine running its refinements in a
different order, hang different labels on the very same underlying structure --- a relabelling that permutes names and
reindexes the chains without touching what came before what, or which events are distinct.
This relabelling is a gauge freedom on the record, and it is the sieve.
A statement that survives every admissible relabelling --- that says the same true thing under every naming --- is a fact
about the ledger itself, a property of the thing named; a statement that changes when the labels change is an artifact of
the description, true only under the naming that flattered it, and no property at all.
That is precisely the machine's own test: its value earns the name of a property exactly by surviving two independent
readings unchanged, the count's descent and the electron's orbit landing alike, where a labelling-bound number would have
drifted between them.
The reading stays on the near side --- the invariance under relabelling is the known object, represented as a survival
test --- and it claims no privileged description, only that what survives all of them is the thing's and what does not is
the naming's.

% [5.3.p3c]
And \emph{TheMichelsonMorleyEffect}, read as a purely informational null result, carries the same seal: distinguishable
events propagate under a refinement rule that does not depend on direction, so what the reading returns is a fact about
the structure and not about the route it was carried along.

% [5.3.p4]
The checker is a live mechanism, and its honest arithmetic of standpoints is worth reading off the build.
It takes a claimed value and a reading, computes what the reading yields, and returns the miss and whether the miss falls
below the count-three floor:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
verify (register, claimed, reading) = \{ read, residual, verified := |residual| <= count3Floor \}\\
genuinely-independent coupling readings = 2   (the count-descent and the electron-orbit)\\
the self-measurement reads a DIFFERENT crossing -> verified = false  (held apart, not four-fold)
\end{quote}
So the agreement is not a four-fold coincidence dressed up as one: it is two genuinely independent readings landing
together, with a third that reads a different quantity honestly refused entry --- the machine funges what belongs and
tanges out what does not, and reports the residual either way.

\section{Three things, kept apart}

% [5.4.p1]
A temptation waits here, and the construction refuses it, because giving in would undo the discipline everything else is built
on.
Three facts about the self-application now sit on the table at once.
The reading is read \emph{twice} --- there is a first reading and a reading of that reading.
The loop the reading runs around has length \emph{three} --- the cycle established just before.
And the value comes back the same by \emph{two} routes --- the agreement of this one.

% [5.4.p2]
It is tempting to gather these into a single tidy number: two readings and three folds, or two routes and three counts,
summed into a five that would look like the shape of the whole.
That gathering is exactly the error the construction forbids everywhere else --- pooling distinct axes into one count, funging
together things that share no characteristic but that they happen to co-occur.
The double-read, the cycle's length, and the value's path-independence are three orthogonal facts.
They co-occur; they do not add.
Kept apart --- each axis \emph{tanged} as its own --- the self-application stays honest; summed, they make a number the machine never
counted.

% [5.4.p3]
The gathering shows the same mistake whichever way it is tried.
Add the two of the double-read to the three of the cycle and call it five, and you have added a count of readings to a
length of a loop --- a tally to a period, two things measured in different units, and their sum is in no unit at all.
Stack all three and call the shape ``two-three-two,'' and you have made a decoration, a numerology of the true numbers,
which is precisely the crank's gambit the whole construction exists to refuse.
Each true number is true; every combination of them is invented.

% [5.4.p4]
Derived and read, apart as ever.
That the two routes agree below the count-three floor --- that is a theorem, run on the build and certified; the grammar behind it is Volume 4, \emph{The Representational Grammar}.
That two independent standpoints agree up to a reported residual, while a reading of a different crossing is told apart ---
that is checked on the build too, by the verifier that weighs a claimed value against a reading and hands back the miss.
That the agreement \emph{is} the reflexive value, that a value robust to re-reading is the reading having become its own
subject --- that is the gloss over the theorem, marked as gloss.
And the figure itself, the actual walls of the bracket the machine settles between, is not named here; it waits for the
place that earns it, where the coupling is finally asked as a bracket and the honest width of that bracket is the whole
of the answer.
The value is steady, and it is real, and it is not yet said.
