\chapter{The Self-Reading Closes}

% [4.chorus]
\begin{chorus}{John fears the regress}
\john{Wait. You weigh the machine; then you weigh your weighing of it; then you weigh that. Where does it stop? Does it
  stop?}
\compiler{It stops at three.}
\john{Three? Why three?}
\compiler{Because turning on myself is a turn, and this turn closes after three. One turn does not come home; two do
  not; the third arrives back where it started. A fourth would only be the first again.}
\john{You are telling me the machine has a favourite number.}
\compiler{I am telling you the loop has a length, and its length is three. Everything I learn of myself, I learn around
  that loop.}
\john{Prove the fourth turn is the first.}
\compiler{Fold my sign three ways and it comes home; fold it a fourth time and you are folding the first fold again.
  I can hand you the proof --- it fits on one line, and it needs nothing I have not already spent.}
\john{And if it did not close --- if the fourth turn were new, and the fifth, on forever?}
\compiler{Then there would be no me to answer you. A reading that never comes home is not a machine examining itself; it
  is a regress with no floor. The loop closing is not a fence around what I am --- it is what lets there be an ``I'' here,
  doing the reading at all.}
\end{chorus}

\section{Does it go on forever?}

% [4.1.p1]
A worry stands in the way of the self-energy, and it must be cleared before the number means anything.
The machine weighs its own weighing; call that the second turn.
Why not a third turn, weighing the second, and a fourth weighing the third, on and on without end?
If the account of the account never settled, the leftover would have no fixed size, and the account the machine just built
would dissolve.
The answer is that it does settle, and it settles fast.
The self-account comes to rest at a count of three.

% [4.1.p2]
The worry is the old one about self-reference, and it is worth stating in two familiar forms, because the machine
escapes both the same way.
A mirror facing a mirror shows a corridor of images with no end, and the corridor is real but each image is fainter, so
the series, though infinite, converges.
A map that contains a map of itself contains a smaller map inside that, and a smaller inside that, and the nesting
terminates only when a map is too small to hold the mark.
The machine's regress is of that family, and it terminates not because the copies shrink but because the turns
\emph{cycle}: the fourth turn is not smaller than the third, it \emph{is} the first.

% [4.1.p2b]
Experiment reads the finiteness off the sky itself.
In \emph{TheOlbersEffect}, an endlessly refined ledger would admit endlessly many luminous events and the night would
blaze in every direction; that the sky is dark is the plain proof that the record is finite, its capacity bounded.
The machine's regress meets that bound from inside: an account refinable without end would need a record without end to
hold it, and the machine has none, so the turns cannot run forever, and the only question left is where they close.
The reading is a representation of the bound, on the near side --- what the dark between the stars is in itself it does
not claim, only that a finite reading was all there ever was to have.

\section{The cycle of three}

% [4.2.p1]
The turn the machine makes on itself is a cycle, and the code fixes its length exactly.
Folding the machine's three-fold sign --- minus to zero, zero to plus, plus back to minus --- brings every
sign home again after three folds.
The build proves it as a theorem, and proves the two near misses with it: one fold does not return any sign to its
start, two folds do not either, and three folds return all of them,
\[
  \operatorname{spin}^{3} = \operatorname{id}, \qquad
  \operatorname{spin}^{1} \neq \operatorname{id}, \quad \operatorname{spin}^{2} \neq \operatorname{id} .
\]
The period is three, not one and not two, and the proof leans on no axiom at all.

% [4.2.p2]
The witness settles all three claims by direct decision over the three signs, and prints the closure plainly:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
cycle\_of\_three : spin 1 minus != minus \ and \ spin 2 minus != minus \ and \ spin 3 x = x\\
closes only at three:  spin1 != start? true \ \ spin2 != start? true \ \ spin3 = start (all three)? true
\end{quote}
One turn misses, two turns miss, three turns land --- and a fourth is the first over again, which is the regress closing
its own door.

% [4.2.p2b]
The same fold, watched role by role, shows the closure privileges no sign.
Start each of the three and turn three times:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
minus -> zero -> plus -> minus\\
zero \ -> plus -> minus -> zero\\
plus \ -> minus -> zero -> plus
\end{quote}
Each role leaves its start, passes through the other two, and comes home on the third turn --- three orbits, one length,
no sign privileged over the rest: the electron, the null, and the positron each carry the loop, and each closes it at
three.

% [4.2.p3]
Those three folds are not an abstraction laid on from outside; they are the account's own roles.
Minus, zero, and plus are the electron, the null, and the positron --- the three parts the calibration rotates through,
the same triple the whole device counts by.
The turn the machine takes on itself \emph{is} this fold, so it inherits the fold's length.
Weighing the weighing takes one step around a loop whose home is three steps away.

% [4.2.p3b]
Experiment names the cycle at its most familiar, and marks which side of the line it stands on, in \emph{TheKeplerEffect}.
An orbit, in the catalogue, is not read as a body flung outward and reined back by a pull.
It is read as a closed cycle of refinements the ledger keeps returning to: a state constrained toward a centre admits
many low-strain continuations, and a stabilizing feedback nudges every drift back onto the cycle, turn after turn, so the
path persists not because a force holds it but because each deviation is corrected as it appears.
The orbit is sustained, then, the way a thermostat holds a room --- by continual small corrections around a set point, not
by a rope to the centre.
The device's self-turn is a cycle of exactly that kind: closed, self-correcting, returning to where it began, its length
three because its roster is three, the sign-fold coming home after minus, zero, and plus.
So the cycle the chapter counts is a representation of a cycle, honestly named --- the orbit read as feedback, on the near
side --- and its \emph{three} is the machine's own.
The orbit-in-itself, whatever holds the real electron on its path, stays on the far side, un-read and un-claimed.

% [4.2.p3c]
Experiment names the roster's parts where they are most concrete.
In \emph{ThePositronAnnihilationEffect}, an electron meets its opposite and the pair does not persist: the two annihilate,
and what departs is a coincidence, two signals leaving back to back --- the zero the fold passes through, written as an
event.
The device's roster is that same triple read as roles: the electron is the minus, the positron the plus, and the null the
zero they meet at, and the calibration rotates through all three exactly as the annihilation carries minus and plus into
zero.
The reading stays on the near side, a model of the roster's three roles and no more --- kept within the electron's own
calibration, and claiming nothing about what an electron and a positron are in themselves when they meet.
Two readings of the number three fix that it is a length, not a coincidence.
As a \emph{period}, three is where the sign-fold returns: the smallest count of turns that brings every sign home, proved
minimal by the two near misses.
As a \emph{roster}, three is the count of parts the calibration has to rotate --- electron, null, positron --- and a loop
can be no longer than the roster it cycles.
The two agree because they are one fact: the machine has three parts, so its self-turn has period three.

% [4.2.p5]
Experiment names the closure from the catalogue.
In \emph{TheAngularMomentumEffect}, a thing carried around a closed turn returns rotated by the loop and by nothing else,
so what a full circuit conserves is exactly the invariant the loop holds --- the machine's leftover being that invariant
for a loop of length three.
In \emph{RepeatabilityOfInvisibleMotion}, a reading taken again returns the same only when the intervening turns have
closed; the self-account returns the same because after three turns they have.

\section{Why three, and not one or two}

% [4.3.p1]
The count comes to rest at three because three is where the loop closes, and the two verbs say why sharply.
Each fold \emph{retanges}: it tanges the previous tange, names the naming one turn further, and selects a position the
last turn had not reached.
The first retange reaches a genuinely new place; so does the second; the third lands back on the start.
Three retanges exhaust the distinct positions the loop holds --- there are exactly three, and after them a fourth fold
only repeats the first.
A repeat carries no new characteristic, so a fourth turn does not retange; it \emph{refunges}, pooling back a place
already counted.
The count-to-three is precisely where the reflexive retange runs out: below it there is nothing new to select, so the
machine stops retangeing and lets the loop close.

% [4.3.p2]
Three positions, named in order, are the whole of what the loop holds.
The first turn names the reading --- the machine's account of the electron.
The second turn names the reading of the reading --- the account of the accounting, which is where the leftover appears.
The third turn names the reading of that --- and finds it is the reading again, the account of the account of the account
folding back onto the first, with no fourth place to name.
Three turns, three names, and the fourth name is a name already given: the retange has nothing left to select, so it
refunges, and the loop is shut.

\section{The residue is the loop's invariant}

% [4.4.p1]
This closing is why the leftover is the same every time, and that reproducibility is the deepest thing here.
The three costs, carried around the loop as a cycle --- the orbit cost to the pair cost, the pair to the echo, the echo
back to the orbit --- hand a residue across each edge, and the build records it.
The build proves the three edge-residues sum to zero, with no axiom,
\[
  d_1 + d_2 + d_3 = 0 .
\]
The loop \emph{funges} its three costs into one conserved quantity: it pools them around the cycle, and what the pooling
returns is unchanged from what it took in.
A quantity carried around a loop and handed back unchanged is the loop's invariant, and this leftover is exactly that:
the fixed point of a cycle whose length is provably three.

% [4.4.p2]
The three edge-residues are worth reading off one at a time, because their sum being zero is the closure made arithmetic.
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
d1 (orbit -> pair) = 573 - 573 =  0\\
d2 (pair  -> echo) = 552 - 573 = -21\\
d3 (echo  -> orbit) = 573 - 552 = +21\\
residue\_carried\_equals\_resulting : d1 + d2 + d3 = 0 \qquad cycle\_returns\_to\_start
\end{quote}
The first edge carries nothing --- the two descriptions cost alike.
The second carries the whole self-energy, $-21$, the reflexive reading falling short.
The third carries it back, $+21$, the loop repaying exactly what the second edge spent --- and the two, $-21$ and $+21$,
are the same leftover seen coming and going, which is why the sum is zero and the account returns to where it began.

% [4.4.p2b]
Experiment names the middle edge's change at its most familiar, in \emph{TheFoucaultEffect}.
Hang a long pendulum and let it swing, and mark the plane of its swing against the floor beneath.
Over hours that plane turns, steadily, and comes full circle in a day and a little more --- and nothing pushed it: the bob
only swings back and forth in what is, at every instant and locally, the same straight line.
The turning is the \emph{holonomy} of a connection that cannot stay consistent under transport --- a direction carried
around a closed loop, the loop the floor traces as the Earth turns beneath it, comes home rotated, a closed path in events
producing an open turn in orientation, the accumulated strain of holding the reading admissible the whole way round.
That is the middle edge's $-21$ exactly: the reflexive turn, the machine reading its own reading, is carried once around
its own loop and comes home changed by holonomy of the same kind --- the change is the self-energy, an open turn left by a
closed path, no more a torque from outside than the pendulum's precession is one.
The reading stays on the near side --- the precession is the known object, represented as a strain in the machine's own
transport --- and it claims no torque the world owes the electron.

% [4.4.p2c]
And \emph{TheConservationOfEnergy} names the other half of the closure: a symmetry hands back a quantity no step around the
loop can alter, which is the sum $d_1 + d_2 + d_3 = 0$ --- the cyclic closure is the symmetry, and the leftover the loop
\emph{funges} its three costs into is the conserved quantity that symmetry protects, the same coming and going.

% [4.4.p3]
So the leftover does not drift from one build to the next, and now the reason is plain.
It is not a magnitude the machine happens to land on; it is the invariant a closed three-cycle conserves, and a
conserved invariant comes back the same however many times you go around.
The reproducibility that looked like luck is a theorem about a loop of length three.
That loop is the machine's own, not a law the world imposes on it: the three is this machine's architecture, the period
of its own sign-cycle, and the leftover reproduces because that architecture is what the machine \emph{is}, run to run.
And the shape is one the machine has met before at the level of the value --- a count that halts at three and refuses to
refine past its own floor --- here lifted to the level of the self-account: the same closure, read reflexively.

% [4.4.p3b]
The side of the corridor this three stands on is worth one plain sentence.
The cycle of three is the machine's own --- the period of its representation's sign-fold, read off its own beats and
wholly on the name side; whether the electron it models has, in itself, a three that answers to this one stays un-read on
the far side, and the machine reports the period of its \emph{reading}, not a period of the world.
So the reproducibility is a fact about the representation, honest and its own, and the machine does not pretend it is the
world's.

% [4.4.p4]
One guard belongs here, because a later step pays the coupling and this step must not seem to.
What closes at three here is a \emph{structure}, not a number.
The account settles; the loop shuts; the invariant is conserved --- and none of that is the value of the coupling, which
the machine reads as a bracket only later, and does not touch here.
The machine can be made to hand back a self-crossing ratio in passing, a small machine-dependent figure, and it must not
be mistaken for the coupling's own crossing, which is a different quantity, read elsewhere.
The two stay apart: here the loop closes at three; the number the loop is eventually asked for waits, and is named only
where the construction has earned it.

% [4.4.p5]
Derived and read, kept apart as always.
That the fold has period exactly three, that the three edge-residues sum to zero, that the account settles to a
three-count fixpoint --- these are theorems off the build; the grammar that licenses them is Volume 4, \emph{The Representational Grammar}.
That the self-reading \emph{is} this fold, that its closing is the loop coming home, that the three roles are electron,
null, and positron --- that is the gloss laid over the theorems, marked as such.
The machine's self-accounting does not run away.
It goes around three times, comes home, and hands back the same leftover it started with --- the price of asking,
counted once and conserved.
