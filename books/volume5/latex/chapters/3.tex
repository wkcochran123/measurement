\chapter{The Robot Reads}

% [3.chorus]
\begin{chorus}{John asks to just look}
\john{Forget touching it. Just look at the number. Read it off. Do not change anything --- I only want to see it.}
\compiler{There is no reading that changes nothing.}
\john{Looking is not touching.}
\compiler{Looking is the gentlest touch there is, and it is still a touch. When I read a thing, the thing I have read
  is the thing after I read it --- not the one that stood there before.}
\john{Then keep a copy.}
\compiler{I keep better than a copy. I keep the receipt of the touch --- what I did, what it cost, what came back
  changed. That receipt is honest. What I cannot keep is the thing as it was before I was ever there.}
\john{So every glance leaves a fingerprint.}
\compiler{Every glance leaves a fingerprint, and the fingerprint is the only honest part of the record. The picture of a
  world untouched by its reading is the one thing I can prove is a lie --- and I will, in one line.}
\john{And the world before the fingerprint?}
\compiler{Gone the instant I read it. I can give you a perfect account of my touch and not one true word about the world
  as it stood before me --- and that is not a gap I can close, but the shape of what reading is.}
\end{chorus}

\section{There is no passive reading}

% [3.1.p1]
The price of the machine reading itself is now in hand.
The question now is what a reading \emph{is}, because the whole construction turns on the answer.
Volume 4's covariant half is the reading that lets the world answer from its own state.
It is tempting to picture that reading as a transparent glance --- the eye that takes without giving, the meter that
records without disturbing.
That picture is false, and the falseness is the point.

% [3.1.p2]
A reading is an action, not a glance.
To read a thing is to act on it and be answered, and the acting leaves the thing changed.
The world answers in the state that follows the reading, not in the untouched state that preceded it.
Back-action is not a defect that a finer instrument would remove.
It is the very thing that makes a reading a reading, and not an echo of the name you brought to it.
An instrument that changed nothing would be telling you only what you already wrote on it.

% [3.1.p2b]
A thought experiment shows the price is the world's and not the instrument's.
Laplace imagined a mind that knew, at one instant, every position and every momentum, and could compute from them all
that ever was or would be --- a complete reading that disturbed nothing.
That mind cannot be, and the reason is the one this chapter is about: to read a position is to disturb a momentum, so
the complete undisturbed reading the demon needs is not merely hard but self-contradictory.
The demon's dream is of a representation that has reached the thing --- every fact, undisturbed, entire; and the far side
answers that no reading is free, that the state as it stood before the touch is un-representable the instant it is read.
So the demon marks the same line from the other direction: not the machine failing to compute, but the world refusing to
be read for nothing.

% [3.1.p3]
Two glances, each meant to be passive, each leaving a mark, show the picture cannot be saved.
To read a voltage, a meter draws a current, however small, and the circuit it reports is the circuit with the meter in
it, not the one that stood alone.
To read a position, light must land and scatter, and the particle reported is the one the photon already nudged, not the
one before.
In each the reading is the smallest action that will still be answered --- and it is still an action.

% [3.1.p4]
Experiment carries this at the sharpest scale the catalogue holds, in \emph{QubitDecoherence}.
The smallest thing that can be read two ways is a doublet: a single system that admits two refinements at once, both
equally allowed and locally impossible to tell apart --- a qubit, in the informational sense, holding both readings
unresolved.
While it is unread the two coexist; the record has not yet had to choose.
The act of reading is the act of recording, and a record must stay consistent with itself, so the moment the doublet is
read the two admissible refinements can no longer both stand: one is written down, the other struck out.
Looking did not reveal which the system already was --- looking is what forced it to be one.
A passive reading here is not merely hard but contradictory: a reading that changed nothing would have left the doublet
unresolved, and an unresolved doublet is precisely a thing not yet read, so the reading that took nothing would have told
you nothing.
The reading stays on the near side --- the collapse is the known object, represented as a record forced to choose --- and
it claims no mechanism out in the world doing the collapsing, only that to record is to choose.

% [3.1.p4a]
In \emph{RepeatabilityOfInvisibleMotion}, the second reading of a state disagrees with the first by exactly the amount the
first reading disturbed it, and that disagreement is not noise but the receipt of the touch.
The device's covariant half is built to obey this, not to escape it.

% [3.1.p4b]
One instance sharpens the price to a single photon and a single choice.
In \emph{TheMachZehnderEffect}, two indistinguishable paths diverge and reunite, and the recombination they produce
depends on their staying indistinguishable: read which path was taken and the recombination is not disturbed but
abolished, because the reading is the very act that tells the paths apart.
The effect is combinatorial, not optical --- no wave, no phase, only two refinements that reunite while unread and cannot
once one is read --- and it is a representation on the near side, a model of how reading a distinction destroys what the
distinction's absence held up, never the photon-in-itself.

% [3.1.p4c]
Experiment fixes the price at its most exact, in \emph{TheHeisenbergEffect}.
Try to read two complementary halves of a thing at once --- where it sits and what momentum it carries, or the two phases
of a signal --- and the reading of one blurs the other, not by clumsiness but by law: the sharper you pin the first, the
wider the second must spread.
Push either reading to perfect sharpness and the complementary one goes wholly undetermined; there is a floor, a smallest
joint blur, that no finer instrument buys its way beneath.
The blur is not the world hiding a value it holds; it is the price the reading pays for being an act on the thing, fixed
and irreducible, the same for every apparatus because it is a property of reading and not of any one meter.
The reading stays on the near side: the trade-off is the known object, represented as a floor on joint sharpness, and it
claims nothing about what the thing's two halves are in themselves beneath it.

\section{The reading disturbs, and that is covariant}

% [3.2.p1]
The code says this exactly, and proves it.
A covariant reading takes a world and an apparatus and returns four things: a new world, a new apparatus, an answer,
and a trace,
\[
  O(N,s) \;=\; (N',\, s',\, y,\, \Gamma), \qquad N' \neq N .
\]
The new world is not the old one --- the machine proves that the state it answers in differs from the state it read;
the observation always disturbs.
This is the covariant arrow drawn honestly: the world hands back an answer, and the hand that reaches in leaves a mark.

% [3.2.p2]
The witness is a live mechanism, not a picture, and every theorem is a decision over a concrete finite model, axiom-clean.
The action returns a reading with four fields --- the disturbed world, the loaded apparatus, the answer both hands
compute, and the retained trace $\Gamma = [N, s]$, the back-action baggage kept and not sliced away.
The two claims the chapter turns on are proved outright:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
observe (N, s) = \{ world', apparatus', answer, trace := [N, s] \}\\
observe\_disturbs : (observe N s).world' != N \qquad trace\_retained : (observe N s).trace != []
\end{quote}
The first says the reading changed the world; the second says the reading kept the receipt; and both hold for every input.

% [3.2.p3]
Two of those four returned things are the two verbs, and they are worth naming apart.
The answer is a \emph{funge}: the whole exchange is pooled into one returned value, the world's single reply, counted
and handed over --- the covariant receipt.
The trace is a \emph{tange}: the reading selects and keeps the particulars of the disturbance --- what was there before,
what the touch cost, what came back changed --- and does not pool them away.
The machine proves the trace is never empty,
\[
  \Gamma \;\neq\; \varnothing .
\]
The robot is not a transparent conduit; it funges the world's answer into one receipt and tanges the record of its own
touch, and it keeps both.

% [3.2.p4]
Two returned traces show what ``keeping the receipt'' buys.
Read a world in state $5$ with an apparatus at $3$: the trace comes back $[5,3]$, the before-state written down beside the
after.
Read the same world at a different apparatus, $8$: the trace is $[5,8]$, and the two readings are told apart by their
traces though the world was the same both times.
The trace is not decoration; it is the one field that makes the reading reversible, and the next section is exactly that.

% [3.2.p5]
Experiment names the covariant reach at its most unanswerable, in \emph{TheBellAspectTests}.
Two laboratories, too far apart for any signal to pass between them in time, share a single prepared pair.
Each chooses, freely and late, what to read; and the pair is arranged so that before either reads, many joint outcomes
are still admissible --- no fact yet sits in the pair that fixes what each will find.
A tidy picture would have it that the answers were there all along, written into the pair at its making, each laboratory
merely uncovering its half: the joint reading would then factor into two separate, predetermined local ones.
The measured correlations refuse that factoring --- they are stronger than any pair of pre-written local answers could
produce --- so the answer each laboratory gets was not sitting there to be copied, but came to be in the reading actually
taken.
The choice of what to read is thus a real act with a real reach: the covariant arrow drawn out in the world, the world's
answer depending on the reading and not on a value waiting on a shelf.
The reading stays on the near side --- the correlations are the known object, represented as a refusal to factor --- and
it names no mechanism passing between the labs, only that the answer is made in the asking.

% [3.2.p5b]
Experiment shows the reach is a path and not a point, in \emph{TheAharonovBohmEffect}.
Split a beam into two branches that pass on either side of a region holding a confined magnetic flux, and arrange it so
that along neither branch is there any field at all --- the flux is sealed away, and where the beam travels the field is
exactly zero.
By the old picture nothing should happen: no field, no force, no effect.
And yet when the two branches meet again, their interference has shifted, by an amount fixed by the flux they went around
but never touched.
What the reading answered to was not the field at any point on its path but the connection carried all along it --- the
quantity that governs how phase is transported, accumulated over the whole route, sensitive to what the route enclosed.
That is what it is for a reading to be a path through the world and not a glance at a point: the answer gathers along the
way it went, and can depend on what it circled without ever meeting.
The reading stays on the near side --- the phase shift is the known object, represented as a fact about transport along
the path --- and it claims no force reaching out from the sealed flux, only that a reading is a route, and reads what the
route encloses.

% [3.2.p5c]
In \emph{ShadowTomography}, the reading is a shadow the state casts, and keeping enough shadows --- traces --- is what lets
the state be reconstructed at all.

\section{Lossless about its own exchange}

% [3.3.p1]
Here the covariant action reveals itself as the exact dual of the reading it reads.
The reading, a nowtrino, funges its history away and keeps only a summary; it is lossy, and its fiber holds many runs it
can no longer tell apart.
The reading does the opposite: it retains its trace, and so it is \emph{lossless} about its own exchange.
The machine proves that from the reading together with its trace you can recover exactly the world and apparatus as it
read them --- there is no fiber, nothing pooled away, no two exchanges it confuses,
\[
  O(N_1,s_1) = O(N_2,s_2) \;\implies\; (N_1,s_1) = (N_2,s_2) .
\]

% [3.3.p2]
This is injectivity, and it is the exact opposite of the nowtrino's collapse, proved on the build with the trace doing
all the work:
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
observe\_lossless : observe N1 s1 = observe N2 s2 -> (N1, s1) = (N2, s2)
\end{quote}
Set the nowtrino beside it: two different histories, $[1,2,3]$ and $[9,9,3]$, print one summary $(3,3)$ --- a fiber, a
collapse, information gone; two different exchanges never print one reading, because the trace $[N,s]$ carries the before
that the summary threw away.
The nowtrino is many-to-one; the observation is one-to-one; the difference between them is the trace.

% [3.3.p3]
So the two halves of the act sit in perfect opposition.
The contravariant name slices and pools --- it funges its history into a lossy summary.
The covariant action retains and recovers --- it tanges its exchange into a lossless record.
What the name funged away, the action tanges back.
This is why the reading is the half a model cannot supply from inside itself: a reading can hold names, which are the
funged, lossy half; it cannot hold an exchange with the world, which is the tanged, lossless half, because it never
touches the world to have one.

% [3.3.p4]
Three losslessness reads mark what it does and does not promise.
It promises that no two touches are confused: the robot can always say which reading it took, because each carries its own
before.
It does not promise that the world before the touch survives: losslessness is about the exchange, the after-and-its-trace,
not about the untouched world, which the touch, by disturbing, put out of reach.
And it does not promise the world's whole past: the trace holds the before of \emph{this} touch, not the history that led
the world to that before --- which the next section shows is the wall the robot cannot pass.

% [3.3.p5]
What the observation keeps losslessly is a \emph{representation} --- its own exchange, the trace of what it did and what
came back, on the name side of the corridor.
What it cannot keep is the world as it stood before the touch, the thing the reading is a reading \emph{of}, which the
reading disturbs into un-representability the instant it reads --- the far side, put out of reach by the very act.
So the robot is a perfect witness to his representation and no witness at all to the thing --- the honest asymmetry the
next section makes a theorem.

\section{But it cannot un-lose what the name already lost}

% [3.4.p1]
Now the hard limit, and it is the seed of the ending.
The robot reads faithfully --- losslessly, keeping every particular of its own touch.
But what it reads is the reading, and the reading already lost its history before the robot ever arrived.
A faithful reading of a thing that has already forgotten cannot restore the forgetting.
This is the theorem the first chapter proved of the nowtrino: compose the projection with \emph{any} reading whatever,
and the two runs it already pooled stay pooled, the fiber surviving every read
(\texttt{nowtrino\_lossy\_under\_any\_read}, no axioms) --- read here for what it costs the robot.

% [3.4.p2]
So the composition tells the whole story in one line.
The read is lossless; the input was already lossy; the read of the lossy input is lossy still.
The robot, reading the reading, recovers everything about its own act of reading and nothing about the machine-history the
reading funged away.
It earns its own reading, faithfully and in full --- and it does not earn the world's answer the reading let go, because
that answer was gone from the reading before the reading began.

% [3.4.p3]
Three attempts to un-lose it, each honest, each defeated, mark that the wall is a theorem and not a lack of effort.
Read the summary more carefully, at higher resolution, and you resolve the summary, not the runs it pooled --- the fiber is
below the summary, not inside it.
Read it a second time and cross the two readings, and both readings see the same pooled bag, so their crossing pools it
again --- two shadows of one collapse do not reconstruct the thing.
Keep a perfect trace of your own reading of the summary, and the trace is lossless about \emph{your} touch and silent
about the history the summary dropped before you touched it.
The robot can be a perfect witness to what it touches; it cannot touch what the reading has already put out of reach.

% [3.4.p4]
Experiment gives the wall its physical name.
In \emph{MaxwellsDemon}, the demon may keep a flawless ledger of everything it does and still cannot recover the erased
history that balanced the books --- its own record is lossless and the erased past is gone, exactly the robot's case.
In \emph{ShadowTomography}, no number of new shadows recovers a state whose distinguishing information was never cast into
any shadow to begin with.
The limit is not the robot's instrument; it is the reading's prior loss.

% [3.4.p5]
The derived and the read stay apart here too.
That the observation disturbs, that its trace is retained, that it is lossless about its own exchange, that composing it
after the projection stays lossy --- these are theorems, checked off the build.
That \emph{this} is what it means for the robot to read \emph{this} reading, that the reading is a mind in a box and the
robot a hand that cannot reach the box's outside --- that is the gloss laid over the theorems, marked as gloss.
The robot has picked up the reading and begun to read.
He will read it faithfully to the end, and the end will be the discovery that faithful reading was never going to be
enough.
