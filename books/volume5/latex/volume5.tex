\chapter*{Preface: The Reading Reads the Reader}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface: The Reading Reads the Reader}

% [pre.p1]
Four times now the instrument has been read.
The first three volumes built it and turned it, in the languages of construction, of physics, of computation.
The fourth followed its representations and receipts and asked what licenses the names it assigns --- \emph{The
Representational Grammar}, the reading that reads the electron.
The one thing left to do with a reading is to turn the reading on \emph{itself}.

% [pre.p2]
That is not a flourish; it is the machine's own move, lifted one level.
The whole apparatus was built to weigh a thing by reading it, and its sharpest reading was the one it took of its own
act --- the cost, in its own beats, of accounting for itself.
That self-reading is where the coupling came from.
The same move, lifted to the scale of a whole reading, is what follows: we take Volume 4, which is a reading, and we
read it the way it reads.
The subject is the reading turned upon itself.
The reader it studies is the reader holding it.

% [pre.p3]
A word on the two verbs, because they run loud here where the fourth kept them silent.
To \emph{tange} is to select a thing by a characteristic --- to distinguish it, to name it, to pull it back toward what
would answer it; that is the contravariant half, and it is ours.
To \emph{funge} is to bag things by a characteristic they share --- to pool them, to count them, to let the world hand
back one answer for many; that is the covariant half, and it is the world's.
The fourth volume performed these two moves on every page and named neither.
This one, being the fourth turned on itself, must name them --- because to read a reading \emph{is} to say which of its
moves were tanges and which were funges.
Self-application is exactly the place the hidden verbs become visible.
So they saturate the construction, and the discipline that governs them is simple: name which verb each move is, and never let
a name stand where the world has not answered.

% [pre.p4]
A chorus opens each chapter.
Two voices carry the story the argument cannot: \textsc{John}, a robot who wants the number and wants it now, and
\textsc{The Compiler}, the device's own voice, which can name anything and can hand John only its own names.
The argument grades; the choruses play.
What a chorus dramatizes, the machine earns off the build --- and where a chorus lets John reach for the world's number,
the machine proves, in the end, that the reaching is exactly what it cannot do from inside.
That proof is the construction's destination.
The reading reads the reader, finds it cannot answer for the reader, and sends the reader out the door.
