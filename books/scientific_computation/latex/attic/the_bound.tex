\chapter{The Bound}

I have the four artifacts and verified the bound arithmetically (\(10^{18}/2^{60}\) floors to \(0\), so
\texttt{dedekindHalt} halts at \(n = 60\)). Here is the reading.

Open \texttt{device/Measurement/AlphaCapstone.lean}. Everything else is scaffolding for the one line that lives
there:

\begin{alltt}
theorem one_eq_point_nine_repeating_count_to_three :
    loopRem 2 4 = 0
    ∧ holonomy flatPath   (pairVariation node1 node2) = -1
    ∧ holonomy tiltedPath (pairVariation node2 node1) =  0
    ∧ holonomy tiltedPath (pairVariation node1 node2) =  1
\end{alltt}

Read it aloud as four claims. The residue closes to null --- \(1 = 0.999\ldots\), discharged \texttt{by decide},
the standard kernel-checked decision procedure you'd reach for in any Lean proof. Then three holonomy readings:
\(-1\), \(0\), \(+1\). The electron, the null identity, the positron --- count to three, and no fourth. The
\texttt{holonomy} function and its \texttt{pairVariation} argument aren't imported from \texttt{Mathlib}; they're
built here, in-house. That's the tell: where the citations run out, the new thing is happening. Pay attention to
those lines.

Now the line that makes it a theorem and not a story. The last line of the file is
\texttt{\#print axioms one\_eq\_point\_nine\_repeating\_count\_to\_three}, and it prints \texttt{[]}. Empty. Not
\texttt{[Classical.choice]}, not even the usual \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]} most Lean developments carry ---
nothing. The entire \textbf{structure} of the coupling --- the electron's state \(-1\), the three states, the
count-to-three, and the dimensionlessness that follows from the residue closing rather than resting on a borrowed
unit --- is derived on an empty axiom footprint. That is a fact off the build. Re-run the print yourself; it doesn't
need your trust.

Next, \texttt{device/Measurement/VerifyAPI.lean} --- the product surface, the part meant to be run.
\texttt{verify (ekg) (claimed)} takes a claimed calibration, runs the deterministic experiment
\texttt{EKG.outgrown?}, and returns a \texttt{VerifyResult}: \texttt{verified : Bool}, and a \texttt{residual} that
lands either \texttt{.aboveFloor} (claimable) or \texttt{.belowFloorAntimatter}. That second constructor is the
positron pun made literal --- a claim that outgrows the budget is cut below the floor, into antimatter, unclaimable.
The wrapper is deliberately thin over the already-built engine. The point isn't the code; the point is that it's a
function you can call. \texttt{\#eval verify EKG.raw flagshipClaim}, again, tomorrow, on your machine. Determinism is
the whole ethic here: repeatability \emph{is} the honesty. Nobody is asking you to believe the reading. They're
handing you the instrument and asking you to check it.

Now \texttt{RichardsonSelfNaming.lean}, and this is the novel move, so slow down.
\texttt{alphaAtLevel n :=}~\(10^{18}/2^{n}\) --- the construction's own per-level self-naming value, scaled for
exact integer arithmetic. \texttt{residueAtLevel n := alphaAtLevel n - alphaAtLevel (n+1)} --- how much the name has
\emph{not yet} closed to the thing. And \texttt{selfNamingExtrapolant :=}~\(2\cdot\alpha(n{+}1) - \alpha(n)\), the
extrapolation of the name ``electron'' to the thing electron, its own fixed point. Interpret this --- and I mark it
as interpretation --- as the electron's self-energy: ask the electron its number and it reports its state for free;
the coupling is what it \emph{charges} for the self-embrace, the electron paying to be itself. The order \(p=1\)
isn't tuned to a target; it's forced by the observed halving \(\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}\). Choosing the order to hit a
number would be fishing in a Richardson costume. It refuses to.

Finally \texttt{AlphaDedekind.lean}, the bound drawn to scale. \texttt{dedekindHalt} is ordinary structural
recursion on fuel --- standard Lean, nothing exotic --- writing the nines of \(0.999\ldots\) one bit per step and
halting the first step the residue drops below \texttt{floorEps := 1}. Then:

\begin{alltt}
def haltIndex : Nat := dedekindHalt 0 512
\end{alltt}

\texttt{\#eval haltIndex} prints \textbf{60}. You can check the arithmetic by hand: \(10^{18} \approx 2^{59.79}\), so
\texttt{alphaAtLevel 59}~\(= 1\), \texttt{alphaAtLevel 60}~\(= 0\), and the residue first vanishes at \(n = 60\).
Sixty bits. That's the bound, and \texttt{\#print axioms haltIndex} is again \texttt{[]}.

The nines it writes are the structure it can see. The place it stops is the floor. The tail it cannot write is the
magnitude. Interpretively --- Ricci trace versus Weyl shape, marked as reading, not proof --- a machine that counts
volume is blind to pure shape, and the size of the coupling lives in the shape. So \(137.036\) appears nowhere in the
reading path. It is only what a Penning trap reads off in the lab: the value the machine \emph{proved} it was
structurally blind to. Not missing. Refused, and the refusal is the theorem.

The four \texttt{\#eval}s and the four \texttt{\#print axioms} are the whole argument. Run them. That's the honesty.
