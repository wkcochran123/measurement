\chapter{What The Machine Can And Cannot Claim}

\section{What is built}

The root construction is a finite dependency graph.  Its neutral layers are
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{finite data representations and equality relations}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{transition, slicing, division, difference, and comparison algorithms}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{logical propositions, implications, equivalences, and conditional equalities}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{evaluated numerical and counter reports}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{certificates, provenance, and reserved interpretation slots}.
\end{array}
\]
An edge means that a downstream node actually consumes the upstream value or declaration.  Importing, storing, or
naming a field without consuming it creates no dependency for the reported result.

The root surface selects a small set of reports: the two reordered proposition descriptions and their equivalence,
the continued-fraction count-to-three jar, the internal ratio reading, the direct and stepped numerical diagnostics,
the three workload counter tuple, and the self-input metering report.  Each report has its own domain, algorithm,
budget, stop, and dependency footprint.  Their coexistence does not make them one result, one number, or one process.

The representation layer contains finite records, ordered lists, positive-denominator ratios, indexed samples,
three-state roles, proposition syntax, workload descriptions, and typed report schemas.  Equality is declared per
domain.  A shared numeral or spelling across schemas is not a conversion.

The algorithm layer contains finite enumeration and pigeonhole search, deterministic permutation and cycle replay,
Euclidean quotient/remainder iteration, scaled-floor projection, second-difference stencils, continued-fraction
recurrence and wall selection, bounded root and node enclosure, paired counter subtraction, dependency-graph replay,
and two-pass reconstruction.  Each algorithm exposes inputs, transitions, projections, erased fields, budgets,
stops, and validation rows.

The logical ledger distinguishes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{proposition content}&\text{the two conjunction orders and their implication maps},\\
\text{logical dependencies}&\text{declared principles used to convert equivalence into proposition equality},\\
\text{decision results}&\text{positive or negative finite branches},\\
\text{numerical implications}&\text{domain-conditional identities and interval ordering}.
\end{array}
\]
A dependency footprint is the transitive set of logical principles and declarations actually used by a claim.
An empty or small footprint reports logical dependence only; it does not establish physical truth, empirical
independence, or absence of implementation assumptions.

The evaluation ledger contains read outcomes rather than logical consequences: exact integers, rational walls,
formatted labels, counter tuples, stops, and traces.  In particular, it retains the open internal jar
\[
                         (129.6,\ 137.7)
\]
from three whole continued-fraction partial quotients and the exact direct/stepped scaled integers as distinct
diagnostics.  The superseded dyadic route owns no current bracket.

The two-pass grammar is built as a typed proposal: roles \(a,b,c\), one unresolved marker, composite
\(\Sigma\), both reconstruction maps, one role exchange between two measurement passes, separately reported overlap rows,
and a period-three role scheduler.  Its internal algebra and scheduler can be checked on finite generated data.
Physical calibration remains conditional on a covariant apparatus action and world response.

\paragraph{Worked audit 1: graph reachability (computation).}
To audit a jar endpoint, start at the endpoint node and traverse parents: inverse-ratio map, rational wall,
continued-fraction recurrence, partial quotients, and crossing equation.  A stored quadrature weight is not reachable
and therefore is not a dependency of the endpoint.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: an imported name is not a built result.}
A report schema may include a field called field closure or particle identity.  Unless an algorithm computes and a
certificate validates that claim under a typed domain, the field is a name or input, not a demonstrated result.

The action ledger has four statuses:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{posited}&\text{schemas, domains, transition laws, maps, budgets, and logical dependencies},\\
\textsc{demonstrated}&\text{finite identities and implications derived under those declarations},\\
\textsc{read}&\text{evaluated or observed report values and branches},\\
\textsc{reserved}&\text{claims not licensed by the preceding three}.
\end{array}
\]
Every node carries exactly the statuses relevant to its claims; a read numeral does not become demonstrated physical
meaning, and a demonstrated identity does not become a world observation.

Built, measured, and interpreted are likewise distinct.  Built means the finite representation, algorithm, relation,
or certificate grammar exists and passes its internal checks.  Measured means a governed run or apparatus returns a
value with provenance.  Interpreted means a declared model assigns meaning to that value.  A physical interpretation
requires the no-name bridge: typed comparison map, calibrated apparatus, observational model, uncertainty, interaction
trace, back-action, and provenance.

The graph-audit algorithm parses nodes and typed edges, validates unique identifiers and versions, checks that every
consumed input is declared, topologically sorts the graph, rejects cycles where acyclicity is required, replays each
finite claim, computes transitive dependency footprints, and slices the ledger by status, report, route, and claim.
Projection nodes record their fibers and erasures.

Budgets distinguish graph nodes and edges, dependency traversal, logical normalization, numerical replay, certificate
validation, storage, and report slicing.  Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{schema-failure},
\textsc{missing-node}, \textsc{type-mismatch}, \textsc{version-mismatch}, \textsc{orphan-dependency},
\textsc{cyclic-dependency}, \textsc{unused-field-claim}, \textsc{logical-dependency-missing},
\textsc{evaluation-missing}, \textsc{budget}, \textsc{replay-failure}, and
\textsc{provenance-incomplete}.

A root certificate records graph schema, nodes and typed edges, consumed and unused fields, representations,
algorithms, proposition and implication rows, logical dependency footprints, evaluations, action statuses, projections
and erasures, budgets, stops, checked scope, unresolved frontier, releases, dependency lock, snapshot hash,
configuration, protocol, run identity, platform, deterministic inputs, and declared nondeterministic inputs.

Comparison reports exact equality after typed conversion, resolution equality under a declared quantizer, uncertainty
compatibility under a declared region model, and same-process under compatible inputs, transitions, traces, resources,
configuration, and provenance.  These are separate rows.  Graph isomorphism additionally requires a typed
node-and-edge bijection preserving consumption and status.

Governance fixes the root report set, graph and status schemas, logical dependencies, algorithm versions, ownership
routes, certificate checks, comparison relations, budgets, stop priority, and release provenance.  A changed root or
dependency begins a new audit.

The construction is a model of one electron reading only.  It is not the world, a nucleus, a catalogue of particles,
or a universal theory.  Contravariant naming produces the model's own classifications.  A covariant world answer
requires
\[
(N,s)\longmapsto(N',s',y,\Gamma)
\]
with apparatus state, prior state and history, intervention, actuation, interaction trace, geometry, calibration,
environment, sensor loading, back-action, energy and material exchange, latency, hysteresis, drift, saturation,
rejected outcomes, uncertainty, and alternative causal models.

The action ledger is exact.  Root selection, schemas, domains, maps, algorithms, logical dependencies, status rules,
comparisons, budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Finite representation invariants, algorithm
identities, logical implications, dependency reachability, replay and footprint implications, and certificate
composition are \emph{demonstrated}.  Evaluated integers, rationals, labels, branches, traces, counters, stops, and
provenance are \emph{read}.  Built as measured, measured as interpreted, a name as world answer, physical truth from
a logical footprint, universal scope, other-particle scope, and every claim absent its typed dependency remain
\emph{reserved}.
\section{What is measured}

A measurement report begins with governed conditions, not a bare numeral.  The computational inventory is
\[
\mathcal M_{\rm comp}=(\text{release},\text{dependencies},\text{snapshot},
\text{configuration},\text{inputs},\text{algorithm},\text{budgets},
\text{raw readings},\text{trace},\text{stop},\text{provenance}).
\]
Changing any identifying field creates a new measurement context.

\]

CPU time, energy, or physical cost.  Their reproducibility is evaluated only across matched releases, dependencies,
snapshots, configurations, workloads, setup, warmup, cache policy, budgets, deterministic inputs, and declared

\paragraph{Worked audit 1: counter build reproduction (computation).}
Two governed builds return \((573,573,552)\).  Exact tuple equality is read.  The audit separately validates releases,

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: equal output under a changed configuration (computation).}

Parameter sensitivity is measured by governed perturbations.  For parameter vector \(\theta\), report
\[
                         y(\theta),\qquad
S_j(h)=\frac{y(\theta+he_j)-y(\theta-he_j)}{2h}
\]
when both perturbed runs are in domain.  This centered sensitivity is a numerical estimate whose interpretation
requires a declared step \(h\), arithmetic model, smoothness or local-fit assumption, and error study.  One-sided
differences are separate methods at boundaries.

Sensitivity iteration evaluates preregistered perturbation levels \(h,h/2,h/4\), records raw changes, checks
cancellation and arithmetic resolution, and estimates stability only within a compatible family.  Richardson
extrapolation requires an analytically justified or independently stabilized order, decreasing raw differences, and
a demonstrated remainder bound.  Selecting \(h\) or an order after seeing a desired target is rejected.

\paragraph{Worked audit 3: discrete parameter sensitivity (computation).}
For integer parameter \(K\), changing \(K=3\) to \(K=4\) changes the continued-fraction report and ownership depth.
The finite difference \(J(4)-J(3)\) compares two reports; it is not a derivative unless a continuous embedding and
model justify that name.  The exact report difference remains useful as a discrete sensitivity.

Build-to-build analysis slices measurements by release, dependency set, snapshot, configuration, platform, cold/warm
state, cache policy, run order, outcome, stop, and deterministic or nondeterministic input.  It retains failed and
rejected runs.  Paired or blocked statistics require the declared sampling and dependence model; naive independent
variance is not used for correlated builds.

Budgets distinguish structure parsing, iterations, counter allowance, arithmetic and bit growth, route evaluations,

A certificate records toolchain and counter semantics, releases, dependency lock, snapshot hash, configuration,

Physical measurement is a different relation:
\[
                         (N,s)\longmapsto(N',s',y,\Gamma).

Governance fixes measurement schemas, counter semantics, releases, configurations, workload and numerical routes,

The measured scope is the model's one electron reading and its computational diagnostics.  It is not the world, a
nucleus, other particles, or a universal computational limit.  The laboratory value \(137.036\) is neither contained,
approached, nor compared by proximity here.

The action ledger is exact.  Measurement contexts, routes, parameters, perturbations, sampling, sensitivity models,

reproducibility across unmatched builds, process identity from equal outputs, derivative meaning for arbitrary
discrete differences, target-fitted sensitivity, hidden magnitude, uncomputability of alpha, universal impossibility,
laboratory containment or proximity, and every world claim absent intervention remain \emph{reserved}.
\section{What is interpreted}

The representational grammar is finite because every map has a checked domain, and local because no successful map
is promoted beyond that domain, route, resolution, and provenance.  Interpretation begins after construction and
reading, not before.  The closing ledger has three columns:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{built}&\text{finite representations, algorithms, relations, and certificate grammars},\\
\textsc{measured}&\text{governed computational reports or calibrated apparatus observations},\\
\textsc{interpreted}&\text{names and models assigned under declared bridges and scope}.
\end{array}
\]
A fourth status, \textsc{reserved}, marks a claim for which no licensed bridge is present.

The name-resonance row is explicit:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\operatorname{nowtrino}(H)&\text{current computational projection after history slicing},\\
\operatorname{newtrino}(s)&\text{next computational state under a declared update},\\
\operatorname{naotrino}&\text{manuscript physical candidate requiring an apparatus record},\\
\nu\text{-trino}&\text{the conventional neutrino-physics register}.
\end{array}
\]
The declared update may connect nowtrino to newtrino after their representations are typed.  No spelling, sound, or
three-fold pattern connects either computational name to naotrino or \(\nu\)-trino.  A physical naotrino remains
reserved until system, observable, units, apparatus, calibration, uncertainty, interaction, and discriminating data
are supplied.  The manuscript coinage derives from Greek \emph{naos}, meaning temple.  This etymology supplies
provenance for the name, not evidence for a physical entity.  It is not the conventional neutrino.

The internal name \(\alpha_c\) belongs first to the compiler and model ledger.  It names the model-owned electron
reading generated by the internal formula and routes.  It does not name a laboratory coupling or a property of the
world.  The computation owns the open jar
\[
                         (129.6,\ 137.7),
\]
whose endpoints are exact diagnostics obtained from adjacent continued-fraction walls at \(K=3\).  The jar is an
internal interval report; it does not assert a true value caught inside.

The reader can replay the built two-pass grammar on governed computational records.  Its pass-one and pass-two roles
exchange once, apply the two reconstruction directions, and report overlap and round-trip rows.  Physical covariant
apparatus passes are auditable only when two corresponding intervention receipts exist.  The scheduler separately has
period three:
\[
                         a\mapsto b\mapsto c\mapsto a.
\]
That algebraic period is not three measurement passes.  Two passes with one role exchange are the calibration
protocol; three scheduler steps are a property of role ordering.

The numerical route ledger also remains split.  At scale \(10^{18}\),

\paragraph{Final audit 4: positron annihilation as a licensing stress test.}
This finite apparatus domain contains a detector
\(d=(L,w,n)\)---line energy, acceptance window, and direction-slot count---and two photon records
\(p=(E_p,\theta_p)\), \(q=(E_q,\theta_q)\).  For a physical directional reading, restrict to the
positive-modulus subdomain
\[
 \mathcal D_+=\{(d,p,q):d.\mathrm{slots}=n>0\}.
\]
On that restricted domain the interpreted transform is
\[
 T_+:\mathcal D_+\longrightarrow\mathsf{Bool},\qquad
 T_+(d,p,q)=\operatorname{decide}\bigl(
 |E_p-L|\le w\ \land\ |E_q-L|\le w\ \land\
 (\theta_p+n/2)\bmod n=\theta_q\bmod n\bigr),
\]
written in the source with natural-safe inequalities rather than signed absolute values.  The Lean structure and raw
\texttt{run} remain total even at \(n=0\), because natural-number remainder is total there; the source does not enforce
the positive-slot invariant.  Consequently the displayed directional interpretation and every physical use are
restricted to \(n>0\), while a zero-slot setup is only an uninterpreted formal Boolean case.

For \(L=511,w=8,n=360\), records \((511,0)\) and \((511,180)\) pass two independent line gates and
\((0+180)\bmod360=180\).  Energies \(502\) and \(520\) exercise opposite window failures; equal directions exercise
the half-turn failure.  Summing to \(1022\) would erase the implemented individual gates, while retaining only the
coincidence bit would erase energies and directions.

For this finite detector, the comparison square commutes exactly:
\[
 T_+(d,p,q)=\mathsf{true}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d.\operatorname{registers}(p,q).
\]
The audited long route calls the same setup-local predicate and agrees with the short route, while the false control
shows that agreement is not obtained from a constant claim.  In a different register, \texttt{claim\_holds} also depends on
the Measurement device's structural cancellation theorem and its sign-sensitive tilted/flat positron-event theorems.
That dependency means a device regression can break the formal receipt.  It does not mean the detector run measured
those device fields, nor that the shared word ``positron'' makes the two processes identical.

The two failure rows remain separate.  A true detector predicate with a broken native dependency fails the composite
claim without rewriting the photon inputs; a false detector predicate is not repaired by intact cancellation and
sign theorems.  One row audits the finite detector transform, the other the imported device proof surface.

The literal \(511\) is an external label, not derived by the device.  The predicate proves two line windows and a
direction relation, not equal energies, total-energy conservation, or annihilation.  The audit accepts that finite
transform and keeps the device cancellation/sign theorems as a distinct native dependency rather than detector data.

The scope remains one model-owned electron reading.  There is no electron identity claim, no extension to a nucleus
or other particles, and no universal theory.  There is no self-energy or physical coupling claim.  The closing
achievement is narrower: the model states its own names, the computation reports what it read, the apparatus boundary
states what would be required for the world to answer, and the ledger refuses every unsupported crossing.
