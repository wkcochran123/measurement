\chapter{Difference Becomes Countable}

\section{Counting is an interface}

Counting begins with four finite capabilities, not with a completed enumeration.  A neutral grammar is
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\textit{counting-capability}&::=&
 \operatorname{counting}(\textit{carrier},\textit{tally},U),\\
\textit{admission-capability}&::=&
 \operatorname{admission}(\textit{counting-capability},A),\\
\textit{indexing-capability}&::=&
 \operatorname{indexing}(\textit{counting-capability},\textit{origin},V),\\
\textit{bounding-capability}&::=&
 \operatorname{bounding}(\textit{indexing-capability},B).
\end{array}
\]
Here \(U\) updates a tally-shaped record, \(A\) asks an admission question, \(V\) updates an indexed record from a
declared origin, and \(B\) asks a bound question.  The capabilities provide places for finite data and questions.
They do not guarantee that repeated updates are distinct, that every candidate appears, or that an index assignment is
one-to-one or onto.

Use the neutral forms
\[
 \operatorname{halt}(\rho),\qquad
 \operatorname{step}(\rho,\nu,T),
\]
with receipt \(\rho\), numeral record \(\nu\), and recursive tail \(T\).  Arithmetic meaning for \(\nu\) remains
reserved.  The default counting update has a head-update/tail-reset shape:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 U(\operatorname{halt}(\rho))
   &=\operatorname{halt}(\rho_*),\\
 U(\operatorname{step}(\rho,\nu,T))
   &=\operatorname{step}(\rho_*,\nu_*,T_*),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\rho_*,\nu_*,T_*\) are the current receipt, carrier payload, and stored tally supplied by the capability.  The
input tail does not survive the second branch.  The default indexing update likewise uses a fixed origin \(O\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V(\operatorname{halt}(\rho))
   &=\operatorname{step}(\rho,c,O),\\
 V(\operatorname{step}(\rho,\nu,T))
   &=\operatorname{step}(\rho,U(\nu),O).
\end{aligned}
\]
It retains the input head receipt, updates the head payload, and resets the tail to \(O\).

\paragraph{Worked example 1: same head, different tail (computation).}
Let
\[
 R_1=\operatorname{step}(\rho,\nu,T_1),\qquad
 R_2=\operatorname{step}(\rho,\nu,T_2),\qquad T_1\ne T_2.
\]
The default recurrence gives
\[
                       V(R_1)=V(R_2)=\operatorname{step}(\rho,U(\nu),O).
\]
Thus the update is many-to-one: records with the same head and different tails share an output fiber.  Iteration cannot
reconstruct the discarded tail, so this recurrence alone cannot certify enumeration of its input records.

The default admission question is also only a question.  On neutral numeral forms it has three branches:
\[
 A(s,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf{yes},&s\text{ is an anchor},\\
 \mathsf{no},&s\text{ is a step and }t\text{ is an anchor},\\
 R(\rho_t)\lor Q(\nu_s,\nu_t),&s,t\text{ are steps}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here \(R\) is the declared question carried by the threshold receipt; an affirmative receipt answer admits the pair
without consulting \(Q\).
The default bound question has a related orientation rule:
\[
 B(s,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf{yes},&s\text{ is an anchor},\\
 \mathsf{no},&s\text{ is a step and }t\text{ is an anchor},\\
 [\operatorname{parity}(\rho_s,\rho_t)=\mathsf{agree}\land Q(i_s,i_t)]\\
 \quad{}\lor[\operatorname{parity}(\rho_s,\rho_t)=\mathsf{disagree}\land Q(i_t,i_s)],
 &s,t\text{ are steps}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Agreement preserves the comparison arguments; disagreement reverses them.  Neither capability supplies a resolver for
\(A\), \(B\), or \(Q\), and none proves reflexivity, transitivity, antisymmetry, totality, or any other order law.
The questions and defaults may be replaced in another declared capability; subsequent slices then concern the
replacements.

The actual iteration is the forward orbit
\[
                   r_0=O,\qquad r_{k+1}=V(r_k).
\]
For an actual finite run with transition budget \(N\), record \(r_0=O\), then apply \(V\) for
\(k=0,\ldots,N-1\).  Stop with \textsc{budget} after the \(N\)-th transition.  The certificate contains the declared
origin, budget \(N\), all \(N\) transition pairs \((r_k,r_{k+1})\), the receipts retained inside the recorded values,
the prefix \([r_0,\ldots,r_N]\) of \(N+1\) values, every declared
erasure, and the stop reason.  It certifies only those \(N\) transitions and says nothing about a further update.

This is a covariant construction.  Given a question \(q\) on orbit values, reading is contravariant pullback:
\[
                   V^*q=q\circ V.
\]
For composable forward maps \(W\) and \(V\),
\[
                   (V\circ W)^*q=W^*(V^*q).
\]
Receipt-directed reversal inside \(B\) is not categorical variance; it is merely a branch that exchanges two
arguments.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: an orbit need not enumerate (computation).}
Let a permitted update satisfy \(V(a)=b\) and \(V(b)=a\).  Starting at \(a\), the orbit is
\[
             [r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4,r_5,r_6]=[a,b,a,b,a,b,a]
             \qquad(N=6).
\]
A six-transition run has seven recorded values but only two distinct values.  It demonstrates iteration and a
two-cycle, not seven distinct indexed objects.  Therefore an update interface, even with a budget and origin, is not an enumeration
certificate.

A separate finite indexing experiment can be posited, but it must be named as external.  Given
\(L=[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\), capacity \(K\), budget \(N\), and an explicitly declared resolver \(\Delta_A\) for the chosen
admission question, visit while \(j\le n,K,N\).  Resolve \(\Delta_A(x_j)\), append admitted rows to a ledger and
rejected rows to a complement slice, then increment \(j\).  Stop with \textsc{complete}, \textsc{capacity},
\textsc{budget}, or \textsc{resolver-domain}.  Its certificate contains the visited prefix, both slices, assigned and
unused indices, unvisited suffix, and stop reason.  For \(v\) visits,
\[
                  |\text{admitted}|+|\text{rejected}|=v.
\]
This generic \(L/K/N\) traversal, its assignment \(\iota\), and its resolver are \emph{posited} analysis machinery;
they are not contained in the four capability names.

A genuine finite enumeration claim needs an encoder and decoder.  For finite sets \(X\) and \(I\), require both
round trips:
\[
             \operatorname{dec}(\operatorname{enc}(x))=x
             \quad(x\in X),\qquad
             \operatorname{enc}(\operatorname{dec}(i))=i
             \quad(i\in I).
\]

\paragraph{Worked example 3: good and bad round trips (computation).}
For \(X=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b,\mathtt c\}\) and \(I=\{1,2,3\}\), a good pair is
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&\mathtt a&\mathtt b&\mathtt c\\ \hline
\operatorname{enc}(x)&1&2&3
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
i&1&2&3\\ \hline
\operatorname{dec}(i)&\mathtt a&\mathtt b&\mathtt c .
\end{array}
\]
Both round trips return their inputs.  A bad assignment is
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&\mathtt a&\mathtt b&\mathtt c\\ \hline
\operatorname{enc}_{\rm bad}(x)&1&1&2
\end{array}
\]
because the collision at \(1\) forces any decoder to choose at most one of \(\mathtt a,\mathtt b\).  Index \(3\) is
also unused.  No decoder can satisfy both round trips, regardless of the interface name.

Shannon's codebook is another map, \(C:I\to\mathcal W\), not the index assignment.  With a declared discrete
distribution \(p(i)>0\),
\[
 I(i)=-\log_2p(i),\qquad
 \bar\ell=\sum_i p(i)\ell(C(i)).
\]
These require probabilities and word lengths~\cite{shannon1948}.  Unambiguous decoding requires a declared uniquely
decodable or prefix condition.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: an index is not its word (communication model).}
Let \(p(1)=1/2\), \(p(2)=p(3)=1/4\), with
\(C(1)=\mathtt0\), \(C(2)=\mathtt{10}\), and \(C(3)=\mathtt{11}\).  The words are prefix-free, and direct calculation
gives entropy \(3/2\) bits and expected length \(3/2\) bits.  This equality is demonstrated for this distribution and
codebook only.  It identifies neither an index with its word nor a capability orbit with an encoded message.

\paragraph{Worked example 5: counted threshold crossings (physics, then interpretation).}
Suppose an apparatus yields a finite acquisition record of threshold crossings.  Physics owns the sensor, threshold,
dead time, sampling interval, missed-event rate, and false-event rate.  The external traversal may index accepted
records.  Its certificate counts those accepted records, not unobserved physical events.  Interpreting the ledger as an
event count remains reserved until the experimental error model is supplied.

The action ledger now separates the claims.  The four capabilities, their defaults, and the external \(L/K/N\)
experiment are \emph{posited}.  A finite orbit trace, fiber equality, cycle, slice, or pair of checked round trips is
\emph{demonstrated}.  Questions on orbit values or indices are \emph{read} contravariantly through forward maps.
Full enumeration, bijection, cardinality, and physical event count remain \emph{reserved}.  Counting is an interface
for finite updates and questions, not a guarantee hidden in a name.

\section{The update and the alleged endpoint}

The local capability stores an indexing capability, a named endpoint record, a current chain, and a chain update.  The
step capability stores that local capability, a current piece, a named accumulation, and a second update.  A neutral
grammar is
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\textit{chain}&::=&\operatorname{end}(\textit{receipt})
 \mid\operatorname{entry}(\textit{receipt},\textit{piece},\textit{chain}),\\
\textit{accumulation}&::=&\operatorname{empty}(\textit{receipt})
 \mid\operatorname{indexed}(\textit{receipt},\textit{chain},\textit{accumulation}),\\
\textit{local-process}&::=&
 \operatorname{local}(\textit{indexing},\textit{named-endpoint},\textit{current-chain},W),\\
\textit{step-process}&::=&
 \operatorname{step}(L_{\rm self},\textit{current-piece},
                     \textit{named-accumulation},U).
\end{array}
\]
These capabilities posit records and updates.  The words endpoint and accumulation attach names; they supply no metric,
error bound, or statement about an infinite tail.

Let \(L_{\rm ext}\) be a separately supplied local process, with stored chain \(S_{\rm ext}\) and update
\(W_{\rm ext}\).  Let \(A_*\) be the stored accumulation, \(O_\rho\) the origin piece made from receipt
\(\rho\), and \(V\) the indexing update on pieces.  The external local update is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_{\rm ext}(\operatorname{end}(\rho))
   &=\operatorname{entry}(\rho,O_\rho,S_{\rm ext}),\\
 W_{\rm ext}(\operatorname{entry}(\rho,p,T))
   &=\operatorname{entry}(\rho,V(p),S_{\rm ext}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both branches reset the output tail to \(S_{\rm ext}\); the input tail \(T\) is discarded.  The default step update is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 U(\operatorname{empty}(\rho))
   &=\operatorname{indexed}(\rho,S_{\rm ext},A_*),\\
 U(\operatorname{indexed}(\rho,S,T))
   &=\operatorname{indexed}(\rho,W_{\rm ext}(S),A_*).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both branches reset the outer tail to \(A_*\).  This update also ignores the stored current piece in the step
capability and the stored \(L_{\rm self}\); it uses the chain and update from external dependency
\(L_{\rm ext}\).  A name suggesting approximation cannot restore any discarded tail or make either unused record
operative.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: equal head, unequal tails (default recurrence).}
Let
\[
 C_1=\operatorname{indexed}(\rho,S,T_1),\qquad
 C_2=\operatorname{indexed}(\rho,S,T_2),\qquad T_1\ne T_2.
\]
Then
\[
             U(C_1)=U(C_2)=\operatorname{indexed}(\rho,W_{\rm ext}(S),A_*).
\]
The two inputs occupy one output fiber.  This tail-reset equality is demonstrated directly from the recurrence.  It
does not establish that either input approaches the named accumulation.

For a finite run, choose origin \(x_0\) and budget \(N\), then compute
\[
                         x_{k+1}=U(x_k),\qquad k=0,\ldots,N-1.
\]
The budget certificate contains \(x_0\), \(N\), all transition pairs, the \(N+1\) recorded values, receipts retained in
them, declared erasures, and \textsc{budget}.  It certifies only those transitions.  A slice may ask any declared
question \(q\) of the recorded values.  Construction is covariant along \(U\); reading is contravariant:
\[
                    U^*q=q\circ U,\qquad
                    (U\circ Z)^*q=Z^*(U^*q).
\]
Stable accepted and rejected slices preserve within-slice order and lose cross-slice interleaving unless ledger
positions are retained.

A Cauchy assertion requires independent mathematical data.  On a declared metric space \((X,d)\), it is
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists M\ \forall m,n\ge M,\qquad d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon.
\]
A modulus gives a rule \(M(\varepsilon)\).  The local and step capabilities contain no metric or modulus, and their
default recurrences do not establish this quantified statement.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: fixed update (external metric experiment).}
Outside the capability grammar, declare \(X=\{3\}\), \(U(3)=3\), and the discrete metric.  With \(N=4\), the finite
trace is
\[
                          [3,3,3,3,3].
\]
All four transition pairs check.  The separately declared rule and metric also justify the Cauchy assertion, because
all pairwise distances vanish.  The justification comes from those external premises, not from a named endpoint.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: alternating update (external metric experiment).}
Declare \(X=\{-1,1\}\), \(U(-1)=1\), \(U(1)=-1\), ordinary distance, and \(N=6\).  The exact trace is
\[
                         [-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1].
\]
For \(\varepsilon=1\), values arbitrarily far along the infinite-tail rule remain distance \(2\) apart.  This supplies
a counterexample to the Cauchy assertion while preserving a valid finite update trace.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: first tolerance hit (external numerical experiment).}
Declare \(x_0=1\), \(U(x)=x/2\), alleged endpoint \(a=0\), ordinary distance, and
\(\varepsilon=1/8\).  Four transitions give
\[
                    [1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac18,\tfrac1{16}].
\]
The question \(q_\varepsilon(x)=[d(x,a)\le\varepsilon]\) first answers yes at ledger position three, producing
\[
 R_\varepsilon=[1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14],\qquad
 A_\varepsilon=[\tfrac18,\tfrac1{16}].
\]
This demonstrates a first hit in the finite trace.  Persistence requires an additional invariant; the stopping question
alone does not certify the unread infinite tail.

Finite descriptions provide a separate computation-side resonance.  Fix a declared prefix-free machine \(M\).  Its
description complexity is
\[
                 K_M(x)=\min\{|p|:M(p)=x\},
\]
when at least one halting description \(p\) produces \(x\).  The value depends on the chosen machine and encoding.
Chaitin's results connect such measures with algorithmic complexity and irreducibility~\cite{chaitin1974}; they do not
turn every short update into an uncomputability result.

\paragraph{Worked example 5: description budget and displayed storage (external accounting model).}
Assume a fixed-width display in which the update description occupies \(h\) symbols and each recorded value occupies
exactly \(b\) symbols.  Displaying an \(N\)-transition trace then uses
\[
                           h+(N+1)b
\]
symbols before receipts and formatting overhead.  This is an accounting formula under the fixed-width assumptions,
not a universal lower bound and not a value of \(K_M\).  A compact update may coexist with a long displayed trace.

The action ledger is exact.  The local and step capabilities, their stored records, and the default \(W,U\)
recurrences are \emph{posited}.  The two tail resets, ignored current piece, ignored stored local process, use of the
external dependency, finite traces, output-fiber equality, and
checked slices are \emph{demonstrated}.  Questions are \emph{read} contravariantly through forward updates.  A Cauchy
property, modulus, persistent enclosure, mathematical endpoint, physical steady state, or algorithmic-complexity claim
remains \emph{reserved} until its additional premises are supplied.  The capabilities update finite records; their
names do not certify an infinite tail.

\section{A finite trace is not an infinite object}

A finite run and a request without a preset bound are different grammatical forms:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\textit{finite-run}&::=&\operatorname{run}(U,x_0,N),\\
\textit{open-request}&::=&\operatorname{continue}(U,x_0,\textit{stop-question}),\\
\textit{trace}&::=&[x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_k],\qquad x_{j+1}=U(x_j).
\end{array}
\]
The first supplies a transition budget \(N\).  The second supplies a stopping question but no advance claim that the
question will ever answer yes.  Neither grammar stores an infinite object.  Words such as iterative, infinite, or
unbounded can name capabilities or intentions; they do not establish termination, nontermination, distinctness, or
coverage.

For a budgeted run, apply \(U\) for \(j=0,\ldots,N-1\).  The certificate contains the origin, budget, all transition
pairs, the \(N+1\) recorded values, retained receipts, declared erasures, and stop reason.  It certifies exactly the
visited prefix.  Construction carries states forward through \(U\).  A question \(q\) about a state is read
contravariantly:
\[
                 U^*q=q\circ U,\qquad
                 (U\circ V)^*q=V^*(U^*q).
\]
A slice may separate first occurrences from repeated occurrences, accepted from rejected states, or positions before
and after a detected collision.  Stable slicing preserves within-slice traversal order and loses interleaving unless
indices remain attached.

Pigeonhole reasoning must be scoped.  If a finite set \(X\) has \(m\) states, any list of \(m+1\) states contains a
collision.  That statement alone proves only
\[
                    \exists\,i<j\le m,\qquad x_i=x_j.
\]
It says nothing about how the list was generated.  To infer an eventual periodic orbit, one additionally needs a
single deterministic, time-independent update satisfying \(x_{k+1}=U(x_k)\).  Then equality at \(i<j\) implies by
induction
\[
                    x_{i+t}=x_{j+t}\qquad(t\ge0),
\]
so the orbit is periodic from \(i\) with candidate period \(\lambda=j-i\).  Calling \(i\) the least preperiod
\(\mu\), or \(\lambda\) the least period, additionally requires first-repeat and minimality checks.  Collision plus
transition compatibility earns a periodic tail; collision alone does not.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: collision without dynamics (computation).}
Take the finite list
\[
                         [\mathtt a,\mathtt b,\mathtt a,\mathtt c].
\]
Pigeonhole identifies the repeated \(\mathtt a\).  No update rule is declared, so there is no licensed next state after
either occurrence and no cycle certificate.  Calling the list an orbit would add a premise not present in the data.

\paragraph{Worked example 1b: projected collision is not state collision (computation).}
Let \(S=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b,\mathtt c\}\), with
\[
 U(\mathtt a)=\mathtt b,\qquad U(\mathtt b)=\mathtt c,\qquad U(\mathtt c)=\mathtt c,
\]
and observation projection
\[
 \pi(\mathtt a)=0,\qquad\pi(\mathtt b)=0,\qquad\pi(\mathtt c)=1.
\]
The first two observations collide even though \(\mathtt a\ne\mathtt b\); their successors also project differently.
Thus a projection collision proves neither state equality nor a cycle, and it does not select which underlying states
should be identified.  For \(m\) observed values in an alphabet of size \(r<m\), finite pigeonhole reasoning proves
only
\[
                 \exists\,i<j<m,\qquad \pi(x_i)=\pi(x_j).
\]
The projection carries observations forward.  A question \(q\) on the readout alphabet is read contravariantly:
\[
                            \pi^*q=q\circ\pi.
\]
Forward observation and pulled-back reading do not upgrade equality of readouts to equality of states.

A visited-map detector makes the missing premise explicit.  Initialize a finite map
\(H[x_0]=0\).  For \(j=1,\ldots,N\), compute \(x_j=U(x_{j-1})\).  If \(x_j\notin H\), store \(H[x_j]=j\).  If
\(x_j\in H\), return
\[
                  \mu=H[x_j],\qquad\lambda=j-\mu,
\]
together with the two equal states and all transitions between them.  Stop with \textsc{cycle}, \textsc{budget}, or
\textsc{resolver-domain}.  This requires decidable state equality and a declared deterministic update.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: visited-map cycle certificate (computation).}
Let \(U\) act on \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) by
\[
             U(0)=1,\quad U(1)=2,\quad U(2)=3,\quad U(3)=2,\quad U(4)=4.
\]
From \(x_0=0\), the detector records
\[
                         [0,1,2,3,2]
\]
and stops at \(j=4\), because state \(2\) first appeared at index \(2\).  It returns
\[
                         \mu=2,\qquad\lambda=2.
\]
The transition pairs \(2\mapsto3\mapsto2\), together with determinism, certify the repeating tail
\(2,3,2,3,\ldots\).  State \(4\) is irrelevant to this orbit; a cycle certificate is not coverage of the state set.

Floyd's detector trades stored history for additional updates.  Set the tortoise to \(U(x_0)\) and the hare to
\(U(U(x_0))\).  Repeatedly update the tortoise once and the hare twice until they meet or a transition budget is
exhausted.  A meeting is a collision under the same deterministic update.  Separate finite passes then recover
\(\mu\) and \(\lambda\).  Its certificate must record every evaluated transition, the meeting state, budgets for all
passes, and the recovered cycle parameters.  Without decidable equality or total updates on the visited states, the
method has no licensed comparison.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: Floyd detection on a finite orbit (computation).}
For the update in the previous example and origin \(0\),
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{round}&0&1\\ \hline
\text{tortoise}&1&2\\
\text{hare}&2&2
\end{array}
\]
so the first meeting is at round one.  A subsequent entry-point pass recovers \(\mu=2\), and a lap from state \(2\)
recovers \(\lambda=2\).  The arithmetic agrees with the visited-map
result, but the algorithms have different traces and storage costs.  Agreement of \((\mu,\lambda)\) does not identify
the methods.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: budget exhaustion is inconclusive (computation).}
Let \(U(n)=n+1\) on nonnegative integer records, origin \(0\), and \(N=5\).  The certificate is
\[
                         [0,1,2,3,4,5],\qquad\textsc{budget}.
\]
No collision appears in the prefix.  This proves only six distinct visited values.  It proves neither that a later
collision occurs nor that the run continues forever.  The open request and the finite certificate must remain
separate.

Turing's halting result concerns a precisely encoded universal decision problem: no single algorithm decides for
every encoded machine and input whether that machine halts~\cite{turing1936}.  It does not imply that a particular
finite run lacks a termination certificate, and a budget stop is not evidence of undecidability.  Conversely, a local cycle
certificate settles the behavior of that declared deterministic orbit without solving the universal problem.

Shannon supplies a probability fence.  Information calculations require a declared finite alphabet and readout map;
empirical frequencies in one finite trace are estimates, not automatically source probabilities.  Entropy needs a
probability distribution, while capacity additionally needs a channel law and an optimization domain
\cite{shannon1948}.  Repetition introduced by an encoding or deterministic update is not, by itself, a stochastic
process.

Chaitin supplies a second computation-side fence.  Fix a declared prefix-free machine \(M\) and define
\[
                         K_M(x)=\min\{|p|:M(p)=x\}.
\]
The value depends on \(M\) up to the usual machine-dependent additive constants, and claims about algorithmic
randomness require an infinite bit specification and the relevant complexity bounds~\cite{chaitin1974}.  A finite
trace, a repeated state, or a capability labeled infinite supplies none of those assumptions.  No uncomputability or
randomness conclusion follows from naming alone.

\paragraph{Worked example 5: finite trace storage (external accounting model).}
Under a fixed-width display with \(b\) symbols per state and \(h\) symbols for the update description, an
\(N\)-transition trace occupies
\[
                          h+(N+1)b
\]
symbols before receipts, indices, and formatting.  This is a conditional storage calculation, not a universal lower
bound and not \(K_M\).  A cycle certificate may compress future description to \((\mu,\lambda)\) plus one period, but
that compression is licensed only after the cycle has been demonstrated.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: relabeling is not refinement.}
The finite indexing experiment begins with a catalog \(X=\mathsf{Ledger}(\mathsf{Book})\), where every book stores an event identifier and a finite tag list.
For a supplied function \(f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\), the transform
\[
 T_f:X\longrightarrow X
\]
maps \(f\) over event identifiers while leaving each tag list and the ledger shape unchanged.
A different transform \(R_b:X\to X\) prepends a supplied new book \(b\).
The setup stores a two-book catalog, the shift \(f(n)=n+100\), and one new book, but the exported run ignores \texttt{refineBy} and decides only whether \(T_f\) preserves catalog count.
The strict increase \(\#R_b(C)=\#C+1\) is an auxiliary theorem used by the canonical top-level claim, not a branch traversed by \texttt{run}.
The commuting test is \(\#T_f(C)=\#C\); the residual is the changed identifiers, which count deliberately ignores.
The contrasting test \(\#R_b(C)=\#C+1\) prevents a relabeling from masquerading as a new distinction.
The finite catalog ceiling does not address human identification or empirical distinguishability.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: equal prefixes do not determine the next record.}
The Chaitin-labelled experiment uses \(X=\mathsf{List}(\mathsf{HaltRecord})\) and the prefix transform
\[
 P_t:X\longrightarrow X,
 \qquad P_t(L)=\operatorname{take}(t,L).
\]
Two consumed ledgers share the same two-record prefix but carry different third records.
Given any proposed predictor \(p:X\to\mathsf{Option}(\mathsf{HaltRecord})\), the code proves that \(p(P_2(L_A))\) cannot equal both unequal third records, because its inputs are definitionally equal.
The run itself only decides \(P_t(L_A)=P_t(L_B)\): it returns true at \(t=2\) and false at \(t=3\).
The trace comprises two bounded prefix traversals and one equality check, so it stops after at most \(t\) list cells; short input is handled by ordinary prefixing, not filled with phantom records.
The commuting fact is prefix stability under appending a tail before the cut, and the residual is the pair of unequal suffixes erased by \(P_2\).
No probability source, universal prefix machine, or Kolmogorov bound is present; the Chaitin name warns only against
inferring these unseen continuations.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: a local halt over a finite candidate pool.}
The halt experiment deliberately computes a much smaller predicate than universal halting.
Its domain is \(X=\mathsf{List}(\mathsf{Event})\times\mathsf{List}(\mathsf{Event})\), a history paired with a finite candidate pool, and its codomain is \(Y=\mathsf{Bool}\).
For an event \(e\), admissibility requires every history event correlated with \(e\) to have index smaller than \(e\)'s index; the transform is
\[
 H(L,P)=\operatorname{decide}\bigl(\forall e\in P,\;\neg\operatorname{admissible}(L,e)\bigr).
\]
The canonical history contains indices \(5,7\) and the pool contains \(2,4\), all in one correlation class, so the run returns true: every candidate is blocked by ordering.
An empty history admits any candidate, furnishing the failure case that keeps the no-go conditional on the consumed history.
The trace is nested finite membership and universal checks, and its stop is exhaustion of the explicit pool; adding a candidate can change the result, so pool exhaustion cannot be read as a theorem about unlisted events.
The representation test compares the Boolean with the proposition \(\operatorname{halts}(L,P)\); the residual is precisely the unexamined complement outside \(P\).
Physical time and an executing machine are absent; any Turing resonance must preserve the finite-pool fence.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: decoding detects an inflated coarse record.}
The Cantor--Gödel--Cohen experiment declares fine records carrying an index, a distinction, and an extra witness coordinate.
