\chapter{A Difference Appears}

\section{Difference is a predicate, not a particle}

Scientific computation begins by fixing a grammar before assigning meaning to its names.  Let \(M\) be a finite
alphabet of marks:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\textit{mark}&::=&m_0\mid\cdots\mid m_{q-1},\\
\textit{question}&::=&\operatorname{diff}(s,\sigma),\\
\textit{answer}&::=&\operatorname{yes}(w)\mid\operatorname{no}(r).
\end{array}
\]
The payload \(w\) certifies that two marks differ; \(r\) certifies that they do not.  The grammar does not make a mark
a material object, a question a detector, or an answer an observation.  Those names require separate provenance.

For a reference \(\sigma\in M\), the governing question is
\[
        D_\sigma(s):=(s\ne\sigma).
\]
A resolver \(\Delta\) must terminate with an evidenced judgment,
\[
 \frac{w:s\ne\sigma}{\Delta(s,\sigma)\Downarrow\operatorname{yes}(w)}
 \qquad
 \frac{r:\neg(s\ne\sigma)}{\Delta(s,\sigma)\Downarrow\operatorname{no}(r)}.
\]
A later routine may erase the evidence:
\[
 E(\operatorname{yes}(w))=1,\qquad E(\operatorname{no}(r))=0.
\]
This external Boolean encoding is lossy.  Its bit records the selected branch but not the certificate that licensed it.

Reading has a direction.  Let \(X\) be a finite collection of records and let
\(e:X\to M\) represent each record by a mark.  Construction sends records forward through \(e\).  A question
\(q:M\to\{0,1\}\), or its evidence-bearing refinement, is read contravariantly by pullback:
\[
        e^*q:=q\circ e:X\to\{0,1\}.
\]
For a second representation \(g:M\to N\),
\[
        (g\circ e)^*=e^*\circ g^*.
\]
The order reverses: first pull the question on \(N\) through \(g\), then through \(e\).  This is an external
representation schema, not a claim that a particular map \(e\) has already been constructed.  It separates the
covariant forward construction of a representation from the contravariant act of reading it.

An audit record is
\[
 \mathcal R=(X,M,e,\sigma,D,\Delta,E,(A,R),J,F,P),
\]
where \(J\) states invariants, \(F\) states failures, and \(P\) tags computation, physics, or interpretation.  For
\(q=E\circ\Delta(-,\sigma)\),
\[
 A=\{x\in X\mid(e^*q)(x)=1\},\qquad R=\{x\in X\mid(e^*q)(x)=0\}.
\]
The words accepted and rejected are conventional branch labels, not value judgments.

The finite algorithm is explicit.  Given \(L=[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]\) and an independent decision budget \(N\),
initialize two empty output lists and \(i\leftarrow0\).  While \(i<n\) and \(i<N\), compute \(m_i=e(x_i)\), resolve
\(\Delta(m_i,\sigma)\), append the record and its evidence to the corresponding slice, and update
\(i\leftarrow i+1\).  Stop with reason \textsc{complete} when \(i=n\), \textsc{budget} when \(i=N<n\), or
\textsc{resolver-domain} when the declared resolver does not apply to \((m_i,\sigma)\).  If \(k\) records are visited,
its cost is
\[
 C(L,N)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\!
 \left(C_e(x_i)+C_\Delta(e(x_i),\sigma)+C_{\rm append}(i)\right),
 \qquad k\le\min(n,N).
\]
Its certificate contains the visited prefix \([x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1}]\), both evidence-bearing slices, the stop reason,
and every declared Boolean erasure.  Coverage and exclusivity apply only to that prefix: \(|A|+|R|=k\).  A complete
run has \(k=n\); a budget or resolver-domain stop has \(k=i\) and says nothing about the unvisited suffix.  Stable
append preserves relative order \emph{within} each branch, but separating the branches loses their original
interleaving.  Classification error is zero relative to the declared question when every recorded certificate checks.
That internal guarantee is not an empirical error rate.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: printed marks (computation).}
Take \(X=M=\{\mathtt{x},\mathtt{o}\}\), \(e\) the identity, \(\sigma=\mathtt{x}\), and
\(L=[\mathtt{x},\mathtt{o},\mathtt{o},\mathtt{x}]\).  Four decisions give
\[
 A=[\mathtt{o},\mathtt{o}],\qquad R=[\mathtt{x},\mathtt{x}],
 \qquad |A|+|R|=4.
\]
With \(N\ge4\), the stopping certificate is \textsc{complete} with \(i=4=|L|\).  With \(N=2\), it instead certifies
only the prefix \([\mathtt{x},\mathtt{o}]\), one entry in each slice, and stop reason \textsc{budget}; it asserts
nothing about the two remaining marks.  Exchanging the two printed shapes everywhere preserves slice sizes while
swapping their branch names.  No metric follows: matching shapes does not define a distance between them.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: an integer comparator (computation).}
Let \(M=\{0,\ldots,9\}\), \(\sigma=7\), and process \(L=[7,8,2,7,9]\).  The result is
\[
 A=[8,2,9],\qquad R=[7,7].
\]
The separated slices lose the original interleaving between branches and discard magnitude, while stable append
preserves relative order within each branch.  Values \(8\) and \(2\) take the same branch although a separately supplied
arithmetic map gives \(|8-7|=1\) and \(|2-7|=5\).  The name difference does not supply subtraction.  To obtain it, the
representation must add \(d:M\times M\to\mathbb N\) and publish its cost and assumptions.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: a finite packet alphabet (communication model).}
Declare a discrete source on \(M=\{\mathtt A,\mathtt B,\mathtt C\}\) with
\[
 p(\mathtt A)=\tfrac12,\qquad p(\mathtt B)=p(\mathtt C)=\tfrac14.
\]
For \(p(x)>0\), Shannon self-information is
\[
 I(x)=-\log_2p(x),
\]
so the three marks carry one, two, and two bits respectively~\cite{shannon1948}.  With
\(\sigma=\mathtt A\), both \(\mathtt B\) and \(\mathtt C\) enter the same slice.  The erased decision therefore cannot
recover the source mark.  Shannon's channel language belongs here to a separately declared computational model, not
to a physical signal.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: a sampled switch (physics and interpretation).}
Suppose an experiment samples a switch at \(t_k=k\Delta t\) and records \(v_k\in\{0,1\}\).  Physics owns the switch,
sampling interval, noise, and acquisition apparatus.  Post-processing may represent a record by \(e(x_k)=v_k\) and
pull back the question \(D_0\).  If the apparatus has false-positive probability \(\varepsilon_+\), false-negative
probability \(\varepsilon_-\), and on-state prior \(\pi\), its empirical error is
\[
 P_e=(1-\pi)\varepsilon_+ + \pi\varepsilon_-.
\]
This belongs to the physical route from switch state to recorded mark, not to the internal comparator.  The routes
meet only through an explicit comparison map and observations; shared names prove no identity.

\paragraph{Worked example 5: convergence demonstrated (the instrument calibration).}
Here \emph{convergence} means agreement of two completed finite numerical routes at a declared resolution, not an
asymptotic claim.  Both routes begin from the same calibration data
\[
                   C=18,\qquad \text{target}=5,\qquad
                   d_*=\sqrt{C/\text{target}}=\sqrt{18/5}.
\]
The direct route \(1\to3\) is a one-pass forward evaluation at \(d_*\).  In the declared scaled-integer
representation it reports
\[
 d=137011290548979455469\qquad\text{at scale }10^{18}.
\]
The stepped route \(1\to2\to3\) carries the residue through intermediate quasi-Newton updates.  Its finite trace is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{count}&r_k&\text{reported inverse value}\\
\hline
0&2/1&129.6\\
1&17/9&137.7\\
2&\approx1.897309&\approx137.016\\
3&\approx1.897366&s/10^{18}
\end{array}
\]
and it stops at count three with
\[
 s=137011290753751157155\qquad\text{at scale }10^{18}.
\]
These are distinct instrument algorithms.  The stepped route
has mediant/continued-fraction, Newton/BFGS, and residue-carrying secant realizations; these are numerical constructions
whose update and stopping rules must be declared separately.  Both endpoint computations are covariant forward
constructions over the shared calibration data.  The contravariant action enters when the comparison question is
pulled back to those data; it is not a second name for either endpoint algorithm.

The finite comparator certificate is not integer equality.  It records
\[
 |d-s|=204771701686,\qquad
 \frac{|d-s|}{10^{18}}=0.000000204771701686
 \approx2.04771701686\times10^{-7}.
\]
Thus the reported decimal values agree to nine digits.  To state the reading precisely, let \(X\) be the shared
calibration-input space, \(D\) the scaled-integer report space, and
\[
 F=(F_{\rm direct},F_{\rm stepped}):X\to D\times D.
\]
At count-three resolution define the comparison question
\[
 q_3(a,b):=\left[\frac{|a-b|}{10^{18}}<8.1\right],
 \qquad F^*q_3=q_3\circ F.
\]
The pullback \(F^*q_3\) is the contravariant reading of the two covariantly constructed reports.  Write
\(a\equiv_3 b\) when this declared-resolution question answers yes.  For the shared input above,
\(d\equiv_3s\).  The house slogan \(1\to3=1\to2\to3\) abbreviates this relation; it is not integer equality and
establishes path-independence only for these two routes on this calibration input.

The two-pass calibration therefore licenses a path-independent shared numerical result at the declared resolution,
which is what earns the name \(\alpha_c\).  It does not identify the methods, meanings, traces, costs, or
physics/computation provenance of the routes.  Nor does it promote either scaled integer to the owned output.  The
honest result remains the ordered walls written in the repository's house notation
\([129.6,137.7]\), not a point.  The word \emph{open} is the repository's metaphor for an unresolved jar, not
topological interval notation; the displayed bracket records the two owned ordered walls.  The values near
\(137.011290\) are finer reports obtained past the floor.

The roles of the two passes are now exact.  Pass one directly certifies \(C=18\) and
\(\text{target}=5\), then infers the crossing \(\sqrt{C/\text{target}}\); the crossing is not claimed as an
independent measurement.  Pass two traverses the stepped trace and compares its endpoint with the direct endpoint.
Any comparison with a physical quantity requires a separate provenance chain and empirical observations.

\paragraph{Conditional Richardson audit.}
The direct and stepped reports are \emph{not} a valid Richardson pair: they come from different methods, and no common
discretization family \(A(h)\), refinement ratio \(r\), or error order \(p\) has been established.  Only under the
counterfactual assumptions \(D=A(h)\), \(S=A(h/2)\), and leading error proportional to \(h^p\) would the extrapolate be
\[
                 A_R=S+\frac{S-D}{2^p-1}.
\]
Applying that formula conditionally gives the sensitivity ledger
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p&A_R&\text{correction added to }S\\
\hline
1&137.011290958522858841&2.04771701686\times10^{-7}\\
2&137.011290822008391050333\ldots&6.8257233895333\times10^{-8}\\
4&137.011290767402603934066\ldots&1.3651446779067\times10^{-8}
\end{array}
\]
Treating the stepped report as the finer value is itself arbitrary and counterfactual here.  Reversing the orientation
reverses the correction, and no common refinement family selects either orientation.
These are sensitivity values, not measurements, uncertainty bounds, or owned results.  Their variation demonstrates
dependence on an unestablished order assumption.  Licensing Richardson extrapolation would require one declared family
\(A(h)\), a declared ratio \(r\), at least three refinement levels, an observed-order estimate
\[
 p_{\rm obs}=
 \frac{\log\!\left(|A_h-A_{h/r}|/|A_{h/r}-A_{h/r^2}|\right)}{\log r},
\]
and stability under further refinement.  Until those conditions are met, the ordered-wall jar
\([129.6,137.7]\) remains the owned result.

Two finite consequences and losses are immediate.  If \(|M|>2\), the erased map
\(E\circ\Delta(-,\sigma):M\to\{0,1\}\) cannot be injective, so a binary
slice cannot recover every mark.  Also, \(\sigma\) does not determine the question: equality, order, membership, and
threshold rules can share a reference while producing different slices.  A report must publish \((\sigma,D)\), the
representation \(e\), update rule, stopping rule, cost, and certificate.

Symmetry supplies only
\[
 D_\sigma(s)\iff D_s(\sigma).
\]
It preserves a relation under exchange of arguments; it proves no equality of procedures, traces, costs, or physical
protocols.  A two-pass calibration may test those stronger claims, but it must record both passes and state its
comparison map.

The result is finite and reusable.  Marks support explicit questions; terminating decisions carry evidence; evidence
may be erased to bits; and pulled-back questions can slice finite records.  Physics enters only when an experiment
supplies an apparatus, units, uncertainty, and observations.  A resonant name belongs to one provenance chain at a
time.  If computation and physics later report the same number or process, each route must be shown separately before
comparison.

\section{The symbol and the receiver}

A receiver is not a second object placed beside a symbol.  It is a declared action that projects a finite alphabet into
the distinctions a reading can retain.  Fix a mark domain \(M\), a reference \(\sigma\in M\), and an answer alphabet
\(B=\{0,1\}\).  The read grammar is
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\textit{projection}&::=&\pi_\sigma:M\to B,\\
\textit{accepted}&::=&\operatorname{yes}(m,w),\\
\textit{rejected}&::=&\operatorname{no}(m,r),
\end{array}
\]
where \(w\) certifies \(m\ne\sigma\) and \(r\) certifies its negation.  The projection acts by
\[
 \pi_\sigma(m)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&m\ne\sigma,\\
 0,&m=\sigma.
 \end{cases}
\]
This is one declared receiver grammar.  Another grammar may replace its question and resolver; all slices below concern
the displayed inequality rule.  Its zero fiber is \(N_\sigma=\pi_\sigma^{-1}(\{0\})\).  The accepted-bit view
suppresses distinctions among marks in that fiber by mapping them all to zero, while the rejected evidence ledger
retains the visited occurrences.  More generally, information may be lost in either many-to-one fiber of
\(\pi_\sigma\).  This is a finite projection, not a claim about a linear kernel.

Direction remains part of the grammar.  A forward representation \(e:X\to M\) carries records into marks, and
\(\pi_\sigma:M\to B\) carries marks forward into bits.  Let \(q:B\to\mathsf{Answer}\) be a question asked of a
projected bit.  Reading pulls \(q\) back:
\[
 \pi_\sigma^*q:=q\circ\pi_\sigma,\qquad
 e^*(\pi_\sigma^*q)=q\circ\pi_\sigma\circ e.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
             (\pi_\sigma\circ e)^*q=e^*(\pi_\sigma^*q).
\]
The order of reading reverses the order of forward maps.  The receiver therefore consists of a finite forward
projection followed by contravariant reading of the projected value; the projection itself is not contravariant.

The numerical method is a budgeted stable partition.  Given
\(L=[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]\), a budget \(N\), representation \(e\), reference \(\sigma\), and resolver \(\Delta\),
initialize \(A\leftarrow[]\), \(R\leftarrow[]\), \(i\leftarrow0\).  While \(i<n\) and \(i<N\):

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
1.&m_i\leftarrow e(x_i),\\
2.&a_i\leftarrow\Delta(m_i,\sigma),\\
3.&\text{append }(x_i,m_i,a_i)\text{ to }A\text{ for yes, otherwise to }R,\\
4.&i\leftarrow i+1.
\end{array}
\]

The run stops with \textsc{complete} at \(i=n\), \textsc{budget} at \(i=N<n\), or
\textsc{resolver-domain} when the declared decision does not cover the encountered pair.  Its certificate contains the
visited prefix, accepted and rejected evidence, erased projection bits, and stop reason.  On the visited prefix,
\[
             |A|+|R|=i,\qquad A\cap R=\varnothing.
\]
Stable append preserves relative order within each slice but loses the interleaving between slices.  A partial
certificate says nothing about the unvisited suffix.

The audit schema is
\[
 \mathcal Q=(X,M,e,\sigma,\pi_\sigma,\Delta,N,A,R,J,F,P).
\]
Here \(J\) records invariants, \(F\) records failure and information loss, and \(P\) records provenance.  The schema is
external scientific bookkeeping.  The computational route owns finite marks, decisions, projection, traversal, and
slices.  Physics owns apparatus variables, units, noise, and observations.  Interpretation owns any proposed bridge.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: repeated finite marks (computation).}
Let \(M=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b,\mathtt c\}\), \(\sigma=\mathtt a\), and
\[
 L=[\mathtt a,\mathtt c,\mathtt b,\mathtt a,\mathtt c].
\]
With \(N\ge5\), the complete run returns
\[
 A=[\mathtt c,\mathtt b,\mathtt c],\qquad
 R=[\mathtt a,\mathtt a].
\]
The projected trace is \([0,1,1,0,1]\).  It cannot distinguish \(\mathtt b\) from \(\mathtt c\), although the
evidence-bearing accepted slice can retain their marks.  If only the projected trace is stored, the original input order
has several compatible preimages.  In this example the loss occurs in the accepted fiber
\(\{\mathtt b,\mathtt c\}\), not in the zero fiber \(\{\mathtt a\}\); either fiber can lose distinctions when it has
more than one member.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: budgeted packet reading (computation).}
Take packet marks \(M=\{\mathtt{idle},\mathtt{data},\mathtt{check}\}\), reference
\(\sigma=\mathtt{idle}\), six records, and \(N=4\).  Suppose their first four marks are
\[
 [\mathtt{idle},\mathtt{data},\mathtt{check},\mathtt{data}].
\]
The run stops for budget with one rejected and three accepted records.  Its coverage equation is \(1+3=4\), not six.
Neither remaining packet may be classified from this certificate.  If each decision costs \(c_i\), the certified work
is \(\sum_{i=0}^{3}c_i\); a claim about six decisions would exceed the ledger.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: Shannon loss under projection (communication model).}
Let a discrete source satisfy
\[
 p(\mathtt a)=\tfrac12,\qquad p(\mathtt b)=p(\mathtt c)=\tfrac14.
\]
For \(p(m)>0\), self-information is \(I(m)=-\log_2p(m)\)~\cite{shannon1948}.  The source entropy is
\[
 H(M)=-\sum_m p(m)\log_2p(m)=\tfrac32\ \text{bits}.
\]
Projection against \(\sigma=\mathtt a\) yields an equiprobable bit, hence \(H(B)=1\) bit.  The lost
\(\tfrac12\) bit is the average distinction between \(\mathtt b\) and \(\mathtt c\) erased by the projection.  This
calculation requires the displayed discrete distribution; the receiver grammar alone supplies no probabilities.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: threshold readout (physics, then interpretation).}
Suppose a finite experiment records integer converter readings \(z_k\in\{0,\ldots,1023\}\).  A declared threshold
\(\tau\) gives
\[
              \pi_\tau(z)=\mathbf 1\{z>\tau\}.
\]
Physics owns the sensor, converter, units relating the converter reading to the measured quantity, calibration of \(\tau\), and observed
false-positive and false-negative frequencies.  Computation owns the finite comparison and resulting slices.  Reading
the projection as a detector action is licensed only by the experimental protocol.  Equal output bits do not identify
two sensor states, and a perfect internal comparison does not imply zero empirical error.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: two readers over one compact-disc mark ledger.}
For the finite-harness experiments through the Dirichlet--Bancroft case in Chapter~5, displayed maps and proved
identities are theorem-level facts, while shown \texttt{run} values are compiler evaluations.  Physical nouns remain
interpretive unless an observation is explicit; the native-apparatus exception is identified where it occurs.
The finite compact-disc experiment supplies a receiver whose operation can be written without borrowing the optics of a laboratory player.
Its fine domain is the two-element type \(X=\{\mathsf{pit},\mathsf{land}\}\), its coarse codomain is the one-element transition type \(Y=\{\mathsf{transition}\}\), and its declared transform is
\[
 T_{\rm CD}:X\longrightarrow Y,
 \qquad T_{\rm CD}(x)=\mathsf{transition}.
\]
Applied pointwise by \texttt{pushLedger}, this transform preserves ledger length even though it erases the pit--land distinction.
The setup also contains a coarse reader threshold \(2\), a fine reader threshold \(1\), a three-mark coarse ledger, and a five-mark fine ledger.
An auxiliary theorem family defines \(\operatorname{canRecord}(R,d)\) from a reader and a natural-number separation by \(R.\mathit{threshold}\le d\), proves rejection below threshold, and proves monotonic visibility when a fine threshold does not exceed a coarse one.
Those theorems do not run a separation probe in the exported computation.
The exported run ignores both reader fields and returns only the counts of the two supplied ledgers and their concatenation, obtaining \(3\), \(5\), and \(8\).
Its actual trace is therefore the two finite input lists, two length computations, one append, and the merged length.
The exported claim separately checks threshold ordering and append bookkeeping, while pointwise decoding and its count theorem remain auxiliary built facts rather than consumed steps of \texttt{run}.
The commuting test is count preservation,
\[
 \#\bigl(T_{\rm CD*}L\bigr)=\#L,
\]
while the residual test is that \(T_{\rm CD}(\mathsf{pit})=T_{\rm CD}(\mathsf{land})\) despite \(\mathsf{pit}\ne\mathsf{land}\).
The name supplies no optical behavior beyond this finite decode-and-count model.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: a four-symbol Fessenden--Shannon frame.}
A second receiver makes the alphabet change visible rather than speaking of ``information'' in the abstract.
Let \(X=\mathsf{Sample}=\mathsf{Symbol}\times\mathbb N\), where the second coordinate is a slot, and let \(Y=\mathsf{Symbol}=\{\mathsf{silence},\mathsf{low},\mathsf{mid},\mathsf{high}\}\).
The exact transform is the projection
\[
 T_{\rm FS}:X\longrightarrow Y,
 \qquad T_{\rm FS}(b,k)=b.
\]
The canonical setup stores a five-sample frame, including two \(\mathsf{mid}\) symbols at different slots, but the exported run ignores that ledger.
The frame-decoding and five-event count-preservation result are auxiliary theorems.
The run consumes only one probe and one expected symbol and decides the equality \(T_{\rm FS}(\mathit{probe})=\mathit{expected}\).
For the canonical probe \((\mathsf{mid},1)\), expectation \(\mathsf{mid}\) succeeds and expectation \(\mathsf{high}\) fails.
There is no iterative search: the run trace is one projection and one decidable equality.
A different but well-formed expected symbol produces a mismatch and false result; it is not malformed input.
The commuting square is the pointwise equality between decoding the sample ledger and mapping \(T_{\rm FS}\) across its list; its residue is the erased slot coordinate, witnessed by distinct samples with the same decoded band.
Carrier waves, noise, capacity, and an empirical law are absent; the name licenses symbol recovery only, not
Shannon's theorems.  The projection's many-to-one fibers state exactly why erased slots cannot be recovered.

\section{From a decision to a record}

A durable record begins with a receipt, not with a familiar arithmetic interpretation.  The neutral grammar is
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\textit{numeral-record}&::=&\operatorname{anchor}(\textit{receipt})
 \mid\operatorname{numeral\mbox{-}step}(\textit{receipt},\textit{numeral-record}),\\
\textit{tally}&::=&\operatorname{halt}(\textit{receipt})
 \mid\operatorname{tally\mbox{-}step}(\textit{receipt},\textit{numeral-record},\textit{tally}),\\
\textit{piece}&::=&\operatorname{origin}(\textit{receipt})
 \mid\operatorname{piece\mbox{-}step}(\textit{receipt},\textit{tally},\textit{piece}),\\
\textit{record}&::=&\operatorname{end}(\textit{receipt})
 \mid\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\textit{receipt},\textit{piece},\textit{record}).
\end{array}
\]
The numeral record is the earlier finite recursive payload, given here only by its anchor and extension forms.  Its
arithmetic meaning is reserved rather than supplied by the name.  The grammar posits finite forms and nothing else.  A
receipt accompanies every form, and each recursive argument stores the remainder.  No magnitude, chronology, or
physical event is supplied by these productions.

Forward construction prepends one step:
\[
\begin{aligned}
T'&=\operatorname{tally\mbox{-}step}(\rho,\nu,T),\\
P'&=\operatorname{piece\mbox{-}step}(\rho,T,P),\\
S'&=\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho,P,S).
\end{aligned}
\]
Repeated prepending places the newest addition at the head.  Tail traversal proceeds by matching the head, recording
its receipt and payload, and continuing with the recursive remainder.  Given budget \(N\), initialize the visit count
\(j\leftarrow1\).  Continue while a step remains and \(j\le N\); stop with \textsc{end}, \textsc{budget}, or
\textsc{resolver-domain}.  The certificate contains the visited prefix in traversal orientation, the final unvisited
tail when present, all encountered receipts, and the stop reason.

Traversal indices are analysis-ledger entries, not members of the grammar.  The ledger assigns \(j=1,2,\ldots\) as a
run visits heads.  A later run may assign the same indices only when it declares the same starting form and traversal
orientation.  Prepend construction and tail traversal point in opposite list orientations: construction adds newest
first; complete traversal reads newest to oldest.  Arrival orientation is recovered only by an explicit reversal.

Three finite slices are available after traversal.  A shape slice separates terminal forms from step forms.  An index
slice selects ledger positions satisfying a declared question, such as \(j\) odd.  Given a declared comparison receipt
\(\rho_*\), a receipt slice selects one value of the derived relation
\(\operatorname{parity}(\rho,\rho_*)\).  Each slice must retain evidence, visited occurrences, and orientation.  Stable append preserves relative
traversal order within each output but loses interleaving between outputs.  For \(v\) visited occurrences,
\[
                         |A|+|R|=v,\qquad A\cap R=\varnothing.
\]
A budget stop certifies nothing about the unvisited tail.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: prepend and budgeted traversal (computation).}
Let \(E=\operatorname{end}(\rho_e)\), and prepend three record steps carrying pieces \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) in that arrival
order:
\[
 S_1=\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho_1,P_1,E),\quad
 S_2=\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho_2,P_2,S_1),\quad
 S_3=\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho_3,P_3,S_2).
\]
Complete tail traversal reports \(P_3,P_2,P_1\), followed by the terminal receipt.  With \(N=2\), it certifies only
ledger entries \((1,\rho_3,P_3)\) and \((2,\rho_2,P_2)\), then returns the remaining tail \(S_1\) with
\textsc{budget}.  It may not report anything about \(P_1\) without another visit.

A head projection deliberately ignores recursive tails.  For step forms define
\[
 \pi_{\rm head}\bigl(\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho,P,S)\bigr)=(\rho,P).
\]
For a terminal form it returns its terminal receipt and a terminal tag.  This is a many-to-one forward map.  Its fibers
contain every form with the same head summary, regardless of tail.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: same head, different tail (computation).}
Let
\[
 S_A=\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho,P,S_1),\qquad
 S_B=\operatorname{record\mbox{-}step}(\rho,P,S_2),
 \qquad S_1\ne S_2.
\]
Then
\[
                    \pi_{\rm head}(S_A)=\pi_{\rm head}(S_B)=(\rho,P).
\]
The head read cannot reconstruct which tail was present.  The pair lies in one fiber of
\(\pi_{\rm head}\), and the missing distinction is exactly the tail information suppressed by projection.  Retaining
the original forms in an evidence ledger preserves them; retaining only the projected head does not.

The relation between two receipts also directs a finite comparison.  Declare a current receipt \(\rho\), a comparison
receipt \(\rho_*\), a derived relation
\(\operatorname{parity}(\rho,\rho_*)\in\{\mathsf{agree},\mathsf{disagree}\}\), current payload \(a\), comparison
payload \(b\), and a binary question \(C\).  The action is
\[
 \operatorname{compare}_{\rho,\rho_*}(a,b)=
 \begin{cases}
 C(a,b),&\operatorname{parity}(\rho,\rho_*)=\mathsf{agree},\\
 C(b,a),&\operatorname{parity}(\rho,\rho_*)=\mathsf{disagree}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Derived agreement sends the current payload into the forward comparison; derived disagreement reverses the arguments.  This rule
establishes no order law.  If \(C\) is asymmetric, reversal may change the answer; if \(C\) is symmetric, it may not.
The relation between receipts chooses argument orientation but does not prove anything about the comparison relation.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: receipt-directed comparison (computation).}
Let two numeral-record payloads be denoted \(\nu_L,\nu_R\), and declare
\(C(a,b)\) to answer yes exactly for the ordered pair \((\nu_L,\nu_R)\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{compare}_{\rho_1,\rho_*}(\nu_L,\nu_R)=\mathsf{yes}
 \quad\text{when }\operatorname{parity}(\rho_1,\rho_*)=\mathsf{agree},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{compare}_{\rho_2,\rho_*}(\nu_L,\nu_R)=\mathsf{no}
 \quad\text{when }\operatorname{parity}(\rho_2,\rho_*)=\mathsf{disagree}.
\]
The outputs differ solely because the second receipt relation reverses the arguments.  Neither output assigns an
arithmetic or physical meaning to \(\nu_L,\nu_R\).  Those resonances remain reserved.

Contravariance belongs to questions pulled through forward projections.  If
\(q:H\to\mathsf{Answer}\) asks about a head summary and
\(\pi_{\rm head}:S\to H\), then
\[
                       \pi_{\rm head}^*q=q\circ\pi_{\rm head}.
\]
For an index analysis, let \(\pi_{\rm idx}\) project each visited ledger entry to its assigned index.  An index question
\(q_I\) is read as \(\pi_{\rm idx}^*q_I\).  The projections move summaries forward; reading pulls questions backward.
The receipt-directed argument reversal above is a separate finite action and is not identified with this pullback law.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: shape, index, and receipt slices (computation).}
Relative to declared comparison receipt \(\rho_*\), suppose a complete traversal yields four ledger rows whose middle
column is the derived parity
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{index}&\text{derived receipt relation}&\text{payload}\\
\hline
1&\operatorname{parity}(\rho_4,\rho_*)=\mathsf{agree}&P_4\\
2&\operatorname{parity}(\rho_3,\rho_*)=\mathsf{disagree}&P_3\\
3&\operatorname{parity}(\rho_2,\rho_*)=\mathsf{agree}&P_2\\
4&\operatorname{parity}(\rho_1,\rho_*)=\mathsf{agree}&P_1
\end{array}
\]
and then a terminal row.  The odd-index slice selects rows one and three.  The derived-agree receipt slice selects rows one,
three, and four.  The shape slice separates the four step rows from the terminal row.  These are three questions over
one traversal, not three reconstructions of the original arrival orientation.

A computation-side resonance with Cohen may now be stated carefully.  From a completed traversal \(S\), define the
finite partial assignment
\[
                       p_S(k)=\text{payload at traversal index }k
\]
for visited indices \(k\).  Extending the budget can extend the assignment when earlier rows remain unchanged.  This
resembles Cohen's finite-condition discipline: compatible finite commitments may be extended
\cite{cohen1963}.  The resemblance concerns finite partial assignments only.  Because traversal reads prepend-built
forms newest first, \(p_S(k)\) is indexed in traversal orientation, not arrival orientation.  No generic extension,
forcing relation, independence result, or claim about every possible continuation follows.

\paragraph{Descent experiment: serializing a finite grid into a record.}
The Turing experiment isolates the record-making operation as a lossless transform on a declared finite domain.
For a grid \(G=(r,c)\), let \(X_G=\{0,\ldots,r-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,c-1\}\) and let \(Y=\mathsf{Step}\), where a step stores exactly one cell.
The transform
\[
 T_G:X_G\longrightarrow Y,
 \qquad T_G(i,j)=\mathsf{step}(i,j),
\]
is traversed in row-major order and placed in a ledger; reconstruction applies the stored-cell projection \(U:Y\to X_G\).
The code proves \(U\circ T_G=\operatorname{id}_{X_G}\) pointwise and proves that serialization followed by reconstruction yields the enumerated cell list.
For the consumed setup \(r=2,c=3\), the run returns ledger count \(6=r c\), and the printed trace lists all six cells in row-major order.
The preconditions are only natural row and column counts and the concrete enumeration generated by the two finite ranges; no bijection with an unbounded tape is assumed.
Traversal stops after \(r c\) generated cells, including immediately for a zero dimension, and a later interpretation cannot silently treat a duplicated, omitted, or reordered external sample as if the theorem covered it.
The commuting test is
\[
 U_*\bigl(T_{G*}(\operatorname{cells}G)\bigr)=\operatorname{cells}G,
\]
and the residual test is empty: \(U\) returns every stored cell because \(\mathsf{Step}\) carries no compressed payload beyond that cell.
Scanning hardware, tape motion, and an unbounded tape are absent; \texttt{physicalClaimLabelOnly} confines the
Turing-style name to this lossless finite sequentialization.

The action ledger closes the section.  The receipt grammar and prepend rules are \emph{posited}.  A finite construction
trace, visited-prefix certificate, projection equality, or checked slice is \emph{demonstrated}.  Head and index
questions are \emph{read} contravariantly through declared projections.  Arithmetic meanings for numeral records and
physical meanings for receipt relations, sides, or arrival events remain \emph{reserved}.  A head projection does not reconstruct a tail; a finite
slice does not certify an unread remainder; argument reversal does not select which side is which; and a compatible
partial assignment does not produce a generic extension.
