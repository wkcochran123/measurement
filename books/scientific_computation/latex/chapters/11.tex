\chapter{The Meter}

\section{What a heartbeat counts}

A heartbeat is an operational counter exposed by a particular language toolchain.  In the configuration considered
here it behaves as an allocation or effort counter consumed by bounded elaboration, reduction, search, and related
internal work.  Its exact increment policy is toolchain-defined and may change with compiler release, libraries,
optimization, cache state, command form, or configuration.  Therefore a reading is not merely \(h\), but
\[
H=(h,\text{counter semantics},\text{release},\text{dependencies},
\text{configuration},\text{input},\text{run conditions}).
\]

Several currencies must remain distinct:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{logical step}&\text{one transition in a declared abstract procedure},\\
\text{reduction/search step}&\text{one event in a declared operational trace},\\
\text{allocation counter}&\text{a toolchain-defined charged event},\\
\text{wall time}&\text{elapsed clock duration},\\
\text{CPU time}&\text{processor time charged by the operating environment},\\
\text{energy}&\text{joules measured by calibrated physical instrumentation}.
\end{array}
\]
No fixed conversion among these follows from their names.  A counter can change while wall time falls, or remain
stable while wall time varies.  Energy requires a physical meter and calibration.

The measurement protocol uses a controlled workload family \(W_j\).  For each workload it records a requested counter
budget \(B_j\), runs the operation, and obtains
\[
                         (o_j,h_j,\sigma_j),
\]
where \(o_j\) is the returned report, \(h_j\) the spent counter, and \(\sigma_j\) the stop.  Requested, spent, and
remaining budget are retained separately.  A budget exhaustion result reports checked scope and unresolved frontier;
it is not a completed cost.

\paragraph{Calibration experiment 1: baseline subtraction (computation).}
Measure an empty or minimal harness \(W_0\) and a target \(W_1\) under the same governed conditions.  Define
\[
                         \Delta h=h_1-h_0.
\]
This subtraction removes only charges common and additive under the protocol.  It does not prove that every overhead
is shared, that counter attribution is linear, or that \(\Delta h\) belongs exclusively to the target.  Interleaved
baseline and target runs can expose drift.

\paragraph{Calibration experiment 2: workload scaling (computation).}
Choose workloads with declared structural sizes \(n_1<\cdots<n_k\), measure counter vectors, and compare candidate
models such as \(a+bn\) or \(a+bn\log n\).  Numerical fitting iterates parameter estimates and slices residuals by
workload, run order, and configuration.  A good fit within the calibrated range is descriptive; it is not a universal
complexity law or a conversion from logical steps to counter units.

\paragraph{Vacuum draw: the finite Amdahl transform (computation).}
The Amdahl experiment makes one limiting transform explicit before any timing interpretation is attempted.  Its input
domain is the finite workload type
\[
                         W=\mathbb N\times\mathbb N,\qquad w=(s,p),
\]
where \(s\) is the count of sequential ledger segments and \(p\) the count declared perfectly parallel.  The
transform
\[
 T_A:W\longrightarrow\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\times\operatorname{Ledger}(\mathbf 1),\qquad
 T_A(s,p)=(s+p, s, [\star]^s)
\]
returns the serial cost, the ideal infinite-worker cost, and a ledger containing exactly the irreducible segments.
This is the mathematics the experiment performs: \texttt{total} adds the two inputs, \texttt{idealParallelCost} projects to \(s\),
and \texttt{sequentialLedger} replicates one event \(s\) times.  No scheduler, processor, clock, or communication channel is
hidden in that map.

The ledger theorem says only that its count is \(s\).  The sequential/parallel partition is an input, not a fact
discovered from a compiler trace: equal \((s,p)\) can hide different dependency graphs, while changing the partition
at fixed \(s+p\) changes the ideal projection.

For the canonical input \((1,3)\), the trace reads serial cost \(4\), ideal cost \(1\), and a one-event sequential
ledger.  The exported run stops after the projection and returns \(1\).  A separate decision checks whether ideal and
serial costs are equal and returns false, because the precondition for \texttt{no\_speedup\_without\_parallel}, namely
\(p=0\), is not satisfied.  The positive-floor theorem has its own load-bearing precondition \(0<s\); if \(s=0\), the
ideal cost is zero and the theorem cannot be invoked, although the total natural-number projection still returns.

There is an exact comparison with the familiar fractional formula, but only after its number system and embedding are
typed.  Let \(\iota:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q\) be the canonical embedding.  For \(s+p>0\), set
\(\phi_{\mathbb Q}(s,p)=\iota(p)/\iota(s+p)\).  If also \(s>0\), then in \(\mathbb Q\)
\[
 \frac{\iota(\operatorname{total}(w))}
      {\iota(\operatorname{idealParallelCost}(w))}
 =\frac{\iota(s+p)}{\iota(s)}
 =\frac{1}{1-\phi_{\mathbb Q}(w)}.
\]
Thus the natural outputs satisfy the rational Amdahl identity under the declared embedding.  This is an external
algebraic comparison, not a theorem executed by \texttt{run}; the source proves only the finite floor and ledger count,
not wall-clock speedup.

Warm and cold conditions are separate strata.  A cold run may include parsing, loading, cache construction, and
initial allocation that a warm run reuses.  A warm run may inherit mutable state from earlier workloads.  The
protocol declares process lifetime, cache policy, workload order, randomization, number of repetitions, and whether
each sample is cold, warm, or mixed.  Discarding a first run without preregistration is a data-dependent slice.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: same input, different configuration (computation).}
Two runs receive identical source text but use different releases or dependency sets.  Equal outputs do not make
their heartbeat counts directly comparable.  A process-comparison row fails or remains unresolved until counter
semantics and configuration are matched or a calibrated conversion is demonstrated.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: stable counter, variable wall time (computation).}
Repeated runs may return the same allocation count while elapsed time varies with scheduling, processor frequency,
contention, or thermal state.  This is consistent: the counter and clock measure different currencies.  Neither one
is a surrogate for energy without separate calibration.

Shannon information supplies a careful description framework.  If a declared random variable \(X\) over workload
descriptions has law \(p(x)\), then
\[
                         H(X)=-\sum_xp(x)\log_2p(x).
\]
For a prefix code with lengths \(\ell(x)\), expected description length is
\(\mathbb E[\ell(X)]\).  These are bits under a declared distribution and code.  Heartbeat counts may be statistically
modeled against description lengths, but a counter is not Shannon information, entropy, probability, or code length.
A correlation requires data and uncertainty; it does not identify the quantities.

The numerical audit iterates over configurations, workloads, repetitions, and cold/warm strata.  It slices readings
by outcome, stop, configuration, run order, and resource class; retains rejected and failed runs with reasons; computes
means, medians, dispersion, paired differences, covariance, and model residuals only where the sampling design
licenses them.  Deterministic inputs can still meet nondeterministic scheduling, randomized search, address layout,
parallel execution, or other environmental state.

Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{counter-budget}, \textsc{search-budget}, \textsc{allocation-failure},
\textsc{arithmetic-overflow}, \textsc{toolchain-mismatch}, \textsc{dependency-mismatch},
\textsc{configuration-mismatch}, \textsc{cache-state-unknown}, \textsc{nondeterministic-input-unknown},
\textsc{output-failure}, and \textsc{provenance-incomplete}.  A failed output and an exhausted resource are different
statuses; neither is silently replaced by a numeric cost.

The certificate records counter semantics, release and compiler identity, dependency lock and versions, snapshot
hash, command and build configuration, workload representation, structural size, requested/spent/remaining budgets,
output, stop, cold/warm state, process lifetime, cache policy, run order, random seeds and schedules, counter readings,
wall and CPU timing when collected, physical-meter provenance for energy if collected, rejected samples, checked
scope, unresolved frontier, and validation slices.

Comparison follows three value relations.  Exact equality compares integer counter readings only after matched
counter semantics.  Resolution equality compares labels under a declared binning or formatter.  Uncertainty
compatibility compares declared sampling or calibration regions.  Each independently passes, fails, or remains
unresolved.  Same-process additionally requires matching workload, transitions, counter policy, releases,
dependencies, configuration, cache and warmup state, deterministic inputs, declared nondeterministic inputs, and
provenance.

Governance fixes workload family, counter semantics, releases, dependencies, configuration, budget, cold/warm
protocol, repetition and ordering, baseline rule, statistical summaries, model family, binning, uncertainty, stop
priority, and certificate grammar.  A changed field creates a new measurement series.

The action ledger is exact.  Counter interpretation, workloads, baselines, configurations, sampling, models, Shannon
law and code, comparisons, budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Integer subtraction, conditional
baseline implications, fitted-model arithmetic, statistical summaries, entropy and expected-length calculations, and
certificate implications are \emph{demonstrated}.  Outputs, counters, timings, configurations, cache strata, rejected

\section{Warmup, read, subtract}

A paired cost experiment has four phases: register the workloads, perform declared setup, execute warmup, and read a
baseline--target pair.  Registration fixes representations, counter semantics, budgets, and evaluation order before
any reading.  Setup may load dependencies, initialize caches, or allocate shared tables.  Warmup is a measured and
reported phase, not evidence discarded merely because its cost is inconvenient.

For pair \(i\), let \(b_i\) be the baseline count and \(t_i\) the target count under matched conditions.  The signed
residual is
\[
                         \delta_i=t_i-b_i\in\mathbb Z.
\]
A negative residual is valid arithmetic and may indicate noise, ordering effects, cache reuse, or a baseline that is
more expensive under that condition.  Clamping \(\delta_i\) to zero would change the data.  Integer subtraction is
exact; attribution of the difference is conditional.

For \(N\geq2\) completed valid pairs, the paired estimator is
\[
                         \bar\delta=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_i
                         =\bar t-\bar b.
\]
Under a declared independent or exchangeable-pair model, its sampling variance is estimated from paired differences,
\[
 s_\delta^2=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N(\delta_i-\bar\delta)^2,\qquad
 \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\bar\delta)=\frac{s_\delta^2}{N}.
\]
Within one pair,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(T-B)=\operatorname{Var}(T)+\operatorname{Var}(B)
-2\operatorname{Cov}(T,B).
\]
This identity handles within-pair dependence only.  It does not account for correlations across pairs induced by
blocks, drift, cache history, or run order.  With residual covariance matrix \(\Sigma_\delta\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\bar\delta)
 =\frac{1}{N^2}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_\delta\mathbf1.
\]
When full covariance is not estimable, a preregistered clustered or block method must match the acquisition design.

Interleaving controls drift.  A balanced sequence such as \(B,T,T,B\) can be randomized within blocks, with run
order retained.  Repetitions are sliced by cold/warm state, block, outcome, configuration, and stop.  A paired row is
valid only when both members complete under the pairing policy; failed or censored partners remain in the audit and
are not silently replaced.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: signed paired residuals (computation).}
Suppose three matched pairs give
\[
                         (b_i,t_i)=(573,552),(570,575),(580,579).
\]
Then \(\delta=(-21,5,-1)\).  These values support exact signed arithmetic and a paired summary.  They do not prove
that the first target has negative intrinsic cost; the attribution model must explain shared and order-dependent
charges.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: subtracting unmatched runs (computation).}
A cold baseline from one configuration and a warm target from another can be subtracted as integers, but the result
is not a calibrated paired residual.  The configuration and warm-state validation rows fail, so interpretation of the
difference remains rejected or unresolved.

Shared-overhead cancellation assumes an additive model,
\[
                         B_i=O_i+\epsilon_{Bi},\qquad
                         T_i=O_i+C_i+\epsilon_{Ti}.
\]
Then \(\delta_i=C_i+\epsilon_{Ti}-\epsilon_{Bi}\).  If setup interacts with the target, if caches change \(C_i\), or
if \(O_i\) differs within a pair, subtraction does not isolate \(C_i\).  Additivity and shared overhead must be tested
with controls, order reversals, and residual diagnostics.

Two numerical routes are kept separate.  The direct route reads target and baseline in one paired block and
subtracts.  The stepped route records setup, warmup, baseline, intermediate workload, and target, then composes
declared differences.  For example,
\[
                         (t-m)+(m-b)=t-b
\]
is an exact arithmetic identity when all three readings belong to one compatible currency and pairing context.
It does not make the direct and stepped acquisition processes identical.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: direct and stepped comparison (computation).}
Let \(\delta_d=t-b\) and \(\delta_s=(t-m)+(m-b)\).  Exact equality follows algebraically if the same \(b,m,t\) are
reused.  If the routes acquire separate readings, equality becomes an empirical comparison with covariance and
provenance.  Near agreement under a formatter is only resolution equality, not same-process.

Build-to-build reproducibility is scoped to matched counter semantics, compiler and dependency releases, snapshot,
configuration, workload representations, budgets, setup, warmup, cache policy, run order, deterministic inputs, and
declared nondeterministic conditions.  Two builds may return equal tuples under this governed relation.  Equality is
read; matching configuration is posited and validated from provenance.  A mismatch does not identify its cause
without further slicing.

The comparison reports independent rows:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{exact equality}&\delta_1=\delta_2,\\
\text{resolution equality}&q_\theta(\delta_1)=q_\theta(\delta_2),\\
\text{uncertainty compatibility}&\mathcal U_1\cap\mathcal U_2\ne\varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
Each passes, fails, or remains unresolved.  Exact equality of residuals does not imply same-process; that relation
also requires compatible inputs, transitions, traces, resource currencies, setup, warmup, configurations, and
provenance.

The numerical algorithm iterates blocks and pairs, validates membership, computes signed differences, updates exact
sums and covariance accumulators, and slices results by all governed strata.  It retains raw pair order and can replay
summaries from the raw table.  Overflow and bit-growth checks precede accumulator updates.

Budgets distinguish setup, warmup, baseline and target readings, repetitions, block count, counter allowance,
arithmetic growth, covariance estimation, and replay.  Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{registration-failure},
\textsc{setup-failure}, \textsc{warmup-budget}, \textsc{counter-budget}, \textsc{unpaired-run},
\textsc{output-failure}, \textsc{configuration-mismatch}, \textsc{cache-state-unknown},
\textsc{overflow}, \textsc{insufficient-pairs}, and \textsc{provenance-incomplete}.  Resource failure is not a
numeric residual.

A certificate records registered workloads, counter semantics, releases, dependency lock, snapshot hash,
configuration, setup and warmup traces, cache/process policy, raw baseline/intermediate/target readings, pair and
block identifiers, order and randomization, signed residuals, exact sums, covariance, rejected and failed runs,
direct and stepped routes, budgets, stops, checked scope, unresolved frontier, protocol, run identity, platform,
deterministic inputs, declared nondeterministic inputs, completed-valid-pair count, and dependence-model validation.

Governance fixes workload representations, pairing, registration, setup, warmup, interleaving and randomization,
baseline, target and intermediate roles, subtraction orientation, additive model, summaries, uncertainty, direct and
stepped comparisons, budgets, stop priority, and certificate grammar.  A changed field begins a new measurement
series.

The action ledger is exact.  Pairing, workloads, roles, setup, warmup, additive model, routes, sampling, comparisons,
budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Signed subtraction, telescoping identities, paired summaries,
covariance arithmetic, conditional cancellation, sufficient-pair and dependence-model validation, and certificate
implications are \emph{demonstrated}.  Raw counts,
pair order, residuals, covariance estimates, configuration rows, route comparisons, stops, and provenance are
\emph{read}.  A fixed structural-rank conversion, isolated target cost without tested additivity, same-process from

\section{Self-application without mystification}

Self-application begins by separating five objects:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
P&\text{an object program or workload},\\
\ulcorner P\urcorner&\text{a finite representation of that program},\\
E&\text{an evaluator under a declared toolchain and configuration},\\
M&\text{a meter that observes declared resource counters during evaluation},\\
\mathcal R&\text{the resulting value, trace, counters, stop, and provenance}.
\end{array}
\]
An experiment may evaluate \(E(P,x)\), \(E(P,\ulcorner P\urcorner)\), or
\(E(E,\ulcorner E\urcorner)\) if the domains admit those inputs.  These are different workloads.  Saying that a
machine measures itself is incomplete until the object, representation, evaluator, meter boundary, and input are
specified.

The meter is not outside all influence.  Instrumentation can change allocation, search order, representation size,
caching, and evaluation path.  A calibration therefore compares an unmetered or minimally metered control with the
metered workload under matched conditions and reports the intervention.  It does not assume measurement overhead is
additive or transparent.

Bounded evaluation has state
\[
                         S_k=(e_k,\rho_k,F_k,B_k,T_k),
\]
where \(e_k\) is the current expression or task, \(\rho_k\) the environment, \(F_k\) transition fuel, \(B_k\) a
resource vector, and \(T_k\) the trace.  Each admitted transition updates state and decreases fuel.  Stops include a
value, explicit failure, domain error, fuel exhaustion, resource exhaustion, or an unresolved external action.  The
fuelled wrapper terminates; that does not decide whether the corresponding unbounded evaluation terminates.

Self-input increases several risks.  Representation can expand before evaluation, quotation and interpretation may
fail to round-trip, diagonal construction can generate a workload unlike the original, and evaluation can recurse
without reaching a value.  The report therefore retains representation bytes or symbols, parser and decoder
decisions, round-trip checks, every self-input boundary, growth, and the precise stop.  A timeout or fuel stop is not
semantic evidence of divergence.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: representation round trip (computation).}
Given \(P\), form \(\ulcorner P\urcorner\), decode it to \(P'\), and compare \(P'\) with \(P\) under a declared
structural relation.  Passing certifies the representation round trip.  It does not certify that
\(E(P,\ulcorner P\urcorner)\) halts or returns the same value as \(E(P,P)\), which may not even be well-typed.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: bounded self-input (computation).}
Run \(E(P,\ulcorner P\urcorner)\) with fuel \(N\).  If it returns value \(v\) after \(k\leq N\) transitions, replay
certifies that finite trace.  If fuel reaches zero first, the report owns the prefix and unresolved next state only.
Increasing \(N\) creates a new run and may reveal a value; the earlier stop remains valid within its scope.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: self-description is not consciousness.}
A text can contain or reproduce its own representation through an explicit quoting scheme.  That is a relation among
finite strings, decoders, and evaluations.  It supplies no operational definition, apparatus, observation, or theory
of consciousness and therefore licenses no such conclusion.

Turing's halting result is precise: no total algorithm decides for every arbitrary program--input pair whether its
unbounded evaluation halts.  A particular bounded trace, or a termination certificate for a restricted evaluator,
does not solve that universal problem.  Conversely, the universal impossibility does not prevent termination
demonstrations or successful runs for specific restricted workloads.

Fix a universal prefix machine \(U\).  Chaitin complexity is
\[
                         K_U(x)=\min\{|p|:U(p)=x\}.
\]
It is generally uncomputable.  Exhibiting a self-description of length \(L\) demonstrates only \(K_U(x)\leq L\).
For universal prefix machines \(U,V\), invariance supplies constants independent of \(x\):
\[
K_U(x)\leq K_V(x)+c_{UV},\qquad K_V(x)\leq K_U(x)+c_{VU}.
\]
The difference is uniformly bounded in both directions, but there is no exact shift and no machine-independent
complexity value.  Incompressibility, randomness, or a lower bound requires separate evidence.  Metered evaluation
cost is not program length or \(K_U\), and the report names the chosen \(U\).

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: a short self-description is only an upper bound.}
If a description of length \(500\) reproduces report \(x\), then \(K_U(x)\leq500\) for the declared \(U\).  Another
description may be shorter.  The invariance inequalities bound changes between universal prefix machines but do not
make their values equal; the report must name the convention.

The numerical metering algorithm iterates representation, parsing, evaluation, and replay states.  It slices counters
by setup, representation, decoding, target evaluation, meter overhead, and reporting where the counter semantics
permit.  Each slice preserves requested, spent, and remaining budgets.  Shared work and interaction terms are retained
rather than assigned to one slice by fiat.

This evaluator--representation relation is internal to a model and supplies no name bridge to the world.  If a
physical meter or robot is introduced, its covariant action instead records
\[
(N,s)\longmapsto(N',s',y,\text{interaction trace}),
\]
including back-action, sensor loading, actuation, energy or material exchange, latency, hysteresis, drift, saturation,
geometry, calibration, environment, prior state, rejected outcomes, and alternative causal models.  Observation
\(y\) answers that intervention in changed world \(N'\), not an untouched world.

Comparison reports exact, resolution, and uncertainty rows separately.  Exact equality compares values or integer
counters only after typed conversion and matched counter semantics.  Resolution equality compares declared labels.
Uncertainty compatibility compares sampling or calibration regions.  Same-process additionally requires compatible
object, representation, evaluator transitions, meter intervention, traces, resource currencies, releases,
configuration, deterministic inputs, declared nondeterministic inputs, and provenance.  Equal outputs or nearby costs
do not establish it.

Budgets distinguish representation length and growth, parser work, evaluation fuel, recursion depth, allocation,
search, trace storage, meter overhead, replay, and report construction.  Stops include \textsc{complete},
\textsc{representation-failure}, \textsc{decode-failure}, \textsc{roundtrip-failure}, \textsc{domain-error},
\textsc{fuel}, \textsc{recursion-bound}, \textsc{allocation-bound}, \textsc{search-bound},
\textsc{growth-bound}, \textsc{trace-capacity}, \textsc{external-action-unresolved}, and
\textsc{provenance-incomplete}.
