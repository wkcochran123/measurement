\chapter{The Native Apparatus Presents the Residue}
\chaptermark{The Native Apparatus}

\section{Apparatus ratio}

The internal apparatus begins with integer data
\[
                         A=(n,d),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,\quad d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
It denotes the exact rational \(n/d\).  Positivity of \(d\) is part of the domain, not a conclusion of division.
If an external representation permits \(d<0\), normalization first replaces \((n,d)\) by \((-n,-d)\).  A zero
denominator is rejected and produces no ratio, quotient, or remainder.

For \(d>0\), Euclidean floor division produces the unique integers \(q,r\) satisfying
\[
                         n=qd+r,\qquad 0\leq r<d,
\qquad q=\left\lfloor\frac nd\right\rfloor.
\]
The sign convention matters when \(n<0\).  For example,
\[
                         -7=(-3)\cdot3+2.
\]
Truncation toward zero would instead report quotient \(-2\) and signed leftover \(-1\); that is a different
operation and does not satisfy the displayed Euclidean remainder range.

Scaled floor at integer scale \(M>0\) applies the same operation to \(nM\):
\[
 Q_M=\left\lfloor\frac{nM}{d}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 R_M=nM-Q_Md,\qquad 0\leq R_M<d.
\]
The pair \((Q_M,R_M)\) retains the exact decomposition.  Reporting only \(Q_M\) is a slicing projection: it erases
the fractional residue \(R_M/d\).  Decimal punctuation or formatting is a later projection and must state its sign,
padding, rounding, and boundary rules.

All arithmetic is integer arithmetic.  Exactness does not mean cost-free: multiplication by \(M\), quotient
selection, greatest-common-divisor normalization, and replay can increase intermediate bit length.  A resource
record therefore distinguishes operation count, requested/spent/remaining arithmetic budget, maximum bit length,
storage, and comparison effort.  A bounded integer representation also checks overflow before multiplication;
wrapping arithmetic is not an exact ratio operation.

The iterative division algorithm starts with \(a_0=n\), \(b_0=d>0\).  One floor-division step yields
\[
                         a_k=q_kb_k+r_k,\qquad0\leq r_k<b_k.
\]
For a single apparatus ratio it stops after this step with \(q_0,r_0\).  If normalization or a greatest common divisor
is requested, iteration continues with \((a_{k+1},b_{k+1})=(b_k,r_k)\) while \(r_k>0\).  Each step strictly decreases
the positive divisor, so finite termination follows.  The quotient sequence and remainder trace must not be confused
with the single scaled-floor projection.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: exact positive division (computation).}
For \(n=17,d=5\),
\[
                         17=3\cdot5+2.
\]
The certificate checks equality and \(0\leq2<5\).  Quotient \(3\), remainder \(2\), and rational \(17/5\) are three
related fields, not interchangeable names.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: scaled floor and retained loss (computation).}
For \(n=17,d=5,M=100\),
\[
                         1700=340\cdot5+0.
\]
The scale happens to resolve this rational exactly.  For \(M=10\), the result is also exact; for \(n=1,d=3,M=100\),
\[
                         100=33\cdot3+1,
\]
so the displayed quotient \(33\) omits residue \(1/3\) in scaled units.  Retaining \(n,d,M,Q_M,R_M\) makes the loss
replayable.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: denominator zero (computation).}
Input \((5,0)\) has no rational interpretation and no Euclidean quotient.  Returning zero, infinity, or a sentinel
numeral as if it were a ratio would merge a domain failure with numerical data.  The report stops
\textsc{zero-denominator} and retains the rejected input.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: equal displayed quotient, unequal ratios (computation).}
At scale \(M=10\), both \(1/3\) and \(17/50\) have floor quotient \(3\), yet
\[
                         \frac13\ne\frac{17}{50}.
\]
They are resolution-equivalent under that declared floor quantizer, not exactly the same number.  Their remainders
and denominators differ, and no uncertainty claim follows from the collision.

\paragraph{Descent experiment 10: the native ratio becomes the exact crossing question (device arithmetic audit).}
The device's Cavendish-named report is used here only through its exact arithmetic surface.  The built calibration
record has numerator \(18\) and denominator \(2132\).  The latter is the product of the report's source-total,
test-mass, and arm fields.  Under the manuscript's positive-rational interpretation \(d=a/b\) with \(a,b>0\),
unfolding the source definition gives
\[
 A:\mathbb N_{>0}\times\mathbb N_{>0}\longrightarrow
 \mathbb N\times\mathbb N_{>0},\qquad
 A(a,b)=\bigl(18\cdot2132\cdot b^2,\ 2132\cdot a^2\bigr).
\]
Its codomain is an unreduced apparatus-ratio record.  The next transform is the exact target-\(5\) crossing question
\[
 C_5(n,d)=[\,5d\leq n\,]:
 \mathbb N\times\mathbb N_{>0}\longrightarrow\{\mathsf{yes},\mathsf{no}\}.
\]
Substitution shows that the common positive factor cancels in the mathematical comparison:
\[
 C_5(A(a,b))\Longleftrightarrow
 5\cdot2132\,a^2\leq18\cdot2132\,b^2
 \Longleftrightarrow 5a^2\leq18b^2.
\]
The executable does not divide or take a square root.  It squares natural-number fields, multiplies, and compares.
At \(d=1/1\) the trace reads \(5\leq18\), hence yes; at \(d=2/1\) it reads \(20\leq18\), hence no.  These opposed
endpoint answers establish a crossing question on the declared endpoints.  They do not by themselves prove
monotonicity over every positive rational or select a unique point.

Let \(R(n,d)=(n/\gcd(n,d),d/\gcd(n,d))\) be positive rational reduction and let
\(A'(a,b)=(18b^2,a^2)\).  The comparison test is the commuting diamond
\[
                         C_5\circ A=C_5\circ R\circ A
                         =C_5\circ A'.
\]
For every admitted positive \(a,b\), all three Boolean results agree because reduction and cancellation preserve
positive-ratio order.  This manuscript calculation demonstrates that the crossing predicate consumes the same
rational quantity the
displayed formula describes even though the stored report retains the unreduced factor \(2132\).  By contrast, the
floor transform \(Q(n,d)=n/d\) does not commute with a crossing decision at an arbitrary noninteger target: floor can
erase which side of a rational boundary a value occupies.  The retained remainder witnesses that possible loss.

The result also exposes the analytic boundary without claiming that the program computes it.  Over positive reals,
equality in the reduced comparison is \(5(a/b)^2=18\), so the model boundary is
\(d_*=\sqrt{18/5}\).  The device performs the integer inequality on rational probes; the square root describes where
that inequality changes under the separately stated monotonic law for \(18/d^2\).  A finite probe trace demonstrates
the implemented predicate, while enclosure of \(d_*\) requires opposed endpoints and a certified refinement route.

The positivity assumptions, target \(5\), exact calibration fields, and comparison orientation are guards for this
mathematical interpretation and its cancellation argument.  They are not asserted as source-level validation
statuses, budgets, stops, or certificate fields.  The source provides the ratio and Boolean computations; the
manuscript supplies the typed comparison and records the assumptions under which its algebra is valid.

Two controlled perturbations locate the result.  Replace target \(5\) by \(4\) while retaining the same distance
transform.  At \(d=2\) the comparison becomes \(16\leq18\), so both endpoints answer yes and the opposed-endpoint row
fails.  The arithmetic transform remains valid; only the requested crossing changed.  Alternatively keep target
\(5\) and replace calibration numerator \(18\) by \(17\).  The endpoint answers remain opposed, but the analytic
boundary becomes \(\sqrt{17/5}\).  Reusing \(\sqrt{18/5}\) after that change would be a stale-description failure.
In both cases the trace localizes the change to a governed input rather than treating a different Boolean or boundary
as numerical noise.

The common factor offers another replay check.  Multiply both calibration fields by positive \(k\).  The stored ratio
and intermediate products change, but \(C_5\) is unchanged after cross multiplication.  Crossing equality passes
under the scale transform while byte-level trace and resource-cost equality need not.  At \(k=0\), the manuscript's
positive-rational interpretation and cancellation theorem are inapplicable because the denominator is no longer
positive.  This is a guard on the stated argument, not a claim that the source exports a \(k=0\) stop.

Finally, face classification is a further transform, not part of the crossing proof.  If the ratio floor is sent to
a three-face map, the manuscript audit retains quotient, remainder, and face rule.  Equal crossing bits do not imply equal
floors or faces, and equal faces need not imply equal crossing bits.  Keeping those rows apart prevents native
corridor vocabulary from hiding the exact inequality that produced the result.

The native face path can nevertheless be replayed as its own finite diagram.  Define
\[
 Q:\mathbb N\times\mathbb N_{>0}\to\mathbb N,\qquad Q(n,d)=n/d,
\]
and
\[
 \Phi:\mathbb N\to
 \{\mathsf{charge},\mathsf{mass},\mathsf{value}\},\qquad
 \Phi(q)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf{charge},&q\bmod3=0,\\
 \mathsf{mass},&q\bmod3=1,\\
 \mathsf{value},&q\bmod3=2.
 \end{cases}
\]
The executed report path is \(d\mapsto A(d)\mapsto Q(A(d))\mapsto\Phi(Q(A(d)))\).  At \(d=2\), exact division reads
floor \(4\), a nonzero remainder, and face mass; at \(d=1\), it reads floor \(18\), remainder \(0\), and face charge.
These endpoint face labels do not order the crossing.  In particular, charge at distance one does not mean
\emph{before} mass in a numerical order; \(\Phi\) is periodic and many larger floors return charge again.

Near the first target-\(5\) crossing, a rational probe on the noncrossed side can have floor \(4\) and mass face,
while a sufficiently close crossed-side probe can have floor \(5\) and value face.  That local adjacency is obtained
only when the trace verifies both floor values and both exact crossing inequalities.  The Boolean square
\[
 C_5(A(d))=[\,5\leq Q(A(d))\,]
\]
commutes for this integer target because \(5\leq n/d\) is equivalent to \(5d\leq n\) under positive denominator.
It would not justify replacing the ratio by \(\Phi(Q(A(d)))\): the face map erases all but the remainder class
modulo three.  The comparison residual after face projection is the lost quotient magnitude and Euclidean remainder,
not a physical force or motion.  This replay connects native crossing and face arithmetic while keeping sign, phase,
and physical-name bridges open.

The \(18/2132\) fields and endpoint Booleans are source computations.  Cancellation, the diamond, scale invariance,
and face comparison are guarded manuscript calculations; the monotonic real model and square-root boundary are
interpretations.  No laboratory \(G\) or torque is measured.  The owned result is exactly the device arithmetic and
crossing predicate displayed above.

Comparison uses three separate relations.  Exact same-number after typed conversion is checked by cross-products:
\[
                         n_1d_2=n_2d_1.
\]
Resolution equality compares declared scaled or formatted labels.  Uncertainty compatibility compares declared
regions after unit and domain conversion.  Each row independently passes, fails, or remains unresolved.  No combined
agreement bit may hide which relation passed.

The ratio is computation-owned.  It has no physical unit unless a later apparatus supplies one, and it is not an
external coupling or physical fine-structure value.  Numerical resemblance cannot create that bridge.  Physical
balance, calibration, force, and uncertainty belong to the next section.
\section{The physical balance}

A torsion balance measures torque through a calibrated angular response.  In a symmetric idealization, two small
masses \(m\) lie at lever arm \(L\) from a suspended axis, two source masses \(M\) produce approximately tangential
forces, and the fiber supplies restoring torque.  Static equilibrium is
\[
                         \tau_{\rm ext}(\theta;\mathbf g)=\kappa\theta,
\]
where \(\kappa\) is the torsional spring constant in newton metres per radian and \(\mathbf g\) records measured
geometry.  Under a simplified inverse-square model,
\[
                         F=G\frac{Mm}{r^2},\qquad
                         \tau_{\rm ext}\approx2LF,
\]
so
\[
                         G\approx\frac{\kappa\theta r^2}{2LMm}.
\]
This estimator is conditional on geometry, force direction, point-mass approximation, equilibrium, and the absence
or correction of competing torques.

The apparatus record includes masses and their calibration, center-to-center distances, lever arms, source-mass
positions, fiber material and dimensions, vacuum and temperature, leveling, magnetic and electrostatic shielding,
readout geometry, timing, damping, and source-position protocol.  Every quantity carries units and uncertainty.
Dimensional consistency is necessary:
\[
\left[\frac{\kappa\theta r^2}{LMm}\right]
=\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}},
\]
but dimensions alone do not identify \(G\) or validate the model.

For an optical lever of path length \(D\), a small angular twist may produce spot displacement
\[
                         x-x_0\approx2D\theta,
\qquad \widehat\theta_{\rm lin}=\frac{x-x_0}{2D}.
\]
A more complete readout model retains the calibrated nonlinear relation, for example
\[
                         x-x_0=D\tan(2\theta),
\qquad
\widehat\theta=\frac12\arctan\frac{x-x_0}{D}.
\]
The linear approximation requires a demonstrated angular error bound over the admitted range.  Saturation, beam
clipping, detector nonlinearity, reference drift, and ambiguous spot position are readout failures.

The torsion constant can be calibrated dynamically.  For moment of inertia \(I\), lightly damped free oscillation has
\[
                         T_0=2\pi\sqrt{\frac I\kappa},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{4\pi^2I}{T_0^2},
\]
under the stated oscillator model.  Period estimation requires timestamp calibration, transient handling, damping
assessment, and uncertainty in \(I\).  A static calibration with known torque is a different route and retains
different systematics.

Numerical estimation begins with raw time, readout, source-position, and environmental series.  It validates units
and calibration intervals, preserves rejected samples and reasons, subtracts a preregistered baseline, estimates
equilibrium or fits a damped-oscillation model, converts readout to angle, computes torque, and fits the physical
parameter.  Iterative nonlinear fitting records every parameter vector, residual, objective value, accepted step,
rejected step, and convergence check.

\paragraph{Worked measurement 1: source-position reversal (physics).}
Alternate source masses between configurations \(A\) and \(B\).  If fitted equilibria are
\(\theta_A,\theta_B\), use
\[
                         \Delta\theta=\frac{\theta_A-\theta_B}{2}
\]
under a model in which the gravitational torque reverses and a constant zero offset does not.  The subtraction
rejects that offset but not drift synchronized with source motion.  Randomization, blinding, dwell time, and a drift
model belong in the certificate.

\paragraph{Worked measurement 2: covariance propagation (physics).}
For
\[
\widehat G=\frac{\widehat\kappa\,\widehat\theta\,\widehat r^2}
{2\widehat L\,\widehat M\,\widehat m},
\]
write \(u=(\log\kappa,\log|\theta|,\log r,\log L,\log M,\log m)\).  First-order relative variance is
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\log|\widehat G|)
\approx a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_u a,\qquad a=(1,1,2,-1,-1,-1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Cross-covariances matter when geometry or calibration standards are shared.  Adding marginal variances while
discarding covariance is not evidence of independence.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: deflection does not prove ontology (physics).}
A nonzero spot shift can arise from gravitational torque, thermal drift, tilt, magnetic coupling, electrostatics,
fiber creep, readout bias, or source-motion disturbance.  Deflection is read data.  Attribution to a parameter is
conditional on calibration, controls, alternative-model tests, and uncertainty; the needle does not name its cause.

Two-pass calibration compares a direct route and a stepped route through independently recorded intermediate
quantities.  The direct route maps calibrated equilibrium data to \(\widehat G_d\).  The stepped route first estimates
\(\widehat\kappa\), \(\widehat\theta\), and geometry, then composes them into \(\widehat G_s\).  Their raw data,
preprocessing, shared calibrations, covariance, numerical traces, budgets, and provenance remain visible.

The comparison reports independent rows:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{exact equality}&\widehat G_d=\widehat G_s\text{ after typed conversion},\\
\text{resolution equality}&q_\eta(\widehat G_d)=q_\eta(\widehat G_s),\\
\text{uncertainty compatibility}&\mathcal U_d\cap\mathcal U_s\ne\varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
Each passes, fails, or remains unresolved.  Physical floating estimates rarely satisfy exact equality, and interval
overlap is not exact same-number.  Shared apparatus can correlate routes, so agreement is not automatically
independent evidence or same-process.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: numerical convergence is not model validation (physics).}
A nonlinear fit may converge tightly under an incorrect geometry or unmodeled drift.  Stable parameters and small
iteration steps demonstrate numerical behavior conditional on the objective; residual structure, held-out source
positions, calibration checks, and alternative models test the physical claim.

The internal ratio of the preceding section is separate.  Its quotient and remainder neither cause a torque nor
measure \(G\).  They are not an external alpha value, orbit, field, spinor, force law, or variational identity.
Connecting an internal numerical report to this balance requires a typed, calibrated, tested bridge.
\section{Slicing the same ledger}

One exact ledger can support several numerical views without making those views interchangeable.  Use the normalized
record
\[
\mathcal L=(n,d),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,\quad d\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
\]
with exact interpretation \(v(\mathcal L)=n/d\).  If raw input has negative denominator, normalization negates both
entries; denominator zero stops before a ledger exists.

The quotient and remainder slice is
\[
\pi_{\rm div}(\mathcal L)=(q,r),\qquad n=qd+r,\quad0\leq r<d.
\]
The scaled-floor slice at \(M>0\) is
\[
\pi_M(\mathcal L)=(Q_M,R_M),\qquad nM=Q_Md+R_M,\quad0\leq R_M<d.
\]
The exact-value slice retains \((n,d)\) or its reduction by a positive greatest common divisor.  A display slice
retains only a formatted label derived from \(Q_M\) and therefore loses \(R_M\), denominator, and exact rational
unless those fields accompany it.

The reciprocal slice has a stricter domain:
\[
\pi_{\rm inv}(\mathcal L)=
\begin{cases}
(d,n),&n>0,\\
(-d,-n),&n<0,
\end{cases}
\]
followed by positive-denominator normalization.  If \(n=0\), the original ratio is valid but its reciprocal is not;
the inverse slice stops \textsc{zero-numerator}.  This failure does not invalidate quotient or scaled-floor slices
already certified.

Slicing differs from iteration.  A slice is a projection of one ledger.  The Euclidean algorithm evolves a state:
\[
(a_k,b_k)\longmapsto(b_k,r_k),\qquad
a_k=q_kb_k+r_k,\quad0\leq r_k<b_k,
\]
while \(b_k>0\).  The trace terminates when \(r_k=0\), yielding a greatest common divisor.  Reducing
\((n,d)\) by that divisor creates a canonically normalized value record; it does not reconstruct any information
discarded by an earlier display projection.

The data structure ties both methods together.  Each iterative state has the same numerator/positive-denominator
shape needed by the division slice, while every iteration step stores its quotient/remainder certificate.  Conversely,
the initial quotient slice is only the first row of the Euclidean trace when further iteration is requested.  The
ledger therefore supplies common exact data, but route and history fields distinguish one-shot slicing from repeated
state transition.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: several slices, one ledger (computation).}
For \(\mathcal L=(17,5)\),
\[
\pi_{\rm div}=(3,2),\qquad
\pi_{10}=(34,0),\qquad
\pi_{\rm inv}=(5,17).
\]
These outputs answer whole-part, scaled-display, and reciprocal questions.  Their agreement through the original
ledger does not make \(3\), \(34\), and \(5/17\) the same number.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: Euclidean iteration (computation).}
Starting from \((17,5)\),
\[
17=3\cdot5+2,\qquad5=2\cdot2+1,\qquad2=2\cdot1+0.
\]
The iterative trace yields greatest common divisor \(1\).  Keeping only first quotient \(3\) erases later quotients,
remainders, and the termination certificate.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: valid ratio, invalid reciprocal (computation).}
For \((0,7)\), division returns \(q=0,r=0\), and every positive scale returns \(Q_M=0,R_M=0\).  Reciprocal formation
fails because it would create denominator zero.  A single global success bit would conceal this slice-specific
failure.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: sign and truncation mismatch (computation).}
For \((-7,3)\), floor division gives
\[
                         -7=(-3)\cdot3+2.
\]
Truncation toward zero gives quotient \(-2\) with signed leftover \(-1\), a different convention.  A parser that
accepts both without a convention tag cannot replay the remainder range or compare reports.

\paragraph{Counterexample 5: formatted collision (computation).}
At \(M=100\), \(1/3\) and \(334/1000\) may both format as \(0.33\) under a two-decimal floor display.  They are
resolution-equivalent under that formatter, not exactly equal.  Exact cross-products and retained remainders expose
the difference.

Comparison retains three value relations.  Exact same-number is cross-product equality after typed normalization.
Resolution equality is equality of labels under one declared formatter.  Uncertainty compatibility is overlap of
declared regions after unit and domain conversion; integer truncation alone supplies no uncertainty model.  Each row
is independent, and process equality additionally requires matching inputs, requested slices, Euclidean transitions,
traces, resource currencies, configurations, and provenance.

This ledger is computational.  Quotient, remainder, trace length, and resource use are separately read fields.  Cost
is not the remainder; deterministic behavior is conditional on governed inputs and configuration; and no serial
fraction or performance ceiling is identified with an arithmetic residue.  No physical interpretation follows.
