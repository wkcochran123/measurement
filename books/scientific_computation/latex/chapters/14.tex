\section{The two propositions}

Let \(u\) and \(d\) be two finite records, let \(a\) be a declared orientation label, and define atomic propositions
\[
                         U:\equiv(u.\mathrm{orientation}=a),\qquad
                         D:\equiv(d.\mathrm{orientation}=a).
\]
The two descriptions are the proposition-shaped expressions
\[
                         P:\equiv U\land D,\qquad
                         Q:\equiv D\land U.
\]
Their content differs only in conjunction order.  No particle, orbit, pair, or physical system is introduced by these
formulas.

There are explicit implication maps
\[
                         P\to Q,\quad (u_0,d_0)\mapsto(d_0,u_0),
\qquad
                         Q\to P,\quad (d_0,u_0)\mapsto(u_0,d_0).
\]
Thus \(P\leftrightarrow Q\).  Propositional extensionality is the declared principle
\[
                         (P\leftrightarrow Q)\Longrightarrow(P=Q),
\]
which converts logical equivalence into equality of propositions.  The equality concerns proposition values in the
logical universe.  It does not identify their syntax, conjunction order, evaluation traces, resource costs, names,
input records, or any objects those names may suggest.

A difference predicate is likewise only a proposition.  For records \(x,y\) with declared equality,
\[
                         \operatorname{Different}(x,y):\equiv x\ne y.
\]
It classifies a relation between two records.  It is not a particle, residual substance, force, or physical event.
A decision procedure returning yes or no for this proposition returns a Boolean classification together with its
declared semantics; word resonance adds nothing.

Decidability and truth evidence remain separate.  Decidability supplies a terminating procedure which, for a given
proposition \(A\), returns either evidence for \(A\) or evidence for \(A\to\bot\).  It does not by itself supply the
positive branch.  A bare Boolean true bit licenses \(A\) only when a demonstrated soundness relation connects that
bit to \(A\).  A procedure name or successful termination is not a truth witness.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: finite evaluation of the atoms (computation).}
Read the two orientation fields, compare each with \(a\), and obtain Boolean rows \(b_U,b_D\).  Under validated
comparison soundness, \(b_U=b_D=\mathsf{true}\) supplies evidence for both \(P\) and \(Q\).  Swapping the two evidence
fields demonstrates equivalence.  The swap changes order even when proposition equality is accepted.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: equal propositions, different computations (computation).}
One evaluator checks \(U\) before \(D\); another checks \(D\) before \(U\).  If the first check can fail early, their
traces and costs can differ even though \(P=Q\) under extensionality.  Proposition equality is therefore not
same-process evidence.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: decision is not the positive branch (computation).}
If \(u.\mathrm{orientation}\ne a\), the atomic decision returns the negative alternative.  The procedure still
terminates correctly, but there is no evidence for \(U\), \(P\), or \(Q\).  Treating decidable as true erases the
returned branch.

\paragraph{Vacuum draw: the prover--verifier receipt and its weak ceiling.}
The Prover--Verifier executable is the common \texttt{CountSetup}
\[
                         S=\mathbb N^3,\qquad s=(r,a,q),
\]
whose fields are recorded, accessible, and required counts.  The transform is the common count runner
\(T_P:S\to\operatorname{CountOutput}\); the exported claim then projects its output to the preservation predicate
\[
                         q\le a.
\]
For \((2,3,2)\), the predicate is true.  For an arbitrary setup the theorem requires the explicit precondition
\((T_P(s)).\mathrm{required}\le(T_P(s)).\mathrm{accessible}\); if it fails, the exported positive claim does not
follow.

More explicitly, \texttt{runCount} copies the three inputs and computes the fourth field
\(\mathrm{slack}=a-q\) using truncated natural subtraction.  The default output is therefore \((2,3,2,1)\).
A setup \((2,1,2)\) returns \((2,1,2,0)\): zero slack hides the deficit, while \(2\le1\) is false.  The separate
\texttt{finiteLedgerModel} tag does not strengthen \(q\le a\), and the preservation branch ignores \(r\).

Under the identity representation of \texttt{CountOutput}, the direct and audited routes commute because they are the
same function with additional bookkeeping:
\[
 \operatorname{id}(T_P(s))=
 \operatorname{id}(\operatorname{auditedExperiment.run}(s)).
\]
The colocated detector also decides \(q\le a\).  These definitional agreements verify wrapper fidelity, not
independence.  The evidence ceiling remains three natural counts and one inequality; there is no proof language or
soundness theorem for arbitrary certificates.

The finite decision algorithm parses the records, validates the orientation domain and equality version, evaluates
the two atomic comparisons, constructs conjunction rows in each order, verifies the swap maps, and---only if
proposition equality is requested---checks that propositional extensionality is among the declared logical
dependencies.  It slices syntax, atomic outcomes, implication maps, equivalence, proposition equality, trace, cost,
and status into separate report fields.

Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{record-missing}, \textsc{field-missing}, \textsc{domain-mismatch},
\textsc{equality-version-mismatch}, \textsc{atomic-decision-failure}, \textsc{negative-branch},
\textsc{implication-failure}, \textsc{equivalence-unresolved}, \textsc{extensionality-not-declared},
\textsc{budget}, and \textsc{provenance-incomplete}.  The negative branch is a semantic outcome, not a resource
failure.  A budget stop leaves the untested proposition unresolved.

A certificate records raw records and labels, proposition syntax, equality and decision semantics, atomic comparison
inputs and outputs, soundness evidence, positive or negative branches, conjunction order, implication maps,
equivalence row, declared extensionality dependency, proposition-equality row, evaluator traces, resource counters,
budgets, stops, checked scope, unresolved frontier, releases, snapshot hash, configuration, protocol, run identity,
platform, deterministic inputs, and declared nondeterministic inputs.

Comparison follows separate relations.  Exact proposition equality is the declared extensional result after typed
conversion.  Syntax equality compares expression trees and fails when conjunction order differs.  Resolution equality
compares labels under a declared projection.  Same-process requires compatible records, comparison order,
short-circuit policy, traces, costs, configuration, and provenance.  No combined equality flag may conceal which row
passed.

These propositions belong to contravariant naming: the model chooses predicates and pulls records into its own
classifications.  They do not supply a covariant answer from the world.  Physical naming would require an apparatus
or robot action
\[
                         (N,s)\longmapsto(N',s',y,\Gamma),
\]
with calibration, geometry, environment, prior state, interaction trace, back-action, sensor loading, actuation,
energy and material exchange, latency, hysteresis, drift, saturation, rejected outcomes, uncertainty, and alternative
causal models.  The proposition equality neither constructs this action nor interprets its observation.

Governance fixes record and orientation schemas, equality and decision semantics, proposition syntax, evaluation
order, short-circuit policy, implication maps, logical dependencies, comparison relations, budgets, stop priority,
and certificate grammar.  A changed field begins a new report.

The action ledger is exact.  Records, label, predicates, equality, decision semantics, evaluation order,
extensionality dependency, comparisons, budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Atomic comparison
soundness, implication maps, logical equivalence, conditional proposition equality, decision termination, and
certificate implications are \emph{demonstrated}.  Atomic outcomes, branches, conjunction rows, traces, costs, stops,
and provenance are \emph{read}.  Positive truth from decidability alone, syntax identity from proposition equality,
same-process or equal cost from equivalence, object identity, particle meaning for difference, electron descriptions,
self-energy, alpha, physical identity, and every world answer absent apparatus remain \emph{reserved}.
\section{The cost readings}

Three counter reports are kept distinct:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
H_{\rm orbit}&\text{the governed counter reading for the first proposition-shaped workload},\\
H_{\rm pair}&\text{the governed counter reading for the reordered proposition-shaped workload},\\
H_{\rm echo}&\text{the governed counter reading for the equivalence/equality workload}.
\end{array}
\]
The names orbit, pair, and echo are manuscript labels for computational workload roles.  They do not introduce an
orbit, a pair of particles, or an acoustic or physical process.

Under one recorded configuration, the exact tuple is
\[
                         (H_{\rm orbit},H_{\rm pair},H_{\rm echo})
                         =(573,573,552).
\]
The signed self-residual is defined by subtraction orientation:
\[
                         R_{\rm self}=H_{\rm echo}-H_{\rm orbit}=552-573=-21.
\]
This is an exact integer identity in the declared counter currency.  Reversing subtraction gives \(21\), a different
reported field.  Clamping the negative result or taking an absolute value would erase orientation.

Proposition equality and counter equality are separate.  The extensional relation between the two propositions does
not imply equal syntax, evaluation order, traces, or costs.  Here \(H_{\rm orbit}=H_{\rm pair}\) is an independently
read counter row under this configuration; it is not a consequence of logical equivalence.  Likewise, the unequal
echo count does not weaken proposition equality.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: exact tuple replay (computation).}
Replay validates the three workload registrations, counter semantics, outputs, and stops, then reads \(573,573,552\).
Arithmetic replay computes \(573-573=0\) for pair minus orbit and \(552-573=-21\) for echo minus orbit.  These are
different slices of one tuple, not competing interpretations of one residual.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: value equality does not force cost equality (computation).}
Two evaluators can return the same proposition value while checking conjunction fields in opposite orders.  If one
field fails early, traces and costs differ.  Same value licenses no same-cost or same-process conclusion.

For repeated paired runs \(i=1,\ldots,N\), \(N\geq2\), define
\[
                         \delta_i=H_{{\rm echo},i}-H_{{\rm orbit},i}.
\]
Under a declared independent or exchangeable-pair model,
\[
\bar\delta=\frac1N\sum_i\delta_i,\qquad
s_\delta^2=\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(\delta_i-\bar\delta)^2,\qquad
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\bar\delta)=s_\delta^2/N.
\]
This last estimate is invalid when blocks, drift, cache history, or run order correlate pairs.  With residual
covariance matrix \(\Sigma_\delta\),
\[
                         \operatorname{Var}(\bar\delta)
=N^{-2}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_\delta\mathbf1,
\]
or a preregistered clustered/block method must match the acquisition design.

The direct route measures the three workloads in a declared paired block.  The stepped route records setup, warmup,
orbit, pair, and echo stages with their intermediate counters before subtracting.  Telescoping differences may satisfy
an exact arithmetic identity when they reuse the same readings, but separately acquired routes remain different
processes with separate covariance and provenance.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: build-to-build reproducibility (computation).}
Suppose two governed builds each return \((573,573,552)\).  Tuple equality and residual equality are read.  Matching
counter semantics, compiler release, dependencies, snapshot, configuration, budgets, warmup, cache policy, workload
representations, deterministic inputs, and declared nondeterministic inputs are posited and validated from their
receipts.  The result is reproducibility under that relation, not intrinsic sameness on every machine.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: near counts are not same process (computation).}
Two routes may return residuals \(-21\) and \(-20\).  A coarse quantizer may give them one label, but the exact values
differ.  Even exact residual equality would not establish matching traces, cache interactions, or workload
transitions.

The measurement algorithm registers workload roles, validates common counter semantics, executes randomized or
balanced blocks, records setup and warmup, reads each output and counter, constructs the tuple, computes signed slices,
and updates paired covariance summaries.  Failed and rejected runs remain with reasons.  Iteration is over blocks and
pairs; slicing produces orbit--pair, echo--orbit, absolute, formatted, or statistical views only when requested, with
every erasure recorded.

Budgets distinguish registration, setup, warmup, each workload, repetitions, blocks, counter allowance, allocation,
search, arithmetic accumulation, covariance estimation, storage, and replay.  Stops include \textsc{complete},
\textsc{registration-failure}, \textsc{setup-failure}, \textsc{warmup-budget}, \textsc{counter-budget},
\textsc{output-failure}, \textsc{unpaired-run}, \textsc{insufficient-pairs},
\textsc{dependence-model-invalid}, \textsc{configuration-mismatch}, \textsc{cache-state-unknown},
\textsc{overflow}, and \textsc{provenance-incomplete}.

A certificate records workload definitions and representations, proposition outputs, proposition comparison rows,
counter semantics, compiler and dependency releases, snapshot hash, configuration, setup and warmup, process and
cache policy, raw tuples, block and pair identifiers, run order and randomization, signed subtraction orientation,
residual slices, exact sums, covariance and dependence model, direct and stepped routes, rejected and failed runs,
budgets, stops, checked scope, unresolved frontier, protocol, run identity, platform, deterministic inputs, and
declared nondeterministic inputs.

Comparison reports exact equality, resolution equality under a declared quantizer, and uncertainty compatibility as
independent pass/fail/unresolved rows.  Same-process additionally requires matching workload inputs, evaluation
transitions, traces, counter policy, setup, warmup, cache state, resource currencies, configuration, and provenance.
No combined agreement bit can replace these rows.

Governance fixes workload roles, representations, counter semantics, subtraction orientation, pairing, block order,
randomization, setup, warmup, cache policy, dependence model, direct and stepped routes, slicing, comparisons, budgets,
stop priority, and certificate grammar.  A changed field begins a new series.

Counter cost is neither a witness value nor a physical measurement.  It is not Landauer energy, heat, self-energy,
alpha, coupling, or evidence of electron identity.  A physical energy claim would require a calibrated world-facing
meter or robot action with interaction trace, loading, back-action, energy exchange, timing, environment, uncertainty,
and alternative causal models.

The action ledger is exact.  Workloads, counter semantics, roles, pairing, subtraction orientation, routes, sampling,
dependence model, comparisons, budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Integer subtraction, tuple
identities, paired summaries, covariance arithmetic, conditional reproducibility comparisons, and certificate
implications are \emph{demonstrated}.  Outputs, counter tuples, residuals, blocks, pair order, covariance estimates,
configuration rows, stops, and provenance are \emph{read}.  Cost equality from proposition equality, proposition
identity from equal cost, same-process from near or exact counts, naive variance under correlated pairs, intrinsic
build independence, Landauer energy or heat, witness value, self-energy, alpha, coupling, electron identity, and all
physical measure remain \emph{reserved}.
\section{The proposed second pass}

The proposed calibration has three typed roles \(a,b,c\).  At any pass, two roles are supplied by direct returned or
verified values, while the remaining role is unresolved and reconstructed through a preregistered relation.  The
unresolved marker is part of state:
\[
                         S=(a,b,c;\,u),\qquad u\in\{a,b,c\}.
\]
It is not a zero, missing-value default, or physical absence.

Let \(A,B,C\) be the three value domains and let \(Z\) be a common composite domain.  A declared composite is
\[
                         \Sigma:A\times B\times C\to Z.
\]
When \(c\) is unresolved, the forward reconstruction map is
\[
                         R_c:A\times B\times Z\to C,
\]
and when \(a\) is unresolved after role exchange, the reverse reconstruction map is
\[
                         R_a:B\times C\times Z\to A.
\]
Both maps require domains, units, versions, ambiguity policies, and failure results.  A single map in one direction
does not establish round-trip calibration.

Pass one returns or verifies \(a_0,b_0\), reads a composite report \(z_1\), and proposes
\[
                         \widehat c_1=R_c(a_0,b_0,z_1).
\]
Pass two changes roles: \(b\) and \(c\) are directly returned or verified under the second protocol, a new composite
\(z_2\) is read, and
\[
                         \widehat a_2=R_a(b_2,c_2,z_2).
\]
The overlap rows compare \(b_0\) with \(b_2\), \(\widehat c_1\) with \(c_2\), and \(\widehat a_2\) with \(a_0\) under
their declared relations.  Agreement is not one Boolean.  Each row separately reports exact equality, resolution
equality, and uncertainty-region compatibility as pass, fail, or unresolved.

Round-trip closure requires both reconstruction directions and comparison maps:
\[
                         a_0\xrightarrow{R_c\ {\rm with}\ b_0,z_1}\widehat c_1
\xrightarrow{R_a\ {\rm with}\ b_2,z_2}\widehat a_2,
\]
followed by the typed comparison \(\widehat a_2\) against \(a_0\).  It also requires the overlap comparison for the
carried role \(b\).  Closure is conditional on the reconstruction model, composite measurements, calibration, and
uncertainty.  It does not make the three roles one object.

The role scheduler is the exact finite rotation
\[
                         a\mapsto b,\qquad b\mapsto c,\qquad c\mapsto a.
\]
Three scheduler applications return every role, while one or two do not.  This certifies role order only.  It does
not certify value equality, reconstruction correctness, a physical cycle, phase, particle identity, or causal
mechanism.

The calibration itself has two measurement passes and one role exchange between them.  Three scheduler applications
returning every role is an algebraic property of the role scheduler, not a requirement for three calibration passes.

Target independence is procedural.  Before observing pass-two outcomes, governance fixes roles, interventions,
reconstruction maps, composite \(\Sigma\), comparison domains, quantizers, uncertainty model, budgets, exclusions,
and stop priority.  Choosing or tuning these after reading the target response invalidates the calibration row.
Preregistration does not guarantee model truth; it makes target-dependent selection auditable.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: exact algebraic round trip (computation).}
Let \(A=B=C=Z=\mathbb Q\), define \(\Sigma(a,b,c)=a+b+c\),
\[
R_c(a,b,z)=z-a-b,\qquad R_a(b,c,z)=z-b-c.
\]
If both passes use the same exact \(z=\Sigma(a_0,b_0,c_0)\) and return \(b_2=b_0,c_2=c_0\), then
\(\widehat c_1=c_0\) and \(\widehat a_2=a_0\).  This demonstrates the algebraic maps under exact shared inputs.  It
does not license a physical interpretation.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: one-pass planting (calibration).}
If \(R_c\) is chosen after seeing \(c_2\), it can be made to return that value by construction.  The resulting
agreement is target-dependent and fails preregistration.  The second pass earns information only when the world-facing
answer was not selected by the model.

The covariant pass acts through an apparatus or robot:
\[
                         (N,s)\longmapsto(N',s',y,\Gamma).
\]
The model supplies the contravariant question---which observable, role, and comparison to ask.  It may not supply
\(y\).  The interaction record \(\Gamma\) retains back-action, sensor loading, actuation, energy and material
exchange, latency, hysteresis, drift, saturation, geometry, calibration, environment, prior state, rejected outcomes,
and alternative causal models.  The world answers the intervention in \(N'\), not an untouched \(N\).

\paragraph{Worked example 3: role reversal with apparatus data (calibration).}
In pass one, channels \(a,b\) are calibrated directly and channel \(c\) is reconstructed from a composite observation.
In pass two, the apparatus directly calibrates \(b,c\) after a preregistered intervention and reconstructs \(a\).
Shared sensor standards and environment create covariance across overlap rows.  The report propagates this covariance
rather than calling the routes independent because their role names differ.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: internal closure is not physical licensing.}
A finite table may satisfy both reconstruction identities and exact role rotation using generated data.  That is
internal consistency.  Without a covariant apparatus action, calibration, uncertainty, and competing-cause tests, it
does not license physical names or claims about the world.

The numerical algorithm iterates passes and role rotations, validates which two roles are direct and which is
unresolved, parses composite reports, applies the appropriate reconstruction, and appends overlap rows.  It slices raw
returns, reconstructed values, composite values, exact/resolution/uncertainty comparisons, interaction trace, and
round-trip status without erasing their provenance.

Budgets distinguish apparatus interventions, samples, role rotations, direct validations, composite evaluations,
reconstruction branches, arithmetic growth, covariance and uncertainty propagation, comparison rows, storage, and
replay.  Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{role-invalid}, \textsc{unresolved-role-ambiguous},
\textsc{direct-value-missing}, \textsc{composite-domain}, \textsc{reconstruction-failure},
\textsc{nonidentifiable}, \textsc{overlap-failure}, \textsc{roundtrip-failure},
\textsc{preregistration-violation}, \textsc{intervention-budget}, \textsc{sensor-saturation},
\textsc{back-action-unresolved}, \textsc{uncertainty-unresolved}, and \textsc{provenance-incomplete}.

A certificate records role and value schemas, scheduler state, direct and unresolved status, \(\Sigma,R_c,R_a\),
domains and versions, preregistration timestamp and contents, raw and rejected values, both apparatus interventions,
world and robot before/after states, interaction traces, calibration, environment, reconstructed values, overlap rows,
covariance, exact/resolution/uncertainty results, round-trip maps, role-rotation checks, budgets, stops, checked scope,
unresolved frontier, releases, snapshot hash, configuration, protocol, run identity, platform, deterministic inputs,
and declared nondeterministic inputs.

Same-number and same-process remain separate.  Exact same-number requires equality after typed conversion; resolution
equality and uncertainty compatibility are weaker independent relations.  Same-process requires matching
interventions, inputs, state transitions, reconstruction maps, traces, costs, apparatus configuration, and provenance.
Round-trip value agreement alone does not establish it.

Governance fixes roles, scheduler, direct/unresolved policy, composite and reconstruction maps, preregistered
interventions, apparatus and observation model, comparison relations, uncertainty and covariance, budgets, stop
priority, and certificate grammar.  A changed field begins a new calibration.

The action ledger is exact.  Roles, scheduler, composite, reconstructions, interventions, apparatus model,
comparisons, uncertainty, budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Rotation closure, reconstruction
identities, conditional overlap and round-trip implications, covariance propagation, and certificate implications are
\emph{demonstrated}.  Direct returns, composite observations, reconstructed values, overlap rows, apparatus state
changes, interaction traces, stops, and provenance are \emph{read}.  An answer chosen by the model, one-pass
calibration, one object from role rotation, proposition equality as object or electron identity, physical licensing
from internal consistency, alpha, coupling, self-energy, and every name bridge absent a world answer remain
\emph{reserved}.
