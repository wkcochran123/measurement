\chapter{The Bracketed Number}

\section{Dyadic data structure}

A dyadically refined bracket is finite data, not an approximate spelling of an unknown point.  Write its primary
representation as
\[
             D=(L,U,Q),\qquad [D]=[L/Q,U/Q],
\]
where \(L,U\in\mathbb Z\), \(Q\in\mathbb N_{>0}\), and \(L<U\).  The integers are stored exactly; the interpretation
map supplies the scale.  A crossing question \(C\) orients the endpoints:
\[
                     C(L/Q)=\mathsf{yes},\qquad C(U/Q)=\mathsf{no}.
\]
Order and opposed endpoint answers are separate invariants.  Neither follows merely from the other, and neither says
that a unique point lies inside the bracket.  If a run starts at explicit denominator \(Q_0\), an optional rung index
\(k\) derives \(Q_k=2^kQ_0\).  Unless \(Q_0\) itself is a power of two, the represented rationals need not be dyadic
rationals in lowest terms; it is the refinement operation, not the initial rational grid, that is dyadic.

Dyadic refinement is a conversion followed by a slice.  Convert both endpoints to the next common denominator,
\[
 (L,U,Q)\longmapsto(2L,2U,2Q),\qquad M=L+U,
\]
so that \(M/(2Q)\) is the exact midpoint.  Reading \(C(M/(2Q))\) chooses one half:
\[
 (L,U,Q)\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
 (M,2U,2Q),&C(M/(2Q))=\mathsf{yes},\\
 (2L,M,2Q),&C(M/(2Q))=\mathsf{no}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The retained interval has width \((U-L)/(2Q)\).  One rung doubles the denominator and halves the interpreted
width.  The conversion is exact; only the predicate answer selects a slice.

Representation and value must not be confused.  The triples \((1,3,2)\) and \((2,6,4)\) denote the same interval.
A canonicalization divides \(L,U,Q\) by their positive greatest common divisor.  Canonicalization preserves the
interpreted interval but can erase the relation \(Q_k=2^kQ_0\), hence a refinement-history clue.  It belongs either
after replay or beside an unreduced trace, never in place of that trace.

An adjacent exact grid pair \(a<b\) with common positive denominator \(Q\),
\[
                 a=L/Q,\qquad b=U/Q,\qquad U-L=1,
\]
converts to \(D=(L,U,Q)\).  The conversion certifies common denominator, adjacency, and order.  It becomes an oriented
crossing bracket only after the separate evidence \(C(L/Q)=\mathsf{yes}\) and \(C(U/Q)=\mathsf{no}\) is read.
Grid conversion alone is not crossing evidence.

The numerical method is a finite state transition.  Given initial data \(D_0\), rung budget \(N\), and arithmetic
budget \(A\), it produces
\[
 D_0\xrightarrow{b_0}D_1\xrightarrow{b_1}\cdots
 \xrightarrow{b_{r-1}}D_r,\qquad r\leq N,\quad b_i\in\{\mathsf{yes},\mathsf{no}\}.
\]
Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{rung-budget}, \textsc{arithmetic-budget}, \textsc{domain-error},
\textsc{endpoint-not-opposed}, \textsc{order-failure}, and \textsc{predicate-unresolved}.  A budget stop owns the
\(r\) checked rungs and unresolved next transition; it does not assert that further refinement is impossible.

A replay certificate contains the initial triple, scale convention, predicate identity, arithmetic model, budgets,
the explicit \(Q_0\), optional rung indices, every midpoint numerator, every read branch bit, every retained triple,
and the stop.  Its checker verifies \(Q'=2Q\), midpoint addition, half selection, order, opposed endpoint answers,
fuel use, numerator gap, and the final laws
\[
 Q_r=2^rQ_0,\qquad U_r-L_r=U_0-L_0,\qquad
 \operatorname{width}(D_r)=\frac{U_0-L_0}{2^rQ_0}.
\]
Checks are sliced into pass, fail, and unresolved rows.  Passing arithmetic replay does not certify predicate
soundness; passing endpoint checks without a trace does not certify the route.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: two exact rungs (computation).}
Let \(D_0=(1,2,1)\), so \(Q_0=1\), with endpoint answers yes and no.  The optional rung index is \(k=0\).  The first
midpoint is \(3/2\).  If its answer is yes, retain \(D_1=(3,4,2)\), denoting \([3/2,2]\), with \(k=1\).  Its next
midpoint is \(7/4\).  If that answer is no, retain \(D_2=(6,7,4)\), denoting \([3/2,7/4]\), with \(k=2\).  The widths
\(1,1/2,1/4\) and both slice choices replay without rounding.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: denominator growth is not value growth (computation).}
The conversion from \((3,4,2)\) to \((6,8,4)\) changes neither endpoint.  Reading the larger integers as unscaled
values would invent \([6,8]\).  Reading only \(Q=4\) while discarding the numerators would lose the bracket.  The triple
is the data structure; none of its fields is a reading by itself.

\paragraph{Counterexample 3: equal endpoints do not certify equal histories (computation).}
One run may reach \((6,7,4)\) by the two rungs above.  Another may receive it as initial data.  Their endpoint
projections agree, but their transition traces and costs do not.  The projection
\[
                    \pi(L,U,Q,\text{trace})=(L,U,Q)
\]
supports interval equality while erasing route, branch sequence, and budget use.  Equal projections cannot be pulled
back into equal processes.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: an unordered payload is not repaired by interpretation (computation).}
The triple \((5,3,4)\) fails \(L<U\).  Sorting it during validation would create a different report and could reverse
the required endpoint answers.  The original is rejected; an explicitly transformed triple requires new provenance
and fresh endpoint checks.

This dyadic route is legacy refinement data.  It must not be renamed as, or substituted for, the canonical seed-free
continued-fraction route.  That route performs Euclidean division with remainder, reads whole partial quotients, and
constructs ordered walls; for the owned count-three result its walls \(17/9\) and \(2\) induce the jar
\([129.6,137.7]\).  It is not denominator doubling, midpoint selection, or adjacent-grid conversion.  A dyadic endpoint
may numerically equal a wall, but equality of a rational value does not identify algorithms, traces, budgets, or
ownership claims.  The continued-fraction jar remains canonical; a dyadic run is a separately labelled diagnostic.

\section{Resolution as an explicit parameter}

Resolution is declared data, not a universal constant inferred from finite output.  Four controls must remain distinct:
\[
 \theta=(\mathcal X,\mathcal A,q,s,f;\,G,N,M,K),
\]
where \(\mathcal X\) is the input domain, \(\mathcal A=\{a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}\}\) a finite alphabet,
\(q:\mathcal X\to\mathcal A\) a quantizer, \(s>0\) its scale, and \(f\geq0\) a floor.  The grid parameter \(G\)
selects a finite exact grid; \(N\) bounds dyadic refinement rungs; \(M>0\) is the integer display scale; and \(K\)
counts whole continued-fraction partial quotients.  Each affects a different route.  The value \(G=0\) is a sentinel
meaning that no ordinary grid has been selected; it is not a grid of zero width and cannot enter division.
Its cells are \(B_j=q^{-1}(a_j)\).  Boundary conventions are required: a value on a cell wall cannot be assigned by an
unstated tie rule.

For a uniform scalar quantizer with origin \(o\), width \(\Delta>0\), and code range
\(\{j_{\min},\ldots,j_{\max}\}\), one grammar is
\[
 q(x)=j\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad o+j\Delta\leq x<o+(j+1)\Delta,
\]
with separate out-of-range stops.  Numerical resolution is \(\Delta\), while \(s\) converts code units to reported
units.  A floor may stop refinement when \(w_{r+1}<f\).  This is a stopping policy, not evidence that nature contains
no smaller distinction.

Rungs count transitions; cells partition a domain; scale interprets codes; the floor bounds admitted refinement; and
the quantizer chooses a cell.  Grid selection changes available exact cells, \(N\) changes dyadic depth, \(M\) changes
display quotient and residue, and \(K\) changes continued-fraction walls.  Collapsing these parameters hides which
operation lost information and falsely merges route ownership.

Parameter-relative equality is the equivalence relation
\[
                 x\equiv_\theta y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_\theta(x)=q_\theta(y).
\]
Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity follow from equality of labels; equality of underlying values does not.
A specification \(\theta'\) refines \(\theta\) when a map \(c:\mathcal A_{\theta'}\to\mathcal A_\theta\) satisfies
\(q_\theta=c\circ q_{\theta'}\), equivalently every fine cell lies inside its indicated coarse cell.  Refinement can
separate a former equivalence class but cannot justify comparisons if route, scale, or boundary conventions are
incompatible.

Finite evaluation retains \(\theta\), raw input, boundary decisions, transitions, spent resources, and the terminating
condition.  A budget stop owns only the checked prefix, not global indistinguishability.

Quantization is a many-to-one projection.  If \(x,y\in B_j\), then \(q(x)=q(y)\), but not necessarily \(x=y\).
Retaining only \(a_j\) erases within-cell order and distance as well as quantizer version, scale, boundaries, budgets,
and stop.  Equal summary symbols cannot identify equal readings across undeclared configurations.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: equivalence changes under refinement (computation).}
Let display quantization use \(q_M(x)=\lfloor Mx\rfloor\), and take \(x=1.2341\), \(y=1.2358\).  Then
\(q_{100}(x)=q_{100}(y)=123\), so \(x\equiv_{\theta_{100}}y\), whereas
\(q_{1000}(x)=1234\) and \(q_{1000}(y)=1235\).  On the aligned declared domain, the coarse-label map
\(c(j)=\lfloor j/10\rfloor\) satisfies \(q_{100}=c\circ q_{1000}\).  Thus the same floor convention gives a genuine
refinement: the coarse quantizer identifies the values and the fine quantizer separates them.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: a wall without a tie rule (computation).}
On the same grid, \(x=1/2\) lies exactly on a wall.  Different tie conventions can assign different symbols.
Reporting only code \(2\) hides the governing choice.  Without the convention, validation returns
\textsc{boundary-unresolved}, not a repaired code.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: finite rungs versus a declared floor (computation).}
An initial width \(1\), halved per rung, has width \(2^{-r}\).  For \(f=1/16\), a policy forbidding the next width
below \(f\) permits four halvings and stops before the fifth.  With \(N=3\), the run instead stops at width \(1/8\) by
\textsc{rung-budget}.  Distinct resource and floor stops prove no empirical minimum length.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: output symbols do not supply a probability law (computation).}
For a declared distribution \(p_j=\Pr(q(X)=a_j)\), Shannon entropy is
\[
                         H(q(X))=-\sum_{j:p_j>0}p_j\log_2p_j.
\]
Four declared equiprobable symbols give \(H=2\) bits.  Four possible symbols do not establish equiprobability, hence
do not establish two bits.  Alphabet size gives the ideal maximum \(\log_2m\), the entropy bound
\(H\leq\log_2m\) for a random variable with a declared law, and a fixed-width label cost
\(\lceil\log_2m\rceil\).  For \(m=8\), fixed-width labels require \(3\) bits, while \(H=3\) only for a declared uniform
law on all eight symbols.  An occupied alphabet observed in one sample is not the possible alphabet, and neither
supplies probabilities by itself.

\paragraph{Counterexample 5: a resource limit is not uncomputability (computation).}
Fix a universal prefix machine \(U\) and define
\[
                         K_U(x)=\min\{|p|:U(p)=x\}.
\]
This function is generally uncomputable.  An exhibited program of length \(n\) proves only \(K_U(x)\leq n\); absent a
separate lower-bound demonstration it does not establish incompressibility.  Likewise, a search stopping after \(10^6\)
transitions owns only that resource stop.  These facts do not make an empirical calibration constant uncomputable.
The count-three jar is not thereby random, incompressible, or empirically uncomputable.

For the canonical \(K=3\) route, whole partial quotients produce walls \(17/9\) and \(2\); the owned resolution
statement is the ordered-wall jar
\[
                             [129.6,137.7].
\]
That finite jar is the floor owned by the declared route and count.  Finer direct, stepped, \(M\)-formatted, \(G\)-grid,
or \(N\)-dyadic
diagnostics may expose narrower cells, but neither replace the jar nor change its walls.  Coincident displayed values
license only a declared-resolution comparison; route and process ownership remain separate.

The physics fence is strict.  Numerical cell width, rung floor, or alphabet is not by itself detector resolution,
noise, quantum uncertainty, repeatability, or uncertainty coverage.  Those require a sensor model, units, calibration
transfer, sampling procedure, environmental controls, and empirical evidence.  Numerical quantization models physical
acquisition only after that correspondence is posited and tested.

Route compatibility keeps \(G\) for grid construction, \(N\) for dyadic rungs, \(M\) for display projection, and
\(K\) for whole-partial-quotient walls.  For each scaled display retain
\[
                  n=\left\lfloor\frac{AM}{B}\right\rfloor,\qquad
                  \rho=AM-nB,
\]
so quotient and remainder expose the loss.  Changing a route parameter creates a new report rather than replacing the
canonical \(K=3\) jar.

\section{Stop rules and budgets}

A finite numerical method must say which mechanism stopped.  Five mechanisms remain separate:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{fuel recursion}&\text{performs at most \(N\) recursive transitions},\\
\text{finite grid scan}&\text{returns the first adjacent yes--no pair or exhausts a declared grid},\\
\text{continued fraction}&\text{reads exactly \(K\) whole partial quotients},\\
\text{bounded live probe}&\text{returns a result, absence, or fallback within a live effort bound},\\
\text{structural outgrowth}&\text{applies a deterministic reference-plus-deadband Boolean rule}.
\end{array}
\]
Fuel, cells visited, arithmetic operations or bit growth, checker effort, and wall time are distinct currencies.
None converts to another without a separately calibrated conversion model.  In particular, elapsed time cannot be
silently substituted for structural fuel.

Fuel recursion carries \(F\in\mathbb N\) and decrements it on every admitted recursive step, so that governed run
terminates in at most its initial fuel.  This establishes totality of the fuelled wrapper only.  Turing's result says
there is no total algorithm deciding, for every arbitrary program and input, whether the unbounded computation halts;
a finite wrapper neither decides that question nor proves nontermination when fuel expires.

A grid scan owns only its declared ordered finite cells.  It stops at the first adjacent pair whose read labels are
yes then no, or with \textsc{cells-exhausted} after the final cell.  Exhaustion is scoped to that grid, ordering,
predicate, and budget.  Continued-fraction count \(K\) is different again: \(K=3\) owns the canonical walls \(17/9\)
and \(2\), hence the jar \([129.6,137.7]\).  Neither grid cells nor recursion rungs may be substituted for \(K\).

A bounded live probe distinguishes \(\operatorname{some}(r)\), \(\operatorname{none}(\textit{scope})\), and
\(\operatorname{fallback}(r')\).  Resource failures such as effort, memory, arithmetic growth, or timeout are not
semantic failures such as invalid domain, false predicate, malformed result, or violated invariant.  Structural
outgrowth is not a live probe.  Given candidate \(x\), scaled reference \(r\), deadband or noise calibration
\(\delta\), and a declared orientation, its deterministic Boolean has the generic form
\[
 O_{r,\delta}(x)=
 [\,x\text{ exceeds the scaled reference }r\text{ under the declared deadband comparison}\,].
\]
The receipt retains \(x,r,\delta\), the scaling rule, calibration, orientation, comparison, and Boolean.  No metric or
symmetric comparison is implied without separate evidence.  The output cannot replace an observation, search result,
or live absence.

Stop predicates are checked both before and after each transition.  The stop grammar distinguishes
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{fuel}&F=0\text{ before another required transition},\\
\textsc{budget}&\text{a declared resource counter is exhausted},\\
\textsc{tolerance}&e(x)\leq\tau\text{ for a declared estimator and tolerance},\\
\textsc{bracket-width}&u-\ell\leq\omega\text{ for a declared width target},\\
\textsc{exact}&\text{an exact target condition has an executable resolver},\\
\textsc{domain}&\text{the next operation is outside its admitted domain}.
\end{array}
\]
Tolerance requires an estimator and a demonstrated implication relating it to error.  Bracket width is an interval fact, not point
accuracy.  Exact stop needs a resolved predicate.  Domain stop rejects an operation, not reports an approximation.

Every rule is tagged \emph{source-live} or \emph{proposed}.  Source-live rules govern the owned trace.  Proposed rules
describe an alternative experiment and cannot reinterpret the source-live result.  A new width target, extra fuel, or
changed live bound yields a separate route and certificate.  Structural and live rules also carry distinct tags and
cannot substitute for one another.

Several predicates can fire at one state.  Governance therefore declares a priority order or a combined-stop policy
before the run.  The receipt records every fired predicate, the selected stop, and a certificate that selection follows the
declared priority.  Recording only the selected reason erases a potentially material simultaneous stop.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: exact fuel traces (computation).}
Starting from \([1,2]\), read half selections give
\[
N=3:\ [15/8,2],\quad w=1/8;\qquad
N=4:\ [15/8,31/16],\quad w=1/16.
\]
These are distinct fuelled runs.  With width tolerance \(\varepsilon=1/10\), the post-transition check first fires
after rung four, because \(1/8>1/10\) and \(1/16\leq1/10\).

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: width is not tolerance (computation).}
Suppose \([\ell,u]\) has width \(10^{-6}\), while a claimed point estimate has no proved relation to the enclosed
target.  The width rule permits \textsc{bracket-width}, not a tolerance claim about that point.  Renaming the stop
would add an implication absent from the certificate.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: simultaneous stops (computation).}
Suppose rung four both exhausts the arithmetic budget and reaches width tolerance \(1/10\).  The fired set is
\(\{\textsc{budget},\textsc{tolerance}\}\).  If declared priority is semantic success before resource exhaustion, the
selected stop is \textsc{tolerance}; under the reverse priority it is \textsc{budget}.  Either certificate must retain
both fired predicates and verify the selected priority, rather than pretending the other event did not occur.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: deterministic recipe, nondeterministic input (computation).}
Two reruns share a transition grammar but receive different schedules or unordered streams.  They may produce
different valid traces.  Repeatability therefore does not mean identical results regardless of conditions.  It means
deterministic and declared nondeterministic inputs, versions, configurations, and rules are recorded so matching runs
can be compared and their certificates replayed.

\paragraph{Counterexample 5: finite fuel does not solve universal halting (computation).}
Wrapping every run in \(F=10^6\) guarantees the wrapper stops.  A fuel stop says only that the simulated process did
not finish within that fuel.  It neither decides whether the unbounded process eventually halts nor turns an
unresolved run into nontermination.

\paragraph{Descent experiment 6: the same bracket under three stop transforms (hand-worked proposed harness).}
This is a hand-worked proposal for a finite test harness, not an exported device computation or a new measurement.
Fix the exact state domain
\[
 \mathcal D=\{(L,U,Q,F):L,U\in\mathbb Z,\ Q\in\mathbb N_{>0},\ L<U,\ F\in\mathbb N\}
\]
and the initial state \(x_0=(1,2,1,3)\).  Let the crossing predicate be the harness function
\(C(z)=[z\leq13/8]\), with yes at the lower endpoint and no at the upper endpoint.  The transform

\[
 T_{\rm half}:\mathcal D\longrightarrow
 \mathcal D\times\{\mathsf{yes},\mathsf{no}\}
\]

forms the exact midpoint, reads \(C\), retains the oriented half, and decrements \(F\).  The proposed wrapper admits
it only while \(F>0\) and the order and opposed-answer preconditions pass.  Its three proposed stop transforms are

\[
 S_F(x)=[F=0],\qquad
 S_w(x)=[(U-L)/Q\leq1/4],\qquad
 S_e(x)=[13/8\in\{L/Q,U/Q\}],
\]

each with codomain \(\{\mathsf{stop},\mathsf{continue}\}\).  The hand-worked exact-arithmetic trace is

\[
 (1,2,1,3)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{yes}}(3,4,2,2)
 \xrightarrow{\mathsf{no}}(6,7,4,1)
 \xrightarrow{\mathsf{yes}}(13,14,8,0).
\]

At the second retained state the width transform says stop, while fuel and exact-endpoint transforms say continue.
At the third retained state all three say stop: width is \(1/8\), fuel is zero, and \(13/8\) is the lower endpoint.
Thus one trajectory can satisfy several stop predicates without making their reasons identical.

The commuting check separates transition from report.  Let \(\pi_B(L,U,Q,F)=(L/Q,U/Q)\), and let
\(H_b\) be half selection on interpreted rational brackets.  For each recorded branch \(b\), replay checks
\[
                     \pi_B(T_{\rm half}(x).1)=H_b(\pi_B(x)).
\]
The equality passes on every hand-worked row.  Projecting away \(F\) does not commute with the fuel-stop read because
\(S_F\) cannot be reconstructed from \(\pi_B(x)\); the residual is precisely the missing fuel field.

Two perturbations test that the stopping result is not an artifact of one report projection.  First change only the
initial fuel from \(3\) to \(2\), keeping predicate, arithmetic, and width target fixed.  The first two transition rows
replay byte for byte, but the hand-worked variant reaches \((6,7,4,0)\).  Width and fuel fire together there, while the exact-endpoint
predicate does not.  This proposed variant is not a prefix silently promoted to the former result: its governed input differs and
its selected stop has a different fired set.  Second keep fuel \(3\) but replace the width target \(1/4\) by \(1/10\).
The same hand-worked transition trace reaches the exact endpoint and exhausts fuel at width \(1/8\), but width no
longer fires.
The comparison residual is the Boolean vector
\[
 (S_F,S_w,S_e)_{\rm base}-(S_F,S_w,S_e)_{\rm perturbed},
\]
reported componentwise rather than arithmetically collapsed.  These perturbations preserve transition equality while
changing stop equality, demonstrating why a stop rule is part of process identity.  Reporting the same vector after
either change would show that a live input had been cached or projected away.

The transition identities and widths are hand-verified; the harness, predicate, targets, and stop policy remain
proposed rather than source-backed.  They license no empirical threshold, universal termination result, or live-device
claim.

The handoff is intentional: the canonical count-three route retains its owned jar \([129.6,137.7]\) at its source-live
stop.  Later proposed refinements may diagnose it under new budgets, but do not overwrite its trace, stop, or ownership.
