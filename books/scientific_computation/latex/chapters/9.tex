\chapter{The Corridor Rotates}

\section{Computational rotation}

A computational rotation is a permutation of a finite declared state set, not a geometric angle.  Let
\[
S=[s_0,\ldots,s_{m-1}],\qquad R:S\to S,
\]
where enumeration proves the listed states unique and evaluation proves \(R\) total and one-to-one.  The explicit
state record contains a variation label, an order index, a sign symbol, and any additional fields that affect the
transition.  Omitting a field creates only a projected transition.

Permutation composition is
\[
                    (R_2\circ R_1)(s)=R_2(R_1(s)),
\]
with identity \(\operatorname{id}(s)=s\).  Iteration is \(R^0=\operatorname{id}\) and
\(R^{n+1}=R\circ R^n\), hence \(R^{a+b}=R^a\circ R^b\).  The order of \(R\) is the least positive \(p\) satisfying
\(R^p=\operatorname{id}\) on every state in the declared domain.  A return of one state does not establish this
global order.

Variation is computed by applying \(R\) and comparing the full before-and-after states.  Order is computed by finite
iteration until identity is reached or the search budget ends.  A sign is a declared map
\(\sigma:S\to\{\mathsf{minus},\mathsf{zero},\mathsf{plus}\}\); it is a label, not arithmetic multiplication unless
operations on signs are separately defined.  Permutation parity is determined from a disjoint-cycle decomposition:
\[
                         \operatorname{parity}(R)=(-1)^{m-c},
\]
where \(c\) counts cycles including fixed points.  Alternatively, a certified transposition decomposition yields the
same parity.  Parity does not identify the permutation: many permutations share one parity bit.

If the disjoint-cycle lengths are \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_c\), including length \(1\) for fixed points, then
\[
                       \operatorname{ord}(R)=\operatorname{lcm}(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_c).
\]
The least common multiple returns every cycle because it is divisible by each \(\ell_i\).  Conversely, if
\(R^k=\operatorname{id}\) globally, then each cycle returns after \(k\), so every \(\ell_i\mid k\); hence their least
common multiple divides every global return exponent.

The numerical procedure traverses the state list, evaluates each image, checks range and uniqueness, builds inverse
and visited tables, slices the permutation into disjoint cycles, and accumulates cycle lengths and parity.  Iteration
then replays each cycle to verify closure and minimal length.  Projection tables for order, sign, or other labels are
kept separate from the full-state table.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: exact three-state rotation (computation).}
For
\[
\mathsf{minus}\mapsto\mathsf{zero}\mapsto\mathsf{plus}\mapsto\mathsf{minus},
\]
full-state replay gives \(R^3=\operatorname{id}\), while \(R\ne\operatorname{id}\) and
\(R^2\ne\operatorname{id}\).  Its order is exactly \(3\).  The single three-cycle has parity
\((-1)^{3-1}=+1\).  Exact return follows from the transition table, not from pigeonhole collision.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: label collision is not rotation order (computation).}
Let six full states rotate by \(R(i)=i+1\bmod6\), and project them by \(\beta(i)=i\bmod3\).  Labels repeat after
three steps, while full states return after six.  The compatibility
\(\beta(x)=\beta(y)\Rightarrow\beta(Rx)=\beta(Ry)\) defines a quotient-label rotation of order three; it does not
make the full permutation order three.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: composition order matters (computation).}
On \(\{0,1,2\}\), let \(A\) exchange \(0,1\) and let \(B\) exchange \(1,2\).  Then
\[
                   B\circ A=(0\ 2\ 1),\qquad A\circ B=(0\ 1\ 2).
\]
Both are three-cycles and both have even parity, but they are different permutations.  Equal order and parity do not
prove same process or equal state-by-state transition.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: same operation schema, different representations (computation).}
Two reports may both say rotate forward, while one enumerates states \((a,b,c)\) and another \((0,1,2)\).  Process
comparison requires a bijection \(J\) satisfying
\[
                              J\circ R_1=R_2\circ J.
\]
Without this checked conjugacy map, shared wording establishes neither same-number nor same-process.

\paragraph{Worked example 5: order and parity are different invariants (computation).}
A permutation with one disjoint two-cycle and one disjoint three-cycle has
\[
\operatorname{ord}(R)=\operatorname{lcm}(2,3)=6,
\qquad
\operatorname{parity}(R)=(-1)^{(2-1)+(3-1)}=-1.
\]
Its order is six and its parity is odd.  Neither invariant determines the other.

Budgets distinguish state enumeration, transition evaluations, comparison effort, table capacity, composition depth,
and replay effort.  Stops include \textsc{complete}, \textsc{enumeration-budget}, \textsc{transition-budget},
\textsc{out-of-domain}, \textsc{duplicate-state}, \textsc{non-total}, \textsc{non-injective},
\textsc{table-capacity}, \textsc{order-unresolved}, \textsc{parity-unresolved}, and \textsc{replay-failure}.  A fuel
stop certifies only the checked iterations.

A certificate records state schema and enumeration, equality, full transition table, projection maps, budgets,
visited and inverse tables, cycle slices, compositions, order candidates, proper-divisor checks, parity calculation,
stops, checked scope, and unresolved frontier.  Validation slices enumeration, totality, injectivity, transition,
composition, closure, minimal order, parity, projection compatibility, resources, and claims into pass, fail, or
unresolved rows.

Comparison follows the established grammar.  Exact state equality, equality under a declared projection, and process
equivalence are separate relations.  Process equivalence requires compatible inputs, a state map, a commuting
transition square, trace correspondence, cost conversion, configuration, and provenance.  Equal cycle length, parity,
or projected labels cannot replace those rows.

\section{Physical phase}

A physical phase is defined relative to a model and reference.  A complex amplitude
\[
                         z=Ae^{i\theta}=A(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta),
\qquad A\geq0,
\]
has phase \(\theta\) modulo \(2\pi\) when \(A>0\).  At \(A=0\), phase is undefined.  The complex plane requires
addition and multiplication satisfying their field laws, a distinguished unit \(i\) with \(i^2=-1\), and a reference
axis.  Two instances, two labels, or a Boolean pair do not construct that structure.

Operationally, phase is inferred by comparing a signal with a calibrated reference.  For quadrature observations
\[
                         I=A\cos\theta+\epsilon_I,\qquad
                         Q=A\sin\theta+\epsilon_Q,
\]
the principal estimate is
\[
                         \widehat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(Q,I)\in(-\pi,\pi].
\]
Its apparatus record includes detector response, reference oscillator, epoch, channel gains and offsets, sampling
clock, bandwidth, noise covariance, saturation limits, and environmental conditions.  A numerical angle without this
record is not a physical phase measurement.

Phase extraction first calibrates \(I,Q\), rejects or flags invalid samples, checks amplitude against a declared
noise floor, evaluates the quadrant-aware angle, and propagates uncertainty.  Away from the origin, first-order
propagation uses
\[
\nabla\theta=\frac{1}{I^2+Q^2}(-Q,I),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta)\approx
\nabla\theta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{IQ}\nabla\theta.
\]
Near \(I=Q=0\), this linearization is unstable and the phase row remains unresolved or uses a declared nonlinear
uncertainty construction.

For sampled phases \(\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_N\), unwrapping selects integers \(k_j\) and sets
\[
                         \widetilde\phi_j=\phi_j+2\pi k_j
\]
so adjacent changes satisfy a declared continuity bound.  Unwrapping is model-based: if the true change can exceed
the bound between samples, several lifts are compatible with the data.  The algorithm must retain principal phases,
chosen integers, rejected alternatives, continuity threshold, and unresolved branch points.

A holonomy is a round-trip quantity defined by a connection and path, not by a repeated label.  In an Abelian model,
\[
                         H(\gamma)=\exp\!\left(i\oint_\gamma A_\mu\,dx^\mu\right).
\]
For a partition \(t_0<\cdots<t_N\) of path \(\gamma\), a finite approximation is
\[
H_N=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
\exp\!\left(i\,A_\mu(\gamma(t_j))[\gamma^\mu(t_{j+1})-\gamma^\mu(t_j)]\right).
\]
The certificate records path orientation, connection units and gauge convention, quadrature rule, samples, products,
mesh width, and error model.  Refinement compares \(H_N,H_{2N}\) in a declared metric; agreement at tolerance is
numerical convergence evidence, not an exact integral absent a demonstrated error bound.

For matrix-valued transport, factor order matters:
\[
                         H_N=U_{N-1}\cdots U_1U_0.
\]
Changing path order or multiplying factors as if they commute changes the model.  Numerical stabilization may
renormalize factors or use structure-preserving products, but every such projection and its error must be reported.

Spinor return is another model-specific phase statement.  For a spin-\(\tfrac12\) representation,
\[
                         U(\varphi)=\exp(-i\varphi\,\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2),
\qquad U(2\pi)=-I,\qquad U(4\pi)=I.
\]
The spinor vector changes sign after \(2\pi\) and returns exactly as a vector after \(4\pi\).  The ray is already
unchanged after \(2\pi\), because \([\psi]=[-\psi]\) for an isolated pure state.  The minus sign is observable only
as a relative phase against another path or reference in an interference or equivalent apparatus; a global sign by
itself is not a detector reading.  These statements are scoped to the declared spin-\(\tfrac12\) representation and
apparatus.  They do not make \(4\pi\) a universal period of physical states, and they are not inferred from a
three-state computational permutation.

\paragraph{Worked measurement 1: calibrated quadrature phase (physics).}
After gain and offset calibration, suppose \(I=3\), \(Q=4\) in common amplitude units.  Then
\(\widehat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(4,3)\).  Repeating the arithmetic exactly verifies the numerical estimate.
Physical certification additionally requires the reference epoch, calibration covariance, amplitude validity, and
uncertainty interval.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: two labels are not a complex plane.}
Calling two states real and imaginary supplies neither addition, multiplication, distributivity, nor an element whose
square is \(-1\).  A transition exchanging the labels is a permutation of order two.  It is not multiplication by
\(i\) until a complex representation and a checked intertwining map are supplied.

\paragraph{Worked measurement 3: discretized loop refinement (physics).}
Compute \(H_N,H_{2N},H_{4N}\) on nested path partitions and compare their principal phase differences.  If successive
differences fall below tolerance while a discretization bound decreases with mesh width, the reported uncertainty
can include truncation and sampling errors.  Equal formatted phases alone are only resolution equivalence.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: computational period three is not physical phase.}
An exact permutation
\(\mathsf{minus}\mapsto\mathsf{zero}\mapsto\mathsf{plus}\mapsto\mathsf{minus}\)
has order three.  It specifies no complex amplitude, connection, path, apparatus, clock, or uncertainty.  Mapping its
labels to angles \(0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3\) is a posited representation; only a checked transition map supports that
numerical encoding, and no physical identification follows.

Budgets distinguish acquired samples, calibration effort, phase-extraction arithmetic, unwrap branches, path
segments, matrix growth, refinement levels, and uncertainty computation.  Stops include \textsc{complete},
\textsc{missing-reference}, \textsc{missing-calibration}, \textsc{below-amplitude-floor}, \textsc{saturation},
\textsc{unit-mismatch}, \textsc{unwrap-ambiguous}, \textsc{segment-budget}, \textsc{refinement-budget},
\textsc{nonconvergent}, \textsc{overflow}, and \textsc{model-failure}.  A resource stop does not become a physical
zero or an absence of phase.

Comparison reports three value rows separately.  Exact same-number means equality after typed conversion.
Resolution equivalence means equal labels under a declared quantizer.  Uncertainty compatibility means overlap of
declared phase regions, with circular topology respected.  Each independently passes, fails, or remains unresolved.
Same-process additionally requires matching apparatus inputs, calibration, sampling, numerical transitions, traces,
costs, configuration, and provenance.

The certificate records apparatus and model, complex representation, units, references and epochs, raw and rejected
samples, calibration, covariance, principal phases, unwrap integers and alternatives, path and connection samples,
ordered products, refinements, convergence and error bounds, uncertainty regions, budgets, stops, checked scope,
unresolved frontier, protocol, run identity, platform, and deterministic and declared nondeterministic inputs.

Governance fixes physical model, apparatus, complex and spin representations, reference, gauge convention, sampling,
calibration, extraction, unwrapping, path quadrature, product order, convergence rule, uncertainty method, budgets,
stop priority, and certificate grammar.  A changed field starts a new report.

The action ledger is exact.  Apparatus, model, representation, reference, calibration, path, connection, algorithms,
uncertainty, budgets, and certificate grammar are \emph{posited}.  Complex identities, conditional extraction and
propagation formulas, unwrap implications, ordered-product identities, discretization bounds, spinor representation
identities, and validation implications are \emph{demonstrated}.  Samples, calibration observations, quadratures,
phases, unwrap choices, products, convergence rows, uncertainties, stops, and provenance are \emph{read}.  A complex
plane from two labels, physical phase from a permutation, exact holonomy from unbounded numerical refinement,
independence from differently named routes, a five-quantity interpretation of one residue, and any uncalibrated
physical identification remain \emph{reserved}.
\section{Resonance audit}

Resonance is a prompt for comparison, not evidence.  The computational report contains a finite state set,
permutation, compositions, traces, and costs.  The physical report contains complex amplitudes, references, apparatus,
calibration, path transport, uncertainty, and observables.  An audit asks which precise maps, if any, connect them.

A typed representation has the form
\[
\rho:S_{\rm comp}\to V_{\rm phys},
\]
where \(V_{\rm phys}\) belongs to a declared physical model.  For a computational transition \(R\) and physical
transport \(U\), one-step compatibility requires
\[
                         \rho\circ R=U\circ\rho.
\]
Same-process is stronger than this value square.  It requires compatible inputs, composition laws, invariants,
state and trace relations, cost or resource correspondence, configuration, apparatus, calibration, and provenance.
If only a projected label commutes, the result is a projected comparison, not full process equivalence.

An invariant also needs a declared read \(r\).  Computational preservation is
\[
                         r(Rs)=r(s)\quad\text{for all checked }s.
\]
A return \(R^p(s)=s\) implies \(r(R^p(s))=r(s)\), but not one-step preservation.  Without \(r\) and the displayed
law, no residue invariance has been established.

\paragraph{Audit 1: relabeling versus angular momentum.}
A finite relabeling is a permutation.  Angular momentum is a physical observable and generator within a continuous
symmetry model, with units, dynamics, apparatus, and calibration.  A discrete permutation does not construct a
continuous one-parameter group, differentiable action, invariant physical action, or conserved observable.
Consequently relabeling is not angular momentum, and a permutation does not invoke a Noether-type conservation law
without the missing physical structure and demonstrated hypotheses.

\paragraph{Audit 2: order-three versus phase.}
The exact three-state rotation has \(R^3=\operatorname{id}\).  Mapping its states to
\(1,e^{2\pi i/3},e^{4\pi i/3}\) gives a numerical representation only after \(\rho\) is posited and the intertwining
square is checked.  That square still supplies no oscillator, path, connection, detector, or uncertainty.  Equal
order therefore does not license physical phase or holonomy.

\paragraph{Audit 3: same number through two numerical routes.}
Let two independently specified refinement routes produce \(y_h\) and \(z_h\) in a common typed domain.  The audit
reports three rows:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{exact equality}&y_h=z_h,\\
\text{resolution equality}&q_\theta(y_h)=q_\theta(z_h),\\
\text{uncertainty compatibility}&\mathcal U(y_h)\cap\mathcal U(z_h)\ne\varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
Each passes, fails, or remains unresolved.  A combined agreement flag must not erase which relation passed.
Agreement of values does not establish same-process.

                         p_{\rm obs}=\log_2\frac{|D_h|}{|D_{h/2}|}.
\]
Observed order must stabilize across further refinements from the same family, while raw differences decrease and a
demonstrated error bound remains valid.  Choosing \(p\) to make an extrapolate hit a target is rejected.  Under the
displayed expansion, extrapolation cancels the leading term and leaves a demonstrated \(O(h^{p+1})\) remainder here;
it does not produce an exact limit.  Nearby values alone do not justify extrapolation, and a route with different
physics or preprocessing is a comparison channel, not automatically a refinement level.

The finite comparison algorithm parses both reports, validates types and units, resolves representation maps,
replays transitions or transport products, checks commuting squares and invariants, aligns refinement levels, computes
exact differences, quantized labels, uncertainty intersections, and---when admissible---Richardson estimates and
observed convergence ratios.  It retains rejected alignments and alternative maps.

\paragraph{Worked example 4: a failed intertwining map.}
Suppose \(R\) cycles three computational states, while proposed \(U\) fixes all represented vectors.  If
\(\rho\) distinguishes the states, then \(\rho(Rs)\ne U(\rho(s))\) for some \(s\).  The representation row fails even
though \(U^3=I\) and \(R^3=\operatorname{id}\).  Equal return exponents alone do not make the processes correspond.

\paragraph{Counterexample 5: apparent Richardson convergence outside scope.}
Three displayed values can accidentally fit a power-law ratio.  Without nested meshes, common target, stable boundary
conditions, and a demonstrated truncation expansion, extrapolation manufactures precision.  The audit reports the
ratio as read data and leaves the extrapolated-limit claim unresolved or rejected.
