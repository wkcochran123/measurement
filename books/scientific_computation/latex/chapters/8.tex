\chapter{The Electron Is Named}

\section{Partition and collision}

Let the finite domain be an explicit list
\[
X=[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}],
\]
and let \(B=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) be a declared set of boxes.  A total box map
\(\beta:X\to B\) induces cells
\[
                         C_j=\{x\in X:\beta(x)=j\}.
\]
The nonempty cells form a partition of \(X\): each listed input belongs to exactly one cell, and distinct cells are
disjoint.  This conclusion depends on total, single-valued evaluation of \(\beta\), not on names attached afterward.

The finite pigeonhole statement is exact:
\[
 n>m\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists\,i<k<n,\ \beta(x_i)=\beta(x_k).
\]
It guarantees some colliding pair.  It does not select which pair, prove that a proposed pair collides, identify the
underlying inputs, or create a state transition.  In particular, a collision does not imply a cycle.  Exact
three-cycle closure is a different claim with a transition map and belongs to the next section.

The numerical algorithm traverses \(X\) in its declared order and maintains a visited table
\[
                       V[j]=\operatorname{none}
                       \quad\text{or}\quad\operatorname{some}(i).
\]
At index \(i\), evaluate \(j=\beta(x_i)\).  If \(V[j]\) is empty, store \(i\); otherwise return the witness pair
\((V[j],i)\).  The table slices prior inputs by box while retaining only the first index per box.  A full certificate
also keeps the input list and all evaluated labels, because the table projection erases later members of each fiber.

Budgets are explicit: input fuel bounds visited indices, map effort bounds evaluation of \(\beta\), table capacity
bounds boxes, and arithmetic bounds label calculation.  Stops include \textsc{collision}, \textsc{domain-exhausted},
\textsc{input-budget}, \textsc{map-budget}, \textsc{invalid-box}, \textsc{table-capacity},
\textsc{map-failure}, and \textsc{overflow}.  Exhaustion without collision proves injectivity only on the traversed
finite list and declared map.  A budget stop leaves the unvisited suffix as an unresolved frontier.

A replay certificate records \(X,B,\beta\)'s declared graph or evaluation rule, traversal order, budgets, every
label, visited-table update, collision witness or exhaustion stop, checked prefix, and unresolved suffix.  Validation
is sliced into domain, box-range, map, table-update, distinct-index, equal-label, count, stop, and claim rows, each
pass, fail, or unresolved.  The existential result licenses the collision conclusion only after finiteness,
totality, input uniqueness, and \(n>m\) rows pass.

\paragraph{Live neutral map: a specific three-to-two collision (computation).}
Let
\[
D=\{\mathsf{base},\mathsf{first\ variation},\mathsf{second\ variation}\},
\qquad B=\{0,1\},
\]
with \(\beta(\mathsf{base})=0\) and
\(\beta(\mathsf{first\ variation})=\beta(\mathsf{second\ variation})=1\).  Its fibers are
\[
C_0=\{\mathsf{base}\},\qquad
C_1=\{\mathsf{first\ variation},\mathsf{second\ variation}\}.
\]
The two variations are therefore a specifically evaluated collision.  The box count \(2\) is scoped to this declared
domain and map.  An arbitrary three-to-two map guarantees some collision but privileges no pair.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: a witnessed collision (computation).}
Let \(X=[10,11,12,13]\), \(B=\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\beta(x)=x\bmod3\).  Labels are
\([1,2,0,1]\).  Traversal returns indices \(0\) and \(3\), since both map to box \(1\).  The witness certifies equal
labels and distinct inputs \(10\ne13\); it does not certify equality of the integers.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: existence does not choose the advertised pair (computation).}
With the same map, pigeonhole guarantees a collision because \(4>3\).  It does not imply that \(11\) and \(12\)
collide: their labels are \(2\) and \(0\).  A selected-pair claim requires its own two map evaluations.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: projection fibers (computation).}
For \(X=[0,1,2,3,4,5]\) and \(\beta(x)=x\bmod2\), the fibers are
\[
                         C_0=\{0,2,4\},\qquad C_1=\{1,3,5\}.
\]
Keeping only labels \([0,1]\) records occupied boxes but erases multiplicity, members, order, and collision witnesses.
The projected alphabet cannot reconstruct the partition.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: repeated label is not a cycle (computation).}
The sequence of independently mapped inputs may have labels \(0,1,0\).  This is a collision in a projection.
Without a state transition \(T\) and checked equations \(T(x_i)=x_{i+1}\), it says nothing about return, period, or
dynamics.

\paragraph{Counterexample 5: an incomplete traversal (computation).}
Suppose \(n=5\), \(m=3\), but input fuel permits only two evaluations with distinct labels.  The mathematical finite
data still satisfy \(n>m\), yet the traversal has not produced a witness because the remaining map values were not
evaluated.  Its report owns two rows and an unresolved suffix; it must not fabricate a pair.

\paragraph{Counterexample 6: distinct indices are not necessarily distinct terms (computation).}
For \([a,a,b]\), indices \(0\ne1\) do not witness distinct domain terms.  Without an input-uniqueness certificate,
returning them proves only repeated occurrence.  The traversal stops with \textsc{invalid-domain}, or applies a
declared provenance-preserving deduplication and restarts.  Silent deduplication changes \(n\) and may invalidate
\(n>m\).

Finite traversal needs no axiom of choice: its declared order returns the first encountered pair.  Existence itself
supplies no canonical pair.  A constant map on three distinct terms has several colliding pairs, and another traversal
order can select another witness without changing the existential result.

The physics fence is strict.  A finite computational collision is not particle identity, indistinguishability,
degeneracy, recurrence, conservation, or a detector event.  A physical use requires an apparatus, observable, units,
calibration, sampling model, uncertainty, and a tested map from observations to boxes.

\section{A collision is not a cycle}

Let \(S\) be a declared state space and \(T:S\to S\) a deterministic total transition on the checked domain.  A cycle
certificate for initial state \(s_0\) and period \(p>0\) requires
\[
T^p(s_0)=s_0,
\]
and a least-period certificate also requires \(T^k(s_0)\ne s_0\) for every \(0<k<p\).  These are full-state
equalities.  If a box projection \(\beta:S\to B\) merely satisfies
\(\beta(T^i(s_0))=\beta(T^j(s_0))\), it has found a label collision.  Unless the projection is proved to reflect
state equality and to respect transitions, that collision supplies no cycle.

The compatibility condition
\[
 \beta(x)=\beta(y)\Longrightarrow\beta(Tx)=\beta(Ty)
\]
defines a transition on labels.  It can certify a quotient-label cycle, but still does not reflect full-state closure.
For example, let \(S=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\), \(T(x)=x+1\bmod6\), and
\(\beta(x)=x\bmod3\).  Labels have period \(3\), whereas every full state has period \(6\).

The visited-table algorithm starts with \(s_0\), stores each full state with its first index, and repeatedly evaluates
\(s_{i+1}=T(s_i)\).  On a previously visited state \(s_j=s_{i+1}\), it reports preperiod \(j\) and period
\(p=i+1-j\).  Least period is checked by replaying the segment and testing all proper positive divisors of \(p\), or
all \(1\leq k<p\) when no divisor optimization is certified.  A memory-bounded alternative advances one state by one
step and another by two; a meeting proves an eventual repetition only under deterministic total transition and still
requires a second pass to recover preperiod, period, and trace evidence.

Transition fuel and table capacity bound only the checked prefix; exhaustion does not prove absence of a later cycle.

If the checked state space has known cardinality \(|S|=M\) and \(T:S\to S\) is total and deterministic, then the
\(M+1\) states \(s_0,\ldots,s_M\) force a repeated full state.  Without that finite bound, fuel alone guarantees only
termination of the bounded search, not that a repetition will be found.

\paragraph{Counterexample 1: repeated boxes without repeated states (computation).}
Let \(S=\{a,b,c,d\}\), \(T(a)=b\), \(T(b)=c\), \(T(c)=d\), \(T(d)=d\), and let
\(\beta(a)=\beta(c)=0\), \(\beta(b)=\beta(d)=1\).  The label trace from \(a\) is
\(0,1,0,1,1,1,\ldots\), so the early \(0\) collision occurs at \(a\ne c\), while the eventual full-state cycle is
the fixed point \(d\).  Projected period two is false as a full-state claim.

\paragraph{Worked example 2: exact neutral three-cycle (computation).}
For
\[
S=\{\mathsf{minus},\mathsf{zero},\mathsf{plus}\},
\qquad
\mathsf{minus}\mapsto\mathsf{zero}\mapsto\mathsf{plus}\mapsto\mathsf{minus},
\]
finite replay proves \(T^3(s)=s\) for every state.  The same table proves \(T(s)\ne s\) and \(T^2(s)\ne s\), so the
least period is exactly \(3\).  This is a transition certificate independent of the three-to-two pigeonhole result in
the preceding section.

For any read \(r:S\to R\), the return identity gives \(r(T^3s)=r(s)\).  It does not give one-step invariance
\(r(Ts)=r(s)\).  In particular, no residue or \(\alpha\) invariance, and no causal explanation of either, follows
without a separately stated read map and preservation law.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: a tail entering a cycle (computation).}
Let \(0\mapsto1\mapsto2\mapsto3\mapsto2\).  From \(0\), the first repeated full state is \(2\), at indices \(2\) and
\(4\).  The preperiod is \(2\), the period is \(2\), and the cycle is \(2\leftrightarrow3\).  Reporting period four
from the return time alone would confuse prefix length with cycle length.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: nondeterministic compatibility failure (computation).}
If state \(u\) may transition to either \(v\) or \(w\), repeated runs can diverge.  A recorded path can still be
replayed as a finite path, but it is not a certificate for a deterministic transition function or for all possible
future paths.  A schedule or choice stream must become part of the full state before deterministic cycle reasoning
applies.

\paragraph{Counterexample 5: finite search does not decide universal halting (computation).}
Fuel \(N\) makes this cycle search terminate and permits \textsc{fuel} when no repetition has appeared.  It does not
decide whether an arbitrary unbounded computation will halt or repeat.  The universal halting problem remains outside
the finite certificate; only the enumerated transition prefix is owned.

\section{When a physical name is licensed}

Neither a collision nor a cycle licenses a physical name.  A collision certifies that distinct domain terms share a
box under a declared projection.  A cycle certifies return under a declared full-state transition.  A physical claim
requires a third structure:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L=(&\text{system},\text{apparatus},\text{observable},\text{model},\text{units},\\
            &\text{calibration},\text{sampling},\text{uncertainty},\text{alternatives},\text{provenance}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The bridge from a computational report to \(\mathcal L\) must be stated, calibrated, and tested.  Finiteness,
repeatability, and word resemblance cannot supply it.

The four-name grammar is useful only because it prevents coercion:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\operatorname{nowtrino}(H)&=\text{current computational projection after history slicing},\\
\operatorname{newtrino}(s)&=U(s),\text{ the next computational state under declared update }U,\\
\operatorname{naotrino}&=\text{physical candidate; manuscript coinage from Greek \emph{naos}, temple},\\
\nu\text{-trino}&=\text{the standard neutrino-physics register}.
\end{array}
\]
After state representation and update are declared, transition supplies
\[
                    \operatorname{nowtrino}(H)\xrightarrow{\,U\,}\operatorname{newtrino}(s).
\]
This is a computational edge.  It does not reach naotrino or \(\nu\)-trino.  A naotrino claim needs a concrete
apparatus, model, observables, units, calibration, uncertainty, and discriminating data.  A standard neutrino claim
must use the established physical model and observables appropriate to that register.  Similar spelling is no map.

A candidate bridge has typed form
\[
\mathrm{Input}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Report}\xrightarrow{\kappa}C
\xleftarrow{\lambda}\mathrm{Observation},
\]
where \(\kappa\) converts a computational report into comparison domain \(C\), and \(\lambda\) converts calibrated
observations into the same domain.  Before notation is shared, three value relations remain distinct:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{exact same-number}&\kappa(R)=\lambda(O),\\
\text{resolution equivalence}&q_\theta(\kappa(R))=q_\theta(\lambda(O)),\\
\text{uncertainty compatibility}&\mathcal U_R\cap\mathcal U_O\ne\varnothing
\text{ under a declared region model}.
\end{array}
\]
Same-number is reserved for exact equality after typed conversion.  None of these relations implies same-process or
physical identity.

The two-pass calibration guards against a name being planted by one route.  A direct acquisition and a stepped
acquisition through an intermediate produce reports \(R_d,R_s\).  Their raw values, uncertainty regions, routes,
budgets, and provenance are retained.  The comparison reports three rows:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{exact equality}&\kappa_d(R_d)=\kappa_s(R_s),\\
\text{resolution equality}&q_\theta(\kappa_d(R_d))=q_\theta(\kappa_s(R_s)),\\
\text{uncertainty-region overlap}&\mathcal U_d\cap\mathcal U_s\ne\varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
Each row independently passes, fails, or remains unresolved.  No combined agreement bit may erase which relation
passed.  Passing any row does not prove route independence, apparatus correctness, or a physical mechanism unless
those hypotheses receive separate tests.

\paragraph{Worked example 1: a licensed computational transition.}
Let a full state be \(s=(x,c,\sigma)\), and let \(U\) update all three fields.  The current summary
\(\operatorname{nowtrino}(H)=s\) and next state \(\operatorname{newtrino}(s)=U(s)\) are connected by replaying \(U\).
If history slicing erased the prior trace, the edge certifies this one update only.  Calling either endpoint a
physical particle would be a type and evidence error.

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: collision plus cycle still lacks apparatus.}
Three computational states may map into two boxes, and the states may also rotate with least period three.  These are
valid finite certificates for projection and transition.  They specify no detector, physical observable, unit,
calibration, noise law, or uncertainty.  Their conjunction therefore licenses no naotrino or standard neutrino claim.

\paragraph{Worked example 3: interval compatibility at uncertainty.}
Suppose two calibrated channels report intervals \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) in the same unit, with a preregistered rule that
accepts when \(I_1\cap I_2\ne\varnothing\).  An observed overlap passes the interval-overlap, or
compatible-at-uncertainty, row.  It is not an exact same-number result.  Shared
calibration or preprocessing may correlate the channels, so covariance and cross-covariance remain in the report.
Overlap does not certify independent evidence or equal acquisition processes.

\paragraph{Counterexample 4: same numeral in incompatible domains.}
A computational summary and a detector fit both display \(3\).  One counts transitions; the other estimates an energy
in declared units.  Without \(\kappa,\lambda\), unit conversion, uncertainty, and model checks, equality of printed
numerals is not even a same-number claim in a common domain.

\paragraph{Descent experiment 8: a name survives a transform only at its declared resolution (hand-worked proposed harness).}
This is a hand-worked synthetic naming proposal, not an exported computation or apparatus observation.  Let the full
state domain be
\[
 S=\{(i,j):i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z,\ j\in\{0,1\}\},
\]
let \(T:S\to S\) be \(T(i,j)=(i+1\bmod6,1-j)\), and let the current-summary transform be
\(\pi(i,j)=i\bmod3\) with codomain \(B=\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\).  The summary update is
\(\overline T(b)=b+1\bmod3\).  Domain, codomain, transition, projection, and update are fixed before the table is
evaluated.  Hand calculation verifies the proposed commuting square
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 S&\xrightarrow{T}&S\\
 \pi\downarrow&&\downarrow\pi\\
 B&\xrightarrow{\overline T}&B
 \end{array}
 \qquad\text{that is,}\qquad \pi\circ T=\overline T\circ\pi.
\]

From \(s_0=(0,0)\), the full trace is
\((0,0),(1,1),(2,0),(3,1),(4,0),(5,1),(0,0)\).  The projected trace is
\(0,1,2,0,1,2,0\).  Thus the projected current name returns after three transitions, whereas the full state returns
only after six.  The transform preserves enough structure to license the statement that the \emph{summary update}
has period three; it does not license period three for the full process.  The failed lifting residual at step three
is the erased state difference \((3,1)\ne(0,0)\), even though both project to \(0\).

The hand check retains all six full transitions and seven projected labels, verifies the square on the finite fibers,
and checks the two periods separately.  A projected collision is never upgraded to a full-state return.

An adversarial projection shows why commutation, not alphabet size, licenses the summary update.  Replace \(\pi\) by
\(\pi'(i,j)=i+j\bmod3\) while retaining the advertised \(\overline T(b)=b+1\bmod3\).  Then
\[
 \pi'(T(i,j))=i+2-j\pmod3,\qquad
 \overline T(\pi'(i,j))=i+j+1\pmod3.
\]
For \(j=0\) these differ, so a proposed checker would classify the first checked fiber
\textsc{noncommuting}.  The new projection
still has three labels, still produces collisions, and may still display repeating strings.  None repairs the failed
transition relation.  One may derive a different quotient transition only if it is well-defined on every
\(\pi'\)-fiber; choosing a rule after seeing the desired trace would be fitting the name.

The two harnesses form a controlled comparison.  Their full \(T\), state domain, fuel, and equality are identical;
only the projection record changes.  The residual is localized to the summary-transition row.  This is the precise
sense in which the name depends on resolution: each reading owns only the dynamics its projection actually preserves.
Recording the failed alternative prevents the successful square from being mistaken for an automatic property of any
three-box naming scheme.

If label \(0\) is called nowtrino in this harness, that name belongs only to the governed projection row
\((\text{name},S,B,\pi,\text{version})\).  The next label under \(\overline T\) may be called newtrino because its
update square is hand-verified.  Both periods and the square are finite hand calculations; harness and names remain
proposed.  Successful quotient dynamics licenses no particle or physical recurrence: that would require an apparatus
record, units, calibration, observations, uncertainty, and a tested bridge.
