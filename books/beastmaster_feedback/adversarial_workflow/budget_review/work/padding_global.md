# Capped Global Padding Scout Report

Scope: cross-book repetition and AI-tell padding risks only, across
`books/expository_monograph/latex/chapters/01.tex` through `32.tex`. No
manuscript edits. Pattern cap: 8.

## Global Verdict

The budget itself is not forcing global bloat. Revision 4's act-relative parity
mostly holds: the ascent has room to build, and the descent is leaner. The
padding risk is cross-book cadence: repeated claim fences, repeated recap closes,
reused bridge language, and symmetrical sentence habits that make honest
qualification sound templated.

## Recurring Patterns

1. Universal recap/accounting close.
   Nearly every chapter ends with the same audit rhythm: "What is demonstrated"
   followed by "What is assumed." Useful for integrity, but globally it becomes
   a predictable end-stop.
   Anchors: Ch1 `01.tex:213`, `01.tex:257`; Ch8 `08.tex:168`, `08.tex:202`;
   Ch19 `19.tex:166`, `19.tex:195`; Ch30 `30.tex:135`, `30.tex:160`; Ch32
   `32.tex:139`, `32.tex:163`.

2. Stacked endings: audit, note, assumption, handoff.
   Several chapters close more than once: a demonstrated/assumed section, an
   indented "A note on..." qualification, then a final next-chapter bridge. This
   is the most visible padding architecture.
   Anchors: Ch1 `01.tex:247`, `01.tex:257`, `01.tex:264`; Ch26 `26.tex:146`,
   `26.tex:159`, `26.tex:172`; Ch32 `32.tex:151`, `32.tex:163`, `32.tex:165`.

3. Repeated claim fences around physical names.
   The manuscript often says the construction proves a finite shape, not the
   full physical theory. That caution is necessary, but the same fence recurs
   enough to become a tell.
   Anchors: Ch20 `20.tex:9`, `20.tex:129`; Ch21 `21.tex:10`, `21.tex:42`,
   `21.tex:108`; Ch26 `26.tex:5`, `26.tex:60`, `26.tex:78`, `26.tex:91`;
   Ch30 `30.tex:114`.

4. Repeated "A note on..." bridge paragraphs.
   Almost every chapter has a note separating formal result from interpretation:
   labels, bridge, identification, convention, borrowed name. The move is honest
   but too uniform.
   Anchors: Ch1 `01.tex:247`; Ch2 `02.tex:216`; Ch3 `03.tex:202`; Ch5
   `05.tex:135`, `05.tex:194`; Ch19 `19.tex:180`; Ch20 `20.tex:153`; Ch29
   `29.tex:160`; Ch31 `31.tex:147`.

5. Overused "It is worth..." transition.
   The phrase and nearby variants ("worth marking," "deserves," "repays") often
   announce importance before stating it. Across the book it creates a
   tutorial-like cadence.
   Anchors: Ch1 `01.tex:40`, `01.tex:160`; Ch5 `05.tex:49`; Ch12 `12.tex:75`,
   `12.tex:132`; Ch18 `18.tex:40`, `18.tex:126`; Ch24 `24.tex:63`; Ch31
   `31.tex:79`.

6. Roadmap openings that repeat the same chapter-entry move.
   Many openings recap the previous chapter, announce the present chapter's job,
   and foreshadow the next turn. Good locally; repetitive globally.
   Anchors: Ch2 `02.tex:4`; Ch3 `03.tex:4`; Ch6 `06.tex:4`; Ch7 `07.tex:4`;
   Ch8 `08.tex:4`; Ch29 `29.tex:4`; Ch30 `30.tex:4`; Ch32 `32.tex:4`.

7. Artificial symmetry and generic certainty.
   The book repeatedly uses contrastive balances: "not X but Y," "rather than,"
   "one thing / two sides," "nothing more." These often mark real distinctions,
   but in clusters they sound over-shaped.
   Anchors: Ch1 `01.tex:60`, `01.tex:185`; Ch3 `03.tex:47`, `03.tex:124`;
   Ch5 `05.tex:41`; Ch10 `10.tex:107`; Ch24 `24.tex:140`; Ch30 `30.tex:151`.

8. Chapters that may be compressed rather than lean.
   The descent should read lean, but a few chapters pack several bridges into
   very little space. This is not padding; it is the opposite risk: compression
   disguised as budget discipline.
   Anchors: Ch20 geometric program / boundary generator / gauge bridge
   `20.tex:4`, `20.tex:69`, `20.tex:116`; Ch27 Godel sentence / boundary
   obstruction / nonlocality analogy `27.tex:4`, `27.tex:38`, `27.tex:82`,
   `27.tex:118`; Ch30 baseline-relative antimatter / holonomy / cosmological
   asymmetry `30.tex:4`, `30.tex:49`, `30.tex:89`, `30.tex:114`; Ch31
   bit-vector / XOR-popcount / closure `31.tex:4`, `31.tex:79`, `31.tex:103`,
   `31.tex:133`.

## Compact Actions

- Keep the act-relative budget. Do not add prose to use the unused ceiling.
- Vary or collapse some demonstrated/assumed closes where the chapter already
  has a separate note.
- Use one strong fence per heavy-physics chapter instead of repeating the fence
  before each label.
- Cut low-information "It is worth..." openers when the following sentence
  already carries the emphasis.
- Check Ch20, Ch27, Ch30, and Ch31 for missing reader bridges before trimming
  them further.
