# Chapter 00 — The Machine

## The instrument we are standing inside

The Preface made a promise: disclose the apparatus once, then fall silent about it and speak only mathematics. This chapter is the disclosure. After it the machine goes offstage, and when the book later says the construction *selects* a sign or *pools* a family of motions, the reader will know exactly which mechanism is meant without our having to point at it again.

The object of study is a development written inside a proof assistant. The organ of a proof assistant is its *elaborator*: the component that reads a written declaration and decides whether it goes through — whether the symbols presented to it cohere into something the system will accept. We treat that elaborator as a physical measuring instrument. This is the whole of the stance, and it is meant literally. When the source speaks of *measuring how true `true = true` is*, it is not making a joke about tautology. It is asking an empirical question about a particular device: presented with this exact claim, what does the elaborator do, how much work does deciding it cost, and what does the act of deciding leave behind?

Cost is not a metaphor here. Deciding a declaration takes the elaborator a definite amount of labor, and the development tracks it: nearly every declaration in the source carries a rough estimate of how hard the machine had to work to admit it — a decision-cost tag, an integer that rises with the effort. A claim the instrument settles instantly and one that nearly exhausts it are both, in the abstract, true; they are not the same measurement, any more than a reading taken off a dial is the same event as one wrung from a long observation. The development is, on this reading, a long sequence of such measurements, arranged so that the records left by the early ones are the raw material of the later ones.

## A Fact: a claim with its decision attached

Everything rests on a single primitive, and it is worth stating in the formalization vocabulary we are permitted here and nowhere else. The construction never works with a bare proposition. It works with a **Fact**: a proposition bundled together with a procedure that decides it. In the proof assistant a Fact is a `class` carrying two fields — a proposition, `truth : Prop`, the claim itself; and `decTruth : Decidable truth`, a procedure that returns a verdict for that claim. The two travel together. A proposition with no decision procedure is not a Fact; a decision procedure with nothing to decide is not one either.

The canonical instance is the Fact whose proposition is `true = true` and whose decision procedure answers, without hesitation, *yes*. This is the atom from which the entire tower is built — no numbers assumed, no set theory imported, nothing underneath it. A Fact, then, is not "something true." It is "something whose truth-value can be produced on demand." That distinction is the hinge of the construction, and it is the reason the elaborator-as-instrument stance is more than a conceit: a Fact is precisely a claim that comes with the means of measuring it, and to measure it is to run the procedure and read the verdict.

A word on the decision procedure, because the whole apparatus turns on it. To be `Decidable` is not a promise that the claim is true; it is a promise that the machine can *find out* — that it will return one of two verdicts, yes or no, in finite work, without ever stalling in some third, undecided state. Some Facts answer for free, by mere inspection of their form; others make the elaborator labor, and the decision-cost tag on the declaration is the record of that labor. Bundling the verdict with the claim is what makes the construction constructive: nothing in it is ever merely true; everything is *decided*, and the cost of deciding is itself a quantity the book can read. A mathematics that admitted claims with no procedure behind them would leave the instrument with nothing to measure.

## Two verbs: to tange, and to funge

Once the machine can decide a characteristic — once it holds a procedure that answers *does this object have property C?* — there are exactly two things to do with that power, and the book leans on both so heavily that they earn their own verbs.

To **tange** an object is to select it by a characteristic: to single it out as the one bearing the property, to separate the bearers from the rest. To **funge** an object is to drop it into the bag of all objects sharing that characteristic: to treat it as interchangeable with its like, indistinguishable from the others in the bag. Tange *individuates*; funge *pools*. They are the two faces of a single decidable characteristic — the one move separates by it, the other identifies through it — and between them they account for nearly every operation the construction performs. From here on these are technical verbs, used as defined, and the reader should hear no whimsy in them: to tange is to distinguish, to funge is to make fungible.

The names are worth a moment. *Funge* is fungibility — the property of coins or grains that lets one stand in for another because nothing but the shared characteristic survives the pooling; far downstream, when the book quotients motions that differ only by a choice of frame, it will be funging, and the principle that licenses it will turn out to be one of the construction's two foundational assumptions. *Tange* is the cut that selects. Keep the pair in view; the whole development is variations on separating and pooling by a characteristic the machine can decide.

A small example fixes the pair. Let the characteristic be *carries a positive sign*. To tange by it is to reach into a collection and draw out exactly the positively-signed members, holding each as the distinguished thing it is — this one, because it is positive. To funge by it is the opposite gesture: to sweep all the positively-signed members into one bag and thereafter decline to tell them apart, so that any one of them may stand for the whole bag, its individuality spent for membership. Same characteristic, two directions. The construction tanges when it needs a witness it can point to and funges when it needs a class it can count, and a surprising amount of the book is the bookkeeping of which it is doing.

## One shape, repeated: symbol, process, gate

The architecture is monotonous on purpose. At every level the construction repeats one figure, three parts to it.

First, a generated family of **symbols** — an `inductive` type that carries its own order built in. The first such family is `Number`; later rungs raise `Natural` and others. An inductive type is just a grammar: a finite set of ways to build a symbol from smaller symbols, with a comparison already attached so the symbols come pre-sorted.

Second, a **process** — a `structure` that holds the rung beneath it together with an `iterate`, a recorded operation that produces the next symbol from the last. The first is `CarrierProcess`; above it `CountingProcess`, and so on. The process is the moving part: it remembers where it has been and how to take one more step.

Third, a **gate** — a `class` that asks one decidable characteristic of whatever the process produced. `DISTINGUISHABLE` is such a gate: it tanges two carriers apart, deciding whether they differ. `ADMISSIBLE` is another, tanging by whether a step is allowed. The gate is exactly where the two verbs live, because a decidable characteristic is precisely what you need either to tange the objects that have it or to funge the ones that share it. No gate decides more than one thing; the construction buys its expressive power by stacking many narrow decisions, never by widening any one of them.

It helps to watch the figure turn once. At the first rung the symbols are raw numbers, the process is the carrier that hands them along, and the gate tanges two carriers apart by whether they differ at all — the coarsest characteristic there is, mere difference. Climb one rung. The symbols become natural numbers, built by iterating a successor; the process becomes a counting process that remembers how many steps it has taken; and the gate now tanges by a finer characteristic — whether a step is *admissible*, whether the count is allowed to advance. Nothing about the shape has changed: inductive symbols, a structure that iterates, a class that decides one property. Only the property has sharpened, from *are these two different* to *is this step allowed*. Every rung the body later describes is this same turn of the same figure, the characteristic refined one notch further, and the reader may always put to a new level the same three questions: what are its symbols, what is its step, and what does its gate tange or funge by?

Symbol, process, gate; inductive, structure, class. Each rung embeds the one below it — the process at one level carries the rung beneath as data — so the tower does not merely sit in a pile but threads through itself, every level holding the history of all the levels under it. This braid is the only structural idea in the construction. Once the reader sees it once, every later chapter is a new rung of the same three-part figure, and the body will describe those rungs in plain mathematics, naming their symbols, their step, and the characteristic each gate tanges or funges by.

## The tower inhabits itself

A grammar of gates is worthless if nothing satisfies the gates. The construction's quiet trick is that it supplies its own evidence: each gate is made to follow from the one beneath it, so that placing a single starting instance at the bottom lights up the entire tower for free.

In the proof assistant this is a chain of `instance` declarations — the system's mechanism for "here is why this gate is satisfied, given that the last one was." The chain begins at a first step and climbs: the bottommost gate, then the next gate derived from it, then the next derived from that, rung over rung, each one handing the elaborator exactly the evidence the rung above demands.

Concretely the climb is a short ladder of named steps: a first instance establishes the base gate; from it the next follows; from that one the next, rung over rung up through the levels that carry counting, encoding, residue, and the binary distinctions the later book will need. Each link asserts only *given the rung below, here is the rung above*, and the elaborator chains them without further help.

The reader does not have to furnish a proof that the construction is inhabited at level forty; the machine assembles that proof from the single instance placed at the foot of the chain. The apparatus, in other words, certifies its own existence. That is an unusual thing for a measuring instrument to do, and the book will return to it: an instrument that generates its own warrant can say a great deal about itself, and there is a precise limit — reached only at the boundary, much later — to what such self-warrant can settle.

## Turning the instrument on itself

The most telling measurement the machine takes is the one it takes of itself. The carrier that ferries symbols can be aimed at propositions directly — a carrier whose symbols *are* the claims — and once it is, the apparatus is pointed at its own subject matter. The decisive reading follows. Ask the instrument to compare, from the inside, the canonical truth against its own negation, and it returns an equality: as propositions, the truth of `true = true` and the denial of that truth are indistinguishable in a precise sense — each admits at most one witness, so each is, to the machine, a single featureless point. The two collapse. From within the instrument, *true* and *false* cannot be tanged apart.

The equality holds by a principle called propositional extensionality: two propositions that stand or fall together may be identified. That principle is one of the few the construction accepts without proof, and the essay "The Axiom of Choice" names the full short list and counts it; here we only record what the principle buys — the reading itself. And the reading is startling on first contact. An instrument turned on itself loses the very distinction the whole enterprise is about. This is not a paradox and not a defect; it is the construction telling the truth about a limit. An interior reading cannot, by itself, separate the case from its denial. Everything dramatic in the later book — the sign that distinguishes matter from antimatter, the sentence the apparatus cannot settle from within, the verdict that only an outside frame can return — is the slow work of prying that collapsed distinction back open at a boundary the interior cannot see. The machine has been pointed at itself and has handed back exactly one fact: from in here, true and false weigh the same.

## Two ideas to carry forward, as mathematics

Two notions are seeded now, in mathematics, so the reader enters the body already oriented.

The first is **truth-parity**. Every order the construction builds decides by the agreement or disagreement of signs. When two signs agree, the order counts upward by *wholes* — one, two, three — and grows; this is its covariant face. When they disagree, it counts by *parts* — one-half, one-third, and on toward zero; its contravariant face. A single duality, surfacing again and again as whole against part, signal against noise, and more. It is a tange/funge engine in miniature: agreement funges, pooling like with like into one growing count; disagreement tanges, cutting the parts apart toward nothing. The reader who holds onto parity holds onto the motor of almost everything that follows. And parity is decided, not assumed: the construction does not posit which face an order shows; it tanges the face out of the signs themselves, agreement against disagreement, and lets the arithmetic follow. Whole and part are not two number systems bolted together but one rule read in two directions.

The second is **the single invariant**, and it is the spine of the book. Everything the construction will eventually read — every charge, every sign, every orientation — reduces to one nonnegative quantity: the second variation of an action, the unique quadratic remainder left when a path is compared with the stationary one nearby. We do not prove this here, and we do not yet say what it is a reading *of*; we only post the promise as mathematics. There is one number. It is never negative. The colorful readings the later chapters announce are its faces, and Chapter 13 states the identity plainly. One number, and a definite shape to it: it is a *remainder* — what is left when the best linear story about a path is subtracted from the path itself — and it is a *square*, hence never negative and zero only when nothing remains to explain. That charge, the matter/antimatter sign, and the orientation of a loop are all faces of one nonnegative remainder is the book's central economy, and it is why a reader who keeps the single invariant in view is never quite lost: whatever a chapter seems to be about, it is about how this one quantity is being read.

## The machine goes offstage

The disclosure is complete. The reader has seen the instrument: the elaborator that decides and counts the cost of deciding; the Fact that carries its own verdict; the two verbs, tange and funge, that separate and pool by a decidable characteristic; the braid of symbol, process, and gate; the chain of instances by which the tower inhabits itself; and the one reading the machine returns when it is turned on its own truth.

From here the book is silent about the machine. It will describe how the construction works as mathematics — symbols and their orders, the two verbs, the gates, what is proved and what is assumed — and it will not name the elaborator, the classes, or the proof terms again. That vocabulary lives in three places only: the Preface, the essay "The Axiom of Choice," and this chapter. When a later page says the construction tanges a sign out of a motion, or funges a family of like motions into one interchangeable bag, it is naming a real operation of the apparatus the reader has now seen — and trusting that, having seen it once, the reader no longer needs to turn back and look.
