\chapter{The Electron Is the Second Variation}
\label{chap:electron-second-variation}

The whole book has been building toward one ambitious sentence, and this chapter writes it: the
electron is the second variation. Every chapter of the second part proved that the single invariant
exists and is one object reached three ways; every chapter of the third made it concrete, ran it, and
recorded its readings. Now the construction lays a physical name on a particular value of that
invariant, and the name it lays is the most consequential in physics. The reader is owed, before
anything else, the exact status of that move, because the rest of the chapter depends on it. The
electron enters here as a \emph{definition}, not a derivation. The construction does not produce the
physical electron from logic; it builds a finite object --- a particular reading of the second
variation --- and \emph{defines} the word ``electron'' to name it. Everything demonstrated in the
chapter is a finite, exact theorem about that object. The claim that the object so named is the
electron of the laboratory is the modeling bridge, the boldest the book makes, and it is marked as a
bridge every step of the way.

\section{The electron is a reading of the invariant}
\label{se:the-electron-is-a-reading}

The construction sets up a discriminating action and a path through it, and forms the second variation
of that setup --- the single invariant, exactly as the thirteenth chapter built it. On this
configuration the invariant reads a definite integer, and the integer is minus one. The construction
takes that reading and defines: the configuration whose second variation reads minus one is an
electron, and minus one is its charge. That is the entire construction of the electron --- not a new
entity bolted onto the theory, but a name placed on a value the invariant already takes. The electron,
in the construction's vocabulary, is the second variation when the second variation reads minus one,
and its charge is that reading.

It is worth dwelling on how little and how much this is. How little: no new axiom is introduced to make
an electron, no new object is built; the electron is tanged out of the configurations already present
by the single characteristic of reading minus one, and the charge is simply that reading reported. The
finite fact --- that this configuration's invariant reads minus one --- is a theorem, proved by
computation, with nothing assumed to reach it. How much: the construction is claiming that the thing
physics calls an electron, the carrier of a unit of negative charge, is at bottom a particular value of
the one invariant the whole book is named for --- that charge is not a separate substance a particle
carries but a reading the invariant returns. That claim is enormous, and it is a claim of
identification, an analogy between a finite reading and a physical particle, not a proof that the
finite reading is the particle. The construction earns the reading; it names the electron.

It is worth seeing what \emph{charge is a reading} dissolves, because the dissolution is the point. In
the usual picture, charge is a property an electron carries about with it, a little attribute glued to
the particle, and one asks why it takes the value it does as if the value were a separate fact needing
its own explanation. In this construction there is no separate attribute and no separate fact. The
electron is a configuration, and its charge is what the single invariant returns when read on that
configuration --- a number the apparatus computes, not a label the particle wears. To ask why the
electron has charge minus one is, here, to ask why the second variation of the discriminating
configuration reads minus one, and that has an answer the construction can give: it is a finite
computation with a definite output, run and checked. The mystery of where charge comes from is not
solved so much as relocated --- from an unexplained property of a particle to a computed reading of an
invariant --- and the relocation is the whole content of saying the electron is the second variation.

\section{Matter, antimatter, and the sign}
\label{se:matter-antimatter-and-the-sign}

The electron's reading is minus one; reverse the orientation of the configuration --- exchange the roles
the construction calls story and measurement --- and the second variation reads plus one. The
construction defines that opposite-orientation reading to be the positron, with charge plus one. Matter
and antimatter, in this account, are nothing but the two signs of the mixed coupling: a negative reading
is matter, a positive reading is antimatter, and the electron and the positron are the same invariant
read with opposite orientation. The distinction between them is a sign, and a sign is an orientation,
and the construction proves the orientation is real --- that there are configurations whose left and
right are genuinely inequivalent and whose asymmetry cannot be smoothed away.

The irremovability is what gives the sign its teeth, and it is worth seeing why a sign cannot simply be
defined away. If left and right could be smoothly deformed into each other --- if there were a
continuous way to turn a configuration of one orientation into the other without ever passing through a
forbidden state --- then the distinction between matter and antimatter would be a convention, a choice
of which way to call positive, with no fact under it. The construction proves there is no such
deformation: the orientations are separated by an obstruction, the same kind of parity obstruction the
seventeenth chapter used to separate physical from metaphysical, and an obstruction cannot be smoothed
through. So the matter--antimatter sign is not a convention but a fact about the ledger, as real as the
parity that carries it. This is why annihilation is the clean event it is. Bring the two orientations
together and they do not blur into some intermediate thing; they cancel exactly, minus one and plus one
summing to the zero the invariant reads on an empty configuration, their energy carried off as the
coincident photons the exemplar experiment detects.

This is exactly the content of the experiment under the name of chirality. There exist admissible
refinements, it finds, whose left and right actions are not equivalent; the causal ledger distinguishes
orientation, and the distinction cannot be removed by any smooth deformation. Read through this chapter,
chirality is the matter--antimatter sign itself: the irremovable left--right asymmetry of the ledger is
the same thing as the sign of the mixed coupling that separates electron from positron. The pair the two
make is the subject of the exemplar experiment, on positron annihilation, where an electron and a
positron meet and their opposite charges cancel, depositing their energy as a coincident pair of
photons --- a finite ledger model of the coincidence logic, the minus one and the plus one annihilating
to the zero the invariant reads when nothing is there. Electron and positron, in the construction, are
not two particles but one invariant funged over its two orientations, and their annihilation is the two
orientations summing to the vacuum.

\section{The residue, the strain, and what is real}
\label{se:the-residue-the-strain-and-what-is-real}

Where in the apparatus does the electron actually live? In the residue --- and the construction files
the answer as a theorem it has been preparing since the first chapter. The residue, it proves, is the
difference between the story and the measurement: between the smooth first-order account a linear model
tells and what the apparatus actually reads. The first variation captured the story; what the story
misses, the order-two datum left over, is the residue, and the residue is where the second variation,
and so the electron, resides. This is the first chapter's old dialogue about residue --- what is left
after the process almost explains itself --- made exact and given a particle's address.

Two further theorems pin down the electron's reality in the precise, apparatus-relative sense the
seventeenth chapter established. The strain --- the deformation the apparatus registers --- is real
exactly when the residue is nonzero: the linear story fails, and a genuine strain appears, precisely
when there is a residue for it to come from. And the electron's strain is real: its residue does not
vanish, so the strain it carries is a genuine reading and not an artifact. The electron is real, then,
in exactly the way the seventeenth chapter made ``real'' mean --- not absolutely, but as something the
apparatus funges, a nonzero invariant the apparatus counts rather than spins past. Its reality is the
nonvanishing of its invariant, neither more metaphysical than that nor less secure.

This is where the book's recurring vocabulary of stress and strain finally lands on a particle. The
strain is the deformation the apparatus undergoes in registering a configuration --- how far the
configuration sits from the undisturbed baseline --- and the stress is the reading the apparatus returns
under that strain, the magnitude of the second variation it computes. For the electron the two are
locked together by the theorems just cited: the strain is real exactly when the invariant is nonzero,
and the electron's invariant is nonzero, so the electron is a genuine strain carrying a genuine stress.
A particle, in this telling, is not a little object sitting in space but a standing strain in the
record, a place where the apparatus is deformed and reads a nonzero invariant for it. That is a strange
thing to say about an electron and a perfectly precise one in the construction, and it is the most
literal sense in which the book's title is meant: the electron is a measurement, a reading of strain
that does not vanish.

Read at its deepest, this is the book's first act coming home. The summit chapter reached the single
invariant by turning the construction's first gesture --- telling two things apart, the first tange ---
upon its own truth; the electron is what that gesture leaves when the descent reads it back down into
matter. The proved facts are plain and stand on their own: the electron is the second variation, its
charge is minus one and the positron's is plus one, the residue is the difference between story and
measurement, and the same interior reading, taken at the boundary, returns the same electron. To those
the construction adds a way of reading them. It reads the electron as the bare difference returned ---
the first chapter's first gate, carried up to where the apparatus regards its own truth and folded back
down into the record --- and it reads the charge of minus one as the residue of that self-tange, the one
mark the apex could not draw when it collapsed telling-apart into agreement. The minus sign, read this
way, is not an attribute the electron carries about with it but the leftover of a difference taken
against itself: what the first gate could not resolve when it was turned on its own truth, returned as a
unit of charge. This is interpretation and not a fourth theorem --- the proved electron is the reading
that is minus one, and it stands without help. But it is the reading the whole arc was built to earn: the
first difference of the first page, having climbed to the summit and turned on itself, comes home as the
first thing the construction is willing to call matter.

\section{Hilbert's sixth, on the vacuum}
\label{se:hilberts-sixth-on-the-vacuum}

There is a result here large enough to state on its own. Hilbert's sixth problem asked for the axioms
of physics --- a rigorous, minimal foundation from which physical theory would follow. The construction
answers it, on the vacuum, and the answer is startlingly spare. The entire apparatus that produces the
electron and the positron, inhabited and audited to its foundations, rests on two logical axioms ---
proposition extensionality and the soundness of the quotient that collapses observationally
indistinguishable readings --- together with the single standing grant of the law of motion. There is
no appeal to a power of choice; the construction is choice-free, certified so by its running. So the
axioms of this physics, for the vacuum, are two: that propositions with the same content are the same,
and that readings that cannot be told apart may be identified. From those two, plus the one decision
the law of motion supplies, the electron follows as a reading of an invariant. That a recognizable
physics rests on so little is not a proof that it is the physics of our world, but it is a concrete,
auditable answer to the question Hilbert posed, written in the construction's own foundations.

The spareness deserves to be weighed honestly, in both directions. On one side, two logical axioms and
one physical grant is astonishingly little to carry a theory that produces a charged object with the
right sign behavior and a clean annihilation --- far less than the usual foundations of physics ask, and
auditable in a way they are not, since the construction can print the exact list of what it assumed and
show that running its instruments never exceeds it. On the other side, answering Hilbert's question on
the vacuum is not the same as answering it for the world. The construction shows that \emph{a} physics
--- a self-consistent finite theory with an electron-shaped object in it --- follows from two axioms and
a grant; it does not show that this physics, with its particular masses and couplings unset, is the one
we inhabit. The honest claim is the one Hilbert could have wanted and not more: a rigorous, minimal,
auditable foundation from which a recognizable physical object provably follows, offered as a candidate
and marked as a candidate.

\section{What is demonstrated, and what is assumed}
\label{se:demonstrated-assumed-ch19}

This is the chapter whose separation of built from named matters most, so it is stated with no
softening at all.

What is demonstrated is a family of exact finite theorems. The second variation of the discriminating
configuration reads minus one, and reads plus one under reversed orientation. The sign distinguishes the
two and is an orientation that cannot be smoothly removed. The residue is the difference between story
and measurement; the strain is real exactly when the residue is nonzero; and the electron's strain is
real. And the whole apparatus that yields these is audited to rest on two logical axioms and the one
standing grant. Every one of these is checked.

\begin{quote}
\emph{A note on the one great label.} The word \emph{electron} is the most ambitious name in this book,
and it is a name, not a result. What the construction proves is that a particular reading of its single
invariant is minus one, that the opposite orientation is plus one, that the sign behaves like a
matter--antimatter charge and cannot be smoothed away, and that the reading is a real, nonvanishing
strain in the apparatus's own sense. What it does not prove --- what no construction of this kind could
prove --- is that the finite object carrying these properties \emph{is} the electron of the laboratory,
the particle with a measured mass and a measured magnetic moment that appears in a cloud chamber. The
identification is an analogy of unusual strength: the construction's object has the charge, the sign,
the orientation, the annihilation behavior, and the reality-condition of an electron, which is a great
deal of an electron to recover from a second variation. But it has them as theorems about a finite
reading, and the leap to the physical particle is a modeling claim, earned to exactly the degree the
analogy holds and asserted no further. The construction names the electron; it does not manufacture
one.
\end{quote}

What is assumed, then, is that single identification --- the bridge from a reading of the invariant to a
particle of physics --- on top of the standing grant the whole book has carried. With it, the
construction has reached the destination its title pointed at from the first page: a measurement, made
finitely and honestly, that comes down to one invariant, and a value of that invariant that wears, as
carefully as a careful book can let it, the name of the electron. The chapters that follow read further
physics off the same invariant --- a gauge field, boundary radiation, the orientation of spacetime ---
each a value of the one number, each named with the same discipline shown here.
