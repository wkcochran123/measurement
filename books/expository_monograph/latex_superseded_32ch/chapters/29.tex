\chapter{Relative Velocity Is a Selection}
\label{chap:relative-velocity-is-a-selection}

The construction's foundations, audited at the vacuum, came down to two axioms: proposition
extensionality, and the soundness of the quotient that identifies readings nothing can observationally
tell apart. The book has used both abstractly for twenty-eight chapters. This final part gives them
physical identities, and the first identity is the striking one. The second axiom --- the quotient that
collapsed true and false at the start and was repaid at the boundary --- is, physically, the relativity
of velocity. Relative velocity, the construction shows, is the whole minus its parts, taken not as a raw
number but up to observational equivalence: a selection, a reading defined only relative to the frames an
observer cannot distinguish. The abstract quotient and the physical principle that there is no absolute
velocity turn out to be one thing, identified through a single application of the quotient axiom.

\section{Velocity as whole minus parts}
\label{se:velocity-as-whole-minus-parts}

The construction begins with a frame reading: an observer's record of a whole and of the parts that whole
decomposes into. From these it defines relative velocity as the simplest thing it could be --- the whole
minus the parts, what the whole carries that its decomposition into parts does not account for. This is
the reciprocal residual of the orientation chapter, read now as a velocity. There, the resolved and the
unresolved summed to the whole; here, the parts an observer resolves and the relative velocity left over
sum to the whole the observer reads. Relative velocity is the unresolved remainder of a frame, the motion
that survives after the parts are subtracted out --- not a substance the frame contains but a residue its
own decomposition leaves.

A worked frame makes the definition concrete. Suppose an observer reads a whole of some definite size and
resolves it into parts that account for all but a little of it; the relative velocity is that little, the
whole minus the summed parts. Another observer, resolving the same whole into a finer or coarser set of
parts, reads a different remainder and so a different relative velocity --- and neither is wrong, because
each velocity is correct relative to the decomposition that defines it. The construction's electron frame
and positron frame are two such readings, and when it computes their wholes and parts it finds the
relative velocities equal in size and opposite in sign. The arithmetic is elementary; what matters is that
the velocity each observer reads is a property of their frame's decomposition, not of an absolute motion
the frames share. Two observers, one whole, two relative velocities, neither privileged: the situation
relativity describes, set up here as a subtraction.

Read this way, relative velocity is already a difference, a tange in the book's sense: the whole is
selected against its parts, and what is selected --- the leftover --- is the velocity. The construction
makes the definition concrete on its electron and positron frames, computing each one's whole, its parts,
and the difference between them. Nothing in this first step is yet relative in the deep sense; it is
arithmetic on a frame reading. The relativity enters with the next step, when the construction declines
to take the raw difference as final.

There is a reason the construction reads velocity as a residual rather than a primitive, and it is the
same reason the whole book has read measurements as residuals. A primitive velocity would be a number a
frame simply has, available to be looked up; a residual velocity is a number that exists only as the gap
between a whole and the parts an observer chose to resolve. The second is the honest one, because no
observer reads a velocity directly --- they read a whole, they read parts, and the velocity is what the
one has over the other. To make velocity the leftover of a decomposition is to admit that it depends on
the decomposition, on what the observer counted as parts; change the parts and the leftover changes. This
dependence is not yet relativity, but it is its seed: a quantity defined as a remainder against a chosen
decomposition is already a quantity no observer reads absolutely, only against the choices their frame
encodes.

\section{The quotient: velocity is a selection}
\label{se:the-quotient-velocity-is-a-selection}

The construction does not let relative velocity be the bare number whole minus parts. It takes that
number up to observational equivalence: two frame readings that no observation can tell apart are
declared to give the same relative velocity, and relative velocity proper is the equivalence class, not
the raw difference. Relative velocity is, in the construction's structure, a quotient --- the readings
funged together whenever they are observationally indistinguishable, the velocity being the pool, not any
representative of it. This is what makes velocity relative. There is no absolute relative velocity to be
had, because the construction has, by the quotient, identified every pair of frames an observer cannot
separate; what survives the identification is only the velocity relative to a frame, defined precisely up
to the indistinguishability the quotient imposes.

This is the principle the experiments on the relativity of motion have always reported. Under the names
of Galileo and Abel, the finding is that uniform motion cannot be detected from within --- no experiment
in a closed cabin reveals the cabin's velocity, because the states of different uniform velocity are
observationally equivalent and nothing inside distinguishes them. The construction's quotient is exactly
that equivalence made structural: the frames of different absolute velocity are funged into one whenever
no observation separates them, and the relative velocity is what remains. The experiment of Michelson and
Morley sharpened the same point by failing to find an absolute frame at all --- no ether, no privileged
rest --- which is the physical statement that the quotient has no canonical representative, that there is
no frame the others are really moving against. Velocity is a selection because the world supplies no
absolute against which to read it, and the construction's quotient is the formal shape of that absence.

It is worth dwelling on how completely the quotient captures the relativity principle, because the fit is
exact rather than approximate. The relativity of uniform motion is not the claim that velocity is hard to
measure absolutely; it is the stronger claim that there is nothing absolute there to measure --- that the
question \emph{what is this frame's true velocity} has no answer, not even a hidden one. A quotient says
precisely this about its classes: to ask which representative of an equivalence class is the real one is
to ask a question the quotient has declared meaningless, because the class is the object and the
representatives are interchangeable. So when the construction makes relative velocity a quotient, it is
not modeling the difficulty of finding an absolute frame; it is encoding the non-existence of one. The
frames of different uniform velocity are not merely hard to tell apart --- they are, by the quotient, the
same point, and asking which is at rest is asking which representative is real, a question with no
content. Relativity is not a limit on knowledge here; it is a fact about what there is.

The quotient identifies, but it does not identify everything, and the line it draws is itself physical.
Uniform velocity is quotiented away --- the frames moving uniformly are the same point --- but not every
distinction collapses. The experiment of Sagnac marks the boundary: a rotating frame can detect its own
rotation, by an effect no quotient washes out, so rotation is absolute where uniform velocity is relative.
The construction's quotient is between observationally equivalent frames, and rotating frames are not
observationally equivalent to resting ones --- the rotation shows. So the relativity the quotient encodes
is precisely the relativity of \emph{uniform} motion, and the construction is faithful to the physics in
quotienting exactly that and no more. What survives the quotient is what an observer can still tell about
their own state, and rotation, unlike velocity, survives.

\section{One quotient, and the routes agree}
\label{se:one-quotient-and-the-routes-agree}

The construction computes relative velocity by two roads and proves they meet. The first is the
measurement road just described: whole minus parts, taken up to the quotient. The second is the invariant
road: the relative velocity read off the second variation, the single invariant the whole book has
turned on. These are different computations with different starting points, and the construction proves
they agree --- and agree through a single application of the quotient axiom, one invocation of the
soundness that lets observationally equal things be identified. The two routes to relative velocity are
the same route, joined at the one quotient.

The agreement of the two routes is not a coincidence to be checked but a constraint the quotient enforces,
and seeing why repays the effort. The measurement route builds relative velocity from a frame's own whole
and parts; the invariant route reads it from the second variation, an object defined without reference to
any particular frame. That these should land on the same number means the frame-bound reading and the
frame-free one cannot disagree --- and they cannot, because the quotient that defines relative velocity is
exactly what identifies the frame-bound representatives with the frame-free class they belong to. The
single application of the quotient is the hinge on which the two routes turn into one: it says the
velocity an observer measures in their frame and the velocity the invariant carries across all frames are
the same velocity, read once locally and once globally. Two roads meeting at one quotient is the structure
of a quantity that is both observer-relative and objectively fixed --- relative in which representative
you read, absolute in which class you read.

This is the physical identity the chapter exists to state. The second of the construction's two
foundational axioms, the quotient, is not merely a logical convenience; it is the relativity of velocity
itself. Where proposition extensionality says that propositions with the same content are the same, the
quotient says that frames with the same observations are the same --- and that is exactly the statement
that velocity is relative, that absolute motion is not a thing the world distinguishes. The construction
spent this axiom at the start to collapse true and false and earned it back at the boundary as a
distinction; now it reads the same axiom a third way and finds relativity. And the orientation returns
with it: the positron's relative velocity is proved to be the negative of the electron's, the two equal
and opposite, so that the pair's relative velocities cancel exactly --- the matter--antimatter sign of
several chapters ago, reappearing as the sign of a velocity, summing to zero when the pair is taken as a
whole.

That one axiom should carry three readings is the deepest economy the book has reached, and it is worth
collecting them. The quotient was first spent to collapse true and false, buying foundations of two
axioms where physics usually assumes a continuum. It was then earned back at the boundary, where on and
off, separated, restored the distinction it had flattened. And it is read here a third time, on frames
rather than truth-values, as the relativity of velocity. These are not three axioms but one, wearing
three faces: a single principle of identification --- that things observationally the same are the same
--- applied to readings, to boundary signals, and to frames. The construction did not add relativity to
its physics; it found relativity already present in the one axiom it had been using since the second
chapter, waiting to be read on the right objects. To economize a foundation to two axioms and then
discover that one of them is relativity is to find that the physics was in the logic all along, folded
into the cheapest assumption the construction could make.

\section{What is demonstrated, and what is assumed}
\label{se:demonstrated-assumed-ch29}

The accounting turns on one identification, and it is a clean one.

What is demonstrated is the structure of relative velocity. It is defined as whole minus parts and proved
to be a quotient by observational equivalence, a selection rather than an absolute. The measurement route
and the invariant route are proved to agree through a single application of the quotient axiom. And the
positron's relative velocity is proved to be the negative of the electron's, the pair cancelling. Each is
a checked statement about finite frame readings.

\begin{quote}
\emph{A note on the identification.} The claim that the quotient axiom \emph{is} the relativity of
velocity is a modeling bridge, but an unusually tight one, and worth distinguishing from the looser
bridges of the physics chapters. There, a finite invariant was given the name of a particle or a field it
structurally resembled. Here the match is closer: relativity of velocity, in its operational content, is
precisely the statement that observationally equivalent frames are not distinguished, and that is, on the
nose, what the quotient asserts. The construction does not merely build something shaped like relativity;
it builds the quotient by observational equivalence, and relativity of velocity is what that quotient
means when its objects are frames. The identification is therefore as much a translation as an analogy ---
the same structure read in two vocabularies, the logical and the physical. Still, that the construction's
frames and observations model the world's is the bridge, and it is named: the structure is the quotient;
calling its physical instance relativity is the reading laid over it.
\end{quote}

What is assumed, then, is the standing grant and this one tight identification, with the quotient itself
built and proved. The second axiom has its physical name. With velocity shown relative --- a selection up
to the frames an observer cannot separate --- the construction has begun reading its spare foundations as
physics. The next chapter follows the orientation sign into the same relative setting, showing that
whether a thing reads as matter or antimatter depends, like velocity, on the baseline the observer brings.
