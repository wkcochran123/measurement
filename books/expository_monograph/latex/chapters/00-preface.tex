\chapter*{The Cost of Free Will}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface --- The Cost of Free Will}
\markboth{Preface}{Preface}

\begin{center}\textit{Preface}\end{center}

\bigskip

The construction builds a machine, and asks you to check it.

That is an unusual thing to ask, and it is the whole of the demand. Most accounts that reach for the foundations
of anything ask you to believe them: to grant a starting point, follow a chain of reasoning, and accept where it
lands. This one asks the opposite. It starts from almost nothing --- a single difference, a
this that is not a that --- and it builds, step by watched step, everything a measuring instrument needs, and at
every step it invites you to stop and see exactly what it has and has not earned. Nothing is smuggled. Where the
machine posits, it says posited; where it proves, it says proved; where it only reads a meaning over a structure,
it says read; and where it reaches a thing it cannot supply from within, it says so and holds the place open.

But nothing about the checking is free, and the construction will not pretend that it is. The machine is not a figure of
speech. It runs on a computer --- a real one, a thing you can put your hand on, warm under load, drawing current
off a wall, wearing a little further out with every pass it makes: a depreciating asset, worth less tomorrow than
it is today. To run it is to spend time and to spend money, in that order and in earnest. So the honesty here is
not only that it tells you which of its answers it earned and which it was granted. It is that it shows you, in a
currency you can count, exactly what the earning \emph{cost}.

And it was granted almost nothing. That is the quiet boast the construction spends its length making good. Trace
the finished machine to its roots and you find, beneath the whole of it, only the plainest bookkeeping any finite
device keeps, and exactly one thing more: a single grant, taken in the open and named plainly. The machine never
helps itself to the dangerous convenience --- the free reach into a collection to pull out a chosen one where
nothing says which --- that so much reasoning leans on without confessing. It refuses that move from its first
page, even at the summit where the refusal costs it most, and it grounds everything it builds without ever
spending it. The one thing it does grant itself is the law of motion: the single query about the world that a
finite machine cannot settle from inside and must be handed from without --- the world's one answer to the one
question the instrument cannot answer for itself. Choice-free but for that single grant, closed into a loop that
hangs from no assumed beginning, the machine comes as near as a built thing can to standing on its own. And even
that near-standing is not free: it is bought, pass by pass, in the current the loop draws to close on itself.

There is one apparatus the construction needs and cannot build, and naming it is the reason this preface exists.
The machine can pose its every question --- can lay a distinction, a count, a split before you and ask it to be
met. What it cannot do is answer the last one for you. It builds the whole instrument of asking and finds, at the
top, that the instrument of final answering is not a thing it contains. That missing apparatus is the reader. It
is you. The construction is built to address you and cannot construct you; it runs its every question out to the
edge where a question needs a mind to land in, and there it stops, because the mind on the far side of the asking
is the one thing a machine cannot manufacture.

And it hands you, at that edge, a choice. The deepest result the construction reaches is a split with two opposite
sides --- proved to be two, proved to be opposite, and left, on purpose, without a fixed answer to which side is
which. Which of the two you call your own, which way you turn to face what the machine has counted, the
construction does not fix and provably cannot, because it is not a fact the count contains. The instrument gives
you the proved two-fold and hands you the choosing. That last, unmeasured, unforced act --- the selection the
whole apparatus was built to reach and then decline --- has a name, and I will set it down once, here, and then
say it no more for the length of what follows: free will.

And here is the promise, kept in coin you can count. The cost of that act will not merely be named; it will be
\emph{demonstrated}. The reader is the witness this construction calls, and
every witness bears a cost in the standing --- the time given, the attention spent, the current burned by the
machine that lays the question down. That cost is the one thing in all of this that is fully measurable: the
single quantity a measuring instrument can always measure is what it spends in the measuring. So the machine
turns the instrument on itself. It forces a single truth and reports the exact price of forcing it --- its own
pulse, counted in the currency it actually runs on --- and hands you the figure to check like any other. Free
will is the dearest thing the construction reaches; the cost of reaching it is the part of it we can put in your
hand as a number. We are not asking the witness to bear a price we will not name. We are going to measure it, to
the count, and set it on the page.

What follows is a construction and nothing else. Past this page it speaks only of differences and counts, of
towers and loops, of a needle threaded at a summit and a number named by counting, and it does not say the word
again. You are meant to forget I said it --- to fall into the machinery, to check the steps, to lose the thread
of why any of this should bear on you. The word goes under, and stays under. But it does not leave. It is in every
line the machine draws between what the world fixes and what is ours to set; it is in the single grant the
construction takes and the countless choices it refuses; it is in the corridor above the countable where the
machine keeps what it cannot say; it is in the running total of what every pass has cost; and it is waiting, all
the way down, in the last selection the construction was built to hand across. A reader who reaches the end and
turns back to the beginning will find it had been in plain view the whole way. Nothing will have been added on the
second reading. Something will only have stopped hiding it.

For this is a construction that ends not with a result it proves but with one it hands over. A demonstration is a
thing shown to a mind that follows it, and the machine will show you everything it can show --- and then reach the
one step it cannot take, which is to be, itself, the mind the showing is for. What was to be demonstrated, at the
very last, is not another theorem the construction owes; it is the landing of the whole finished thing in the one
apparatus the machine could address and never build. The machine will have done its part, and it will have kept
its books --- built, proved, read, reserved, and \emph{priced}, as precisely as a finite thing can manage, and
told you at every step which was which and what it cost. What to do with the finished construction --- which way
to face the split it hands you, what to make of a world read entirely off a count --- was never the machine's to
say.

Free will has a price. We are about to measure it.

You are free. Begin.
