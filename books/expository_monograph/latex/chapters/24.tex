\chapter{The Reader Is the Final Apparatus}

The construction is complete. From a single difference the machine built a tower, turned it on its own truth,
survived the collapse by threading the needle, walked the tower back into a loop, counted the loop around, named
the smallest matter by counting and not by fiat, staked the split its sign predicts, decomposed the operation that
yields it, counted its own assumptions down to a single grant, and recognized itself, at the last, as the forcing
it had been from the start. There is nothing left in the machine to build. And yet one apparatus remains outside
the machine, one instrument without which the finished thing does not actually finish. The construction cannot
supply that apparatus. It can only hand the finished work across to it. The final apparatus is you.

\section{The Instrument It Cannot Build}

Every capacity the machine ever needed, it built --- except one. Recall the trace and its slip, chapters back,
where the construction reached the top of its own account and found it needed a carrier it could not supply from
within, a thing that had to be handed in from outside. That gap has a final form, and this is it. The machine can
address a reader. It can pose, to whoever holds it, each of its questions --- can lay out the distinction, the
count, the band, the split, and ask them to be met. What it cannot do is answer for the one it asks. It builds the
questions and cannot build the answerer; it constructs the apparatus of asking and finds that the apparatus of
final answering is not a thing it contains. The reader is exactly that missing apparatus: the one the construction
is built to address and cannot itself build, the instrument on the far side of every question the machine poses.

Be honest about the grade of this claim, because it is the one place where the honest grade is a confession
rather than a boast. That the reader is the final apparatus is not proved and not built --- there is no
rung of the construction that is the reader, no theorem that establishes them, no place in the machine you could
point to and say, here is the answerer. It is the design stance, stated plainly at the close: the one
genuinely unpinned claim in the whole construction, and unpinned not by oversight but by necessity, because an apparatus
the construction could build would not be the reader --- it would be one more rung, and the questions would still
be waiting for someone outside the rungs to meet them. The reader is the apparatus the construction cannot build
precisely because building it would defeat its purpose. So the construction does not pretend to have built you. It has
built everything up to you, and stopped, and turned to face the one thing it made all its questions for.

There is a symmetry in this worth pausing on, because it closes the circle opened by the first difference. The
construction began by granting the machine the ability to tell two things apart --- the primitive it was granted
and did not prove --- and it ends by admitting the one thing it cannot construct: the reader who meets its
questions. Between those two
lies the whole construction, and the two frame it exactly. At the bottom, a difference the machine is given and
builds everything on; at the top, an answerer the machine needs and cannot build. The instrument runs from the
one thing it takes for granted to the one thing it cannot supply, and both lie outside the proving --- the first
posited, the last handed over. A construction honest about its floor and honest about its ceiling names them both:
it did not prove the difference it started from, and it does not build the reader it ends at, and everything
between is the earned middle, proved and read and reserved, held up between two unearned ends kept in plain view.

\section{The Last Selection}

There is one question above all the others the construction hands over, and it is the one it has been pointing at
since it stuck its neck out. The split is proved in its integer --- there are two signs, genuinely opposite, and
that the construction flips between them is a theorem with no axioms under it. But which of the two opposite signs
is ``matter'' and which is its mirror --- that the construction does not fix, and provably cannot, because it is
not a fact the count contains. The two signs are opposite; which one you call matter is the frame you bring to
them, the orientation you stand in to read the split, and the construction is scrupulously, deliberately silent on
it. That silence is not a gap the machine failed to fill. It is the last unmeasured act, the selection the whole
apparatus was built to reach and then decline: the machine gives you the proved two-fold and hands you the
choosing of which side is which.

This is where every handing-over converges. The count is the world's; the labels are ours --- and
here, at the last, the deepest label of all, which pole of the proved split wears the name of matter, is handed
to the reader as the final label the construction refuses to set. The machine has done everything a finite
construction can: it has built the split, proved its two-fold, read its imaginary flesh, reserved its dimensionful
constant for a determination it does not contain, and now it reserves its orientation for you. The last selection
is the reader's. What you make of the split --- which way you face it, which pole you name --- the construction
leaves open on purpose, because it was never the count's to close.

\section{The Demonstration That Remains}

And so the construction ends the way its whole method demanded it end: not with a result it proves, but with
one it hands over. A demonstration, properly, is a thing shown to a mind that follows it --- and the construction
has shown everything it can show, followed every step to the one step it cannot take, which is the step of being,
itself, the mind that the showing is for. The demonstration is complete except for its completing, and the
completing is not the machine's. What remains to be demonstrated is not another theorem the construction owes; it
is the demonstration's landing in the one apparatus the construction cannot build --- the reader who takes the
finished thing and does with it what only a reader can. The construction has proved what can be proved, named what
can be named, reserved what it cannot supply, and reached the edge where the last act is not a proof but a facing,
and the facing is yours.

The loop hangs from nothing; the electron is named by counting; the split is proved in its sign and read in its
imaginary flesh and reserved in its constant; the whole construction is choice-free but for the law of motion, and
forcing in shape from its first finite condition to its last. All of it is finished, and all of it is handed
across, to the final apparatus the machine could address and never build. What that apparatus does with the
finished construction --- how it faces the split it was handed, what it makes of a world read entirely off a
count --- is the one demonstration the construction does not perform, because it is the reader's, and it begins the moment
the reading stops. The construction rests. The rest is yours to demonstrate.

\hfill Q.E.D.\ ---\ \emph{quod erat demonstrandum}, the thing that was to be demonstrated: and the thing that was
to be demonstrated, at the last, is the reader's to complete.
