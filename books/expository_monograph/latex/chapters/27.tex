\chapter{The Sentence the Interior Cannot Settle}
\label{chap:sentence-interior-cannot-settle}

The boundary obstruction had two faces. From outside it was a nonzero error code, a signal switched on.
From inside it is something stranger and more familiar: a question the interior cannot answer about
itself. The construction has become, over its course, a formal system rich enough to speak of its own
order, and any such system meets the limit Godel found --- there are sentences it can state but cannot
settle. This chapter exhibits one. The construction forms, in its own formal language, a self-referential
sentence that speaks of its own internal order, marks it with a new property it calls distinguished, and
proves that this sentence is exactly the one that reads the boundary obstruction the interior cannot
decide. The undecidable sentence within and the obstruction at the boundary are the same thing, seen from
the two sides one last time. And there is not one such sentence but an indexed family of them, the
incompleteness systematic rather than accidental.

\section{The undecidable face of the obstruction}
\label{se:the-undecidable-face-of-the-obstruction}

A formal system that can describe its own workings can write down sentences that talk about themselves,
and among these are sentences it can neither prove nor refute from within --- the discovery that bears
Godel's name. The construction has reached exactly this condition. It carries an internal language in
which its own closures and orders are expressible, and so it is subject to the same limit: there are
statements it can form and cannot settle. The boundary obstruction of the last chapter is the physical
shadow of one such statement. Asked from inside whether the invariant closes at the boundary, the
construction cannot answer; the question is undecidable to the interior, settled only by the outside
reading that sees the error code directly. What the outside registers as an obstruction, the inside
encounters as an undecidable sentence, and the two are the same fact met from opposite sides.

The route by which the construction earned this predicament is worth recalling, because it was not
sought. Incompleteness is the price of a particular kind of richness: a system pays it precisely when it
becomes able to encode statements about its own provability, to turn its syntax into objects it can reason
over. The construction acquired exactly that capacity when it returned to the number tower and coded its
own conditions into the naturals --- the same coding, run for countability, that makes a system able to
talk about itself. So the undecidable sentence is not an intruder; it is the shadow the construction's
self-description casts the moment the description is rich enough to be faithful. A system poor enough to
avoid incompleteness would have been too poor to describe itself, and the construction chose, chapters
ago, to be the richer thing. The obstruction it cannot settle is the cost of the self-knowledge it has.

It is worth being clear about what the construction does and does not claim here, because the territory
is one where overclaiming is easy. The classical metatheorem --- that any sufficiently rich consistent
system has undecidable sentences --- is long known and is not what the construction proves. What the
construction does, which is the new and concrete thing, is exhibit the actual sentence its own language
forms: not a general existence result but a specific, written-down sentence, the one this device can
build, shown to be undecidable from within and shown to read the obstruction. The metatheorem says such
sentences exist; the construction hands you one of them and tells you where it points.

\section{The distinguished sentence}
\label{se:the-distinguished-sentence}

The sentence the construction forms is self-referential in the Godel manner: it speaks of its own
internal order, a statement whose subject is its own standing in the system that states it. The
construction builds it concretely --- a closure with a source, a target, and a relation between them ---
and proves that it says its own internal order, that its content is precisely a claim about how it itself
is arranged. To a sentence with this property the construction gives a new name, the capstone word of
this stretch of the development: it is \emph{distinguished}. A distinguished sentence is one that has
folded back on itself in the self-referential way, and the construction proves the decisive fact about
it: the distinguished Godel sentence reads the obstruction. The sentence the interior cannot settle is,
exactly, the sentence that reads the boundary obstruction --- the undecidability within and the
obstruction without identified through the one distinguished sentence that touches both.

What earns the sentence the word distinguished is worth making precise, because the construction is
careful with the term. An ordinary sentence in the formal language speaks of something other than itself
--- a closure, an order, a fact out in the system. The distinguished sentence speaks of its own internal
order: its subject and its statement coincide, so that reading it is reading a claim about the very thing
doing the reading. This folding-back is what classical logic achieves by diagonalization, the device that
builds a sentence asserting its own unprovability, and the construction's distinguished sentence is its
concrete diagonal. The mark \emph{distinguished} records that the fold has happened --- that this is a
sentence the system cannot hold at arm's length, because it is about the arm. And it is exactly such
sentences, the ones that cannot be held away, that the interior cannot settle, because to settle them
would be to step outside the very system they are about, which the interior cannot do.

The experiment on provers and verifiers frames what a sentence's standing amounts to in a system like
this. A claim's status is fixed not by inspection from nowhere but by a protocol --- a prover proposing,
a verifier checking --- and a distinguished sentence is one the interior's protocol can state but cannot
close, neither proving nor refuting it by any run of the checker. The construction's distinguished
sentence is undecidable in just this operational sense: the verifier, handed it, never halts with a
verdict, because the sentence's truth is not in the interior to be found. It is at the boundary, in the
obstruction, where the outside reading and not the interior protocol settles it.

\section{The correlation the interior cannot locally settle}
\label{se:the-correlation-the-interior-cannot-locally-settle}

There is a physical resonance here deep enough to dwell on, with the fence kept up. The defining feature
of the distinguished sentence is that it cannot be settled locally, from within the interior, and yet is
perfectly definite once the boundary is read. That is the exact shape of quantum nonlocality. In the
experiments under the names of Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen, and of Bell and Aspect, two parts of an
jointly prepared system share a correlation that no local account of either part can settle --- measure one part
all you like, and the correlation is not in what you find locally --- and yet the correlation is real,
measured to exquisite precision, settled only when the two readings are brought together globally. The
construction's obstruction has this structure precisely: undecidable to the local interior, definite at
the global boundary, the truth living in the relation between the sides rather than in either alone.

The fence is the same one the heavy-physics chapters kept. The construction proves a finite
undecidability and a finite boundary reading that settles it, and that this pair has the shape of a
nonlocal correlation --- a fact decidable globally but not locally. It does not derive quantum mechanics,
build the correlated states, or reproduce Bell's inequality and its violation. What it offers is the
structural form: a definite global fact that no local interior reading can recover, the same form
nonlocality wears. The experiment on what cannot be said marks the edge from the other direction --- the
distinguished sentence is, to the interior, on the far side of the sayable, a truth the interior can
point at but not pronounce. Globally decidable, locally unsettleable, real at the boundary: the
construction recovers that shape and names it as a shape.

The reason the resemblance is worth marking, and not merely noting, is that it points at a common
structure beneath two phenomena usually kept far apart. Godel's undecidability is a fact about formal
systems; the nonlocal correlation of separated measurements is a fact about the physical world; and they
are rarely spoken of together. The construction's obstruction is a single object with both shapes at once:
it is a sentence the interior cannot decide, and it is a fact settled only by reading two sides together
rather than one side locally. That one finite object should wear both faces suggests the two limits ---
what a system cannot prove about itself, and what a local measurement cannot fix about a correlated whole
--- are versions of one limit, the limit on what any inside can know of a fact that lives in its relation
to an outside. The construction does not prove that identity for the world; it exhibits, in a finite case,
an object where the two limits are demonstrably the same limit. That is enough to make the resemblance
more than a pun and less than a theorem about physics.

\section{An indexed family}
\label{se:an-indexed-family}

One undecidable sentence might be dismissed as a curiosity, a single crack in an otherwise closed system.
The construction forecloses that reading by showing the sentence is not alone. There is a second
distinguished Godel sentence, indexed by the successor applied twice --- the number tower's two --- and
the construction proves it, too, reads the obstruction. Where there are two so indexed there is a family,
each member tanged out by its index from the construction's own naturals, each a distinguished sentence
the interior cannot settle, each reading the boundary obstruction. The incompleteness is not a lone
defect but a systematic feature, funged into one indexed family by the shared characteristic of reading
what the interior cannot decide.

This systematicity is the point worth carrying forward. The construction does not have an unfortunate
single blind spot; it has, built into the very richness that lets it describe itself, an indexed sequence
of sentences whose truth lives at its boundary and not in its interior. That the family is indexed by the
construction's own number tower closes a loop opened two chapters ago, when the naturals were rebuilt:
the same successor that lets the construction count is the one that enumerates the sentences it cannot
settle. To be rich enough to count is to be rich enough to harbor the indexed family --- the price and
the signature of a system that has become able to speak of itself.

The indexing repays one more look, because it makes the family something the construction can reason about
rather than merely possess. To tange a single undecidable sentence out of the system --- to point at one
and say \emph{this the interior cannot settle} --- would be a finding about that sentence. To have the
sentences indexed by the naturals is to be able to funge them: to pool the whole family under one
description, the sentences-the-interior-cannot-settle, and reason about the pool as an object. The
construction can quantify over the family, ask how it grows, locate the second member at the successor's
second step, and in principle walk the whole indexed sequence the way it walks any countable list. The
incompleteness is thereby not just present but \emph{addressable} --- the construction holds a handle on
its own blind spots, able to name them in order even as it remains unable to settle any of them. That a
system can catalog precisely what it cannot decide, in the order its own counting provides, is the last
and strangest form of the self-knowledge this part has traced.

\section{What is demonstrated, and what is assumed}
\label{se:demonstrated-assumed-ch27}

The accounting separates a concrete construction from a classical backdrop and a physical analogy.

What is demonstrated is the concrete sentence and its reach. The construction forms a specific
self-referential sentence in its own language and proves it says its own internal order. It marks the
self-referential property as distinguished and proves the distinguished sentence reads the boundary
obstruction. And it exhibits a second such sentence, indexed by the successor twice, proved to read the
obstruction as well, so that the undecidable sentences form an indexed family. Each is a checked fact
about the construction's own formal sentences.

\begin{quote}
\emph{A note on what is borrowed.} Two things here come from outside the construction's own proofs. The
first is the classical incompleteness metatheorem, which is long established and which the construction
does not re-prove --- it assumes the general result and contributes the concrete instance, the actual
sentence this device forms. The second is the resonance with quantum nonlocality: that the obstruction
is decidable globally but not locally has the shape of a nonlocal correlation, and is offered as that
shape, not as a derivation of quantum mechanics or a construction of nonlocal states. The proved core
between them is sharp --- a specific distinguished sentence, undecidable within, reading the obstruction
without, with a family of successors behind it --- and it owes nothing to either the metatheorem it cites
or the physics it resembles.
\end{quote}

What is assumed, then, is the standing grant, the cited classical metatheorem, and the nonlocality
analogy held as a shape. With the obstruction shown to be, on its interior face, an indexed family of
sentences the construction cannot settle from within, the outside reading has reached its sharpest form:
the construction can say what it cannot decide, and can say it precisely. The next chapter completes the
arc by restoring at the boundary the very distinction the opening chapters collapsed --- showing that for
a distinguished sentence, true and false read differently after all, and that the reader may choose how
much of that difference to demand.
