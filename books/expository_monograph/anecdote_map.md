# Anecdote Map

Purpose: mine the older `instrument` manuscript for visual examples that can
help readers see the Measurement grammar. These are not phenomenon blocks and
not historical digressions. Use them as small instrument-boundary miniatures:

```text
before β: distinction unavailable/blurry
β imposed: instrument/apparatus sets admissible surface
ε occurs: boundary crossing
μ remains: durable outcome
κ reckons: partial numeric order
ρ circulates: witness
φ enters: claim + receipt
limit: fact, not total reality
```

Reader-facing rule: anecdotes must serve the metaphysical grammar. They should
not import source-code/proof/build language, information-theory language, or
later physics before the local section has earned it. Use `compact_ledger.md`
as the Σ-key.

Fact/truth motive:

```text
τ may precede the instrument.
φ waits for ρ.
Galileo/Hooke form: the thing may be true before telescope/microscope; it
becomes fact for the ledger when the instrument yields repeatable receipts.
```

## Integration Notes

- One anecdote per numbered section is enough. Do not add one to every
  subsection.
- Prefer early anecdotes that are visually simple: telescope, microscope,
  speedometer, radar, thermometer.
- Reserve Volume/Chapter 6 material for later chapters. It is rich but imports
  minimality, variational language, splines, PDE shadows, Gibbs, and other
  machinery that would overload Chapters 1-2.
- If an anecdote needs more than one paragraph to explain, it is probably not
  ready for the local section.
- The best anecdotes are not "examples of the theory"; they are images that let
  the reader see the grammar becoming countable.
