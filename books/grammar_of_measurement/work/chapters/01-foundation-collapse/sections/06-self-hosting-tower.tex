\section{The Tower Reads Truth}

The construction so far has avoided a dangerous privilege. It has not
begun with identity. It has not begun with number as a completed object.
It has not begun with time as a background or truth as an oracle. It has
begun with permitted difference and has carried each later obligation
only when the earlier grammar made room for it. The tower is therefore
not a pile of assumptions. It is a chain of inherited permissions and
forbiddances.

Self-hosting begins when that chain is pointed at truth itself. The
grammar no longer asks only how a pressure, distance, signal, or trial
is read. It asks how the distinction between affirmation and denial is
read by the very grammar that made distinction possible. The move is
unavoidable once the tower claims to measure. A grammar of measurement
that cannot account for its own truth-conditions has left its foundation
outside the ledger.

The turn is severe because nothing may be added secretly at the top. The
same obligations must be carried upward: distinguishability before
sameness, count by wholes and parts, finite approach through residue,
clocked trial, bounded observation. Truth must enter under those rules
or not enter at all. The tower is not allowed to install a private
exception for the word ``true.'' If truth is measurable, it must be
readable by the grammar that measurement has earned.

This does not reduce truth to an ordinary sample. Rather, it asks what
must be true of the grammar for any sample to be read as true. The tower
has built carriers for difference, count, residue, repetition, and
bound. Now the whole stack becomes the object carried. The reader asks:
can affirmation and denial be separated using only the distinctions that
the foundation has permitted?

The answer cannot be borrowed from common speech. In common speech,
``true'' and ``false'' are already opposed. The grammar is not permitted
to accept that opposition as primitive. Common speech works downstream
from a shared world of identities, names, and practices. This chapter is
working upstream, where those identities have not yet been licensed. At
that level, affirmation and denial must present an interior difference
that the grammar can read. If they cannot, the grammar must say so.

The self-hosting tower also refuses another temptation: it does not make
the reader external in order to save the distinction. A reader certainly
appears later, and later chapters will learn how exterior readings
restore distinctions that the interior cannot settle. But the present
chapter is not allowed that rescue yet. It must ask what happens inside
the bare foundation, before an outside convention has been granted the
right to choose an orientation.

The pressure gathers here. Difference was permitted before sameness.
Counting split into whole and part. Finite approach carried residue.
The clock made repeatable trial possible. The horizon bounded what could
be observed. Now truth asks to be read. If the grammar finds a
difference, the tower can stand on it. If it does not, the tower will
collapse at precisely the point where ordinary reasoning expects the
firmest ground.

This is not a paradox imported for drama. It is the cost of honesty about
foundations. If sameness was not primitive, then truth cannot smuggle in
an identity of its own. If unavailable readings were not false, then
denial cannot be treated as the mere absence of affirmation. If
measurement is bounded, then no infinite survey can be invoked to decide
the matter from above. The grammar has cornered itself by obeying its
own rules. That is why the collapse, when it comes, is forced.
