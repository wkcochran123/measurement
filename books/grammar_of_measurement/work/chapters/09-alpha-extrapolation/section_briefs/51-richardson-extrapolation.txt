Section manager brief: beat 51 -- Richardson extrapolation
==========================================================

Beat:
Depth-doubling flips sampling error into a bounded estimate.

Section task:
Explain bounded Richardson-style extrapolation as a grammar of paired depths.
The trial reads at one depth, doubles the depth, compares the two estimates, and
uses the sign change of the leading sampling error to trap the extrapolated
reading. It does not assert convergence by faith.

Required manuscript moves:
- Coarse and doubled depths are both finite trials.
- The doubled trial must preserve the seed/residue obligations it refines.
- The leading sampling error must be read with opposite sign or paired bracket
  placement; otherwise extrapolation is not licensed.
- The result is a bounded estimate with an explicit unread remainder.
- The grammar forces extrapolation only where the two depths can be compared in
  the same aperture language.

Useful illustrations:
- Richardson coastline effect as a warning: refinement can change the measured
  path unless a residual law is stated.
- Finite precision and repeatability examples.
