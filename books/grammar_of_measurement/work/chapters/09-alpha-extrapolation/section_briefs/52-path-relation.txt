Section manager brief: beat 52 -- The path relation
===================================================

Beat:
The direct path and composed path have the same signed displacement but
different cost.

Section task:
Explain the composed-vs-direct relation. A path from mass to value may be
decomposed through an intermediate rung, or read directly. The grammar may
identify the signed displacement when the residues compose to the same depth.
It must still keep cost separate: the composed route may spend different
trial/settling work than the direct route.

Required manuscript moves:
- Signed displacement is a quotient of path endpoints and orientation.
- Path cost is the ledger of transitions spent to get there.
- Equal displacement does not imply equal cost.
- The composed path carries intermediate residue; the direct path bypasses it.
- Alpha must be recovered through the relation only when both displacement and
  cost have been accounted for.

Useful illustrations:
- Tail latency: the slowest reconciliation governs cost, not shortest path.
- Sagnac/holonomy: closed paths can share local data but differ by cyclic cost.
