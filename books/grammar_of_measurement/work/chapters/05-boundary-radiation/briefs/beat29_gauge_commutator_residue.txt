Section-manager brief: Beat 29
================================

Working title: Successor Residue

Beat:
- The gauge commutator residue: the commutator residue closes only through
  successor counting.

Required section result:
- Explain the commutator residue as the debt left when two admissible gauge
  transports do not commute.
- Tie closure to successor counting, not to a completed continuum or a
  free algebraic cancellation.
- The count must carry first transport, second transport, and their
  ordered comparison; the residue is read when the second successor is not
  interchangeable with the first.
- Closure means the grammar has counted enough to identify the residual
  loop and bound it.

Writer-sized fragments:
1. Define why gauge order matters after the extensional ledger.
2. Explain the commutator as two ordered readings of the same finite
   obligations.
3. Tie the residue to successor count: one step, second step, comparison.
4. Explain why closure is a countable close-out, not an interior shortcut.
5. Hand off to the completion norm.

Examples allowed:
- Aharonov-Bohm or Mach-Zehnder: closed comparison carries a residue even
  when local segments look silent.
- Echo or loop examples only as illustrations of ordered transport.
