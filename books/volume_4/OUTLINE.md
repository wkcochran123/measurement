## The arc in one sentence

Every representation system strong enough to describe its own describing
contains a room — a fixed-point residue whose truth-value does not matter,
which always shows up, at roughly a family-resemblant size, and which can
never be pooled across arguments — and this book earns that sentence clause
by clause from the cited literature, shows the reader the room in four
famous houses, and ends with the one instance this series carries.

## Chapters

### Ch 1 — The Quine (self-reproduction earned small)
- INTUITION: the sentence that writes itself; the mirror held to a mirror.
- CITED: a program that prints its own text exists in every reasonable
  language — the recursion theorem of Kleene (1938) as the guarantee; the
  name "quine" from Hofstadter (1979), honoring Quine's use-mention
  discipline. The quine's KERNEL: the part that does the self-carrying —
  the room at its smallest, and the first sighting of "roughly the same
  size" (every quine, in every language, pays a same-shaped overhead).
- FENCE: existence cited, not mystified; a quine is engineering, not magic.

### Ch 2 — The Diagonal (the one gesture under everything)
- INTUITION: the list that cannot contain its own describer; feed the
  description to itself and watch it flip.
- CITED: Cantor 1891 (the diagonal argument); Russell 1901 (the set of
  sets); Richard 1905 / the Berry sentence (told as the trade's cautionary
  cousins). The gesture named: apply a representation to itself, negate at
  the diagonal, and no listing survives.
- FENCE: the diagonal is a proof gesture, not a paradox-generator; the
  "paradoxes" are the gesture run in an unfenced language.

### Ch 3 — The Room in Arithmetic (Gödel 1931)
- INTUITION: the sentence that says "I am not provable" — and the honest
  shock: whether it is TRUE is not what the theorem needs.
- CITED: Gödel 1931 — arithmetization, the fixed-point (diagonal) lemma,
  incompleteness; the room's truth-value-doesn't-matter made precise:
  INDEPENDENCE (the room can be true or false relative to the house;
  the house stands either way; → the operator's first clause, paid here).
- FENCE: incompleteness fenced to what it states (formal systems of
  sufficient strength); the folk-readings (about minds, about physics)
  named and refused — the no-name-bridge at its most-needed.

### Ch 4 — The Unnameable (Tarski 1933)
- INTUITION: the house that cannot hold its own word for "true."
- CITED: Tarski 1933 (undefinability of truth); the semantic room as the
  syntactic room's twin; the hierarchy as the honest exit (truth named one
  house up — the tower the series' readers already know the feel of).
- FENCE: undefinability fenced to formal languages; no leap to natural
  language or the world.

### Ch 5 — The One Theorem (Lawvere 1969)
- INTUITION: four houses, one floor plan — the suspicion (Vol 3's Ch5
  gesture re-run at the logic level) that the rooms of Chs 1–4 are one
  construction.
- CITED: Lawvere 1969 — the categorical fixed-point theorem from which
  Cantor, Gödel, Tarski, and Russell all fall as instances; the
  family-resemblance clause EARNED here ("roughly the same size" = the
  shared diagonal core the theorem exhibits), and the "always shows up"
  clause earned as the theorem's generality.
- FENCE: unity as the CITED theorem's content this time (unlike Vol 3's
  Ch5, where unity was a reading — the difference stated: Lawvere PROVED
  this one; the volume says so and cites it, which is why this chapter can
  claim more than its sibling did).

### Ch 6 — The Rooms Never Merge (the fence chapter + the lens)
- INTUITION: two houses, two rooms, one floor plan — and no doorway
  between them. The same size is a resemblance of construction, not a
  shared room.
- CITED/EARNED: each fixed point is bound to its representation system —
  the Gödel sentence OF PA is not the Gödel sentence of ZF; the room
  re-forms per house, per argument (standard, cited to the logic
  literature's treatment of theory-relative independence). The operator's
  fence as the chapter's content: you cannot funge them across arguments —
  pooling residues erases exactly the binding that makes each argument its
  own. (The de-selection discipline, cited to the series' own record in
  one clause, method-not-apparatus.)
- THE LENS EXHIBIT (the volume's ONE device appearance, dose 1): the
  series' apparatus — whose description describes its own describing and
  pays a reproducible residue for the closed loop — offered as ONE
  CARRIED INSTANCE of the room: truth-value not the point, always shows
  up, its size ITS OWN. Fenced: illustration-not-identity; the device
  proves no general theorem; whether its residue could be earned as a
  fixed-point residue IN THE CITED SENSE is owed; and the non-funge law
  applied to the exhibit itself — the device's room is not "the" room.
