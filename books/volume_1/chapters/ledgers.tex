\section*{The one identification, described}

Chapter 2 permitted this argument exactly one act of identification and promised it
was located, on file, and pointable. The chapters could not describe it without
spending machinery they had not yet built; the back matter owes the description, and
here it is, in the book's own mathematical words.

The needle sits at the question of truth-sameness. Two descriptions arise in the
apparatus, each lawful, each parseable, and each provable from the other: whoever
holds the first can derive the second, and whoever holds the second can derive the
first. Mutual derivability is as close as proof can bring two texts --- and yet it
is not sameness of text: the two spell differently, parse differently, and no
finite comparison of their letters will ever certify that they say one thing,
because what they share is not their letters but their consequences, and
consequences are not a finite text. That is the genuinely undecidable question of
Chapter 2's description --- sameness invisible to every test the ground owns ---
and there, once, the needle comes down: descriptions that prove each other name
the same truth. One declaration, collapsing derivability-both-ways into identity,
recorded in the apparatus log as the second of its two entries. What the
coming-down bought, the reader has now seen end to end: every null test in the
book spends it (two spellings declared one content), and the only-if's deepest
verb --- the gauge value, the same --- is its direct purchase. One coin, minted at
the sharpest edge the argument meets, spent openly every time and never
duplicated.

\section*{The borrowed ideas}

Every row below was landed in a chapter, in prose, where the borrowed machinery
enters; this list is the gathering. Instruments, not authorities, throughout.

\begin{itemize}
\item Archimedes of Syracuse --- the polygon squeeze, \emph{Measurement of a
Circle}; the calibration exhibit is his experiment re-run (Ch.~5).
\item Euclid of Alexandria --- the division walk, remainder-and-swap (Ch.~4).
\item Moritz Stern and Achille Brocot --- the tree of mediant cuts; the
clockmaker needed the cheapest gears (Ch.~4).
\item John Farey --- the mediant's cheapest-interior-point discipline (Ch.~4).
\item Leonhard Euler and Joseph-Louis Lagrange --- continued fractions;
best-of-its-cost convergents (Ch.~4).
\item Isaac Newton; Broyden, Fletcher, Goldfarb, and Shanno --- descent steered
by accumulated curvature; the walk's runs realized as such a descent (Ch.~4).
\item Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet --- the pigeonhole; two boxes and
the closure of the ring (Chs.~2, 8).
\item Samuel Hunter Christie and Charles Wheatstone --- the bridge; inventor
first, perfecter second, the name stuck to the wrong man (Ch.~6).
\item J.~Willard Gibbs --- the ring at a hard cut; the kill test's charge and
the taper's reason (Chs.~5, 6).
\item W.~G. Unruh --- acceleration read as warmth; borrowed interpretation,
marked as such (Ch.~5).
\item Wolfgang Rindler --- the accelerated frame's geometry; borrowed
interpretation, marked as such (Ch.~5).
\item Maurice Fr\'echet and Ren\'e Gateaux --- the two derivatives; the
impossibility fence and the smooth joint (Chs.~7, 8).
\item Jacques Herbrand --- the ground built of the argument's own terms
(Ch.~8).
\item Henry Cavendish, with John Michell's apparatus --- the torsion balance;
the cellar, the wire, the telescope through the wall (Ch.~5).
\item Carl Friedrich Gauss --- the three-point rule: borrowed, load-bearing
once, superseded by the walk, and DEMOTED in the open to a marked
side-diagnostic. The row stands with its demotion, labeled: an honest ledger
keeps its demotions (Ch.~7).
\item The quotient tradition --- sameness minted by identification, taken with
its safety intact (Ch.~2).
\item The refusal of choice --- no oracle, no unnamed representative; taken as
a vow (Ch.~2).
\item The apparatus --- the terminating proof-checker in which every count was
taken: credited once, here, as apparatus, the way a paper names its
oscilloscope (Ch.~3).
\end{itemize}

Four names were considered and excluded, with reasons the record keeps: Lorentz
(an analogy's label in the prose, no borrowed machinery enters a carrier); Mott
and Cauchy (present in the wider work, touching no carrier of this argument);
Zipf (a different artifact's layer entirely); Picard (the two-pass shape
self-describes as quasi-Newton and is credited on that row). A wrong citation
is worse than a missing one; these four are the discipline applied to the
ledger itself.
