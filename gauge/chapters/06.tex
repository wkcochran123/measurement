\begin{gphenom}{Wittgenstein}
\label{ph:wittgenstein}
\GPhObs Wittgenstein argued in the \textit{Philosophical Investigations} that
``after'' is a grammatical rule, not a description of a causal force.
Saying ``B happened after A'' adds a syntactic relation to the record;
it does not describe a mechanism that pushed B into place.
\GPhCon Temporal order is a rule of the ledger, not a dynamical quantity.
It costs nothing to say B came after A once both have been recorded.
The ledger's irreversibility provides sequence; no force is required.
\GPhSeq The Wittgenstein Effect frees the compiler from having to derive
temporal order from dynamics. \lean{ObservationProcess.before} and
\lean{.after} are structural positions in the ledger, not caused states.
The arrow of time is a syntactic arrow, not a causal one.
\end{gphenom}

\begin{gphenom}{Cantor-Godel-Cohen}
\label{ph:cantor-godel-cohen}
\GPhObs Cantor showed that some infinities are strictly larger than others.
Godel showed in 1931 that any sufficiently powerful formal system contains
true statements it cannot prove. Cohen showed in 1963 that the continuum
hypothesis is independent of the axioms of set theory.
\GPhCon The ledger is necessarily incomplete: there exist admissible
continuations that cannot be derived from the current entries.
Consistency does not imply completeness.
\GPhSeq \lean{TRUTH}'s \lean{martyred?} predicate records the history of
forced revisions. The compiler does not require the ledger to be complete.
It requires only that each new entry be consistent with what is already there.
Incompleteness is not a defect; it is the honest report of the instrument.
\end{gphenom}

\begin{gphenom}{Hall-Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen}
\label{ph:hall-epr}
\GPhObs Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen showed in 1935 that quantum mechanics
implies correlations between separated particles that no local hidden-variable
theory can reproduce. Bell confirmed the bound in 1964. The correlations are real.
\GPhCon Two ledgers are uncorrelant if they cannot be jointly interpreted without
introducing events that neither has recorded. The EPR correlations are real
because both particles share an admissibility structure that neither ledger
alone contains.
\GPhSeq \lean{WITNESSED} models this boundary: a Gospel that has not been
witnessed in the current ledger is not false -- it is uncorrelant with the
current record. The compiler accepts both ledgers. It cannot merge them
without a witness.
\end{gphenom}
