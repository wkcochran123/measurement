% Some programs terminate; some do not; there is no general algorithm to

% PHYSICAL uses ChaitinsNumberSequence as its threshold. The physical

\begin{gphenom}{Chaitin}
\label{ph:chaitin}
\GPhObs Chaitin showed in 1974 that some real numbers are algorithmically
random: they contain no pattern shorter than themselves. No program can
generate them. They must be copied in full or not at all.
\GPhCon Some ledger entries are lawless -- they carry no compressible
structure. No instrument can predict them; no theory can derive them.
They must be witnessed directly.
\GPhSeq The compiler must accept lawless entries. \lean{ChaitinsNumberSequence}
is the honest acknowledgment: some programs never halt, and the ledger
must record that fact without being able to explain it.
\end{gphenom}

\begin{gphenom}{Schrodinger-Young}
\label{ph:schrodinger-young}
\GPhObs Young's double-slit experiment (1801) produces an interference pattern
even when particles are sent one at a time. Schrodinger's formalism (1926)
explains this: the particle carries structural correlation across both paths
simultaneously until the detector fires.
\GPhCon A single particle's ledger entry is not resolved until measurement.
\lean{Jar.superposition} is not temporary ignorance -- it is a first-class
ledger state that the compiler holds without resolving.
\GPhSeq The quantum measurement problem is a typeclass constraint:
\lean{GUNGAN} must be invoked to resolve a \lean{Jar} value before the
compiler can use it. Superposition is not a defect in the ledger.
It is a valid entry that has not yet been closed.
\end{gphenom}

\begin{gphenom}{Descartes}
\label{ph:descartes}
\GPhObs Descartes' coordinate grid (1637) imposes a discrete address on
continuous space. Every point becomes a pair of numbers. Continuity
is replaced by a countable index.
\GPhCon Quantization is the act of assigning a finite address to an otherwise
undifferentiated position. The instrument's resolution determines the
grid spacing; below that spacing, no distinction is admissible.
\GPhSeq The \lean{Metavariable} type is the compiler's Cartesian grid:
it assigns a finite index to the smallest representable distinction at
each level of the tower. The continuum does not precede the grid;
the grid is what makes the continuum measurable.
\end{gphenom}
