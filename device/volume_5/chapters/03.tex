\chapter{The Absence Of Truth In The Presence Of The Question}
\label{chap:absence-of-truth}

% Closed question: What remains when all the necessary names have been spoken?

The procession has ended at \texttt{INFERRED}, not at \texttt{TRUTH}.

A buyer who finishes the inspection has a public sentence. The sentence
licenses an action: buy, renegotiate, walk away. The sentence does not
own the car. The sentence does not predict the car's next fifty thousand
miles. The sentence says only what the record, this morning, under this
inspection, permits the buyer to say. A buyer who treats the sentence as
more than this has misread the procession.

The temptation to end at \texttt{TRUTH} is large. Truth is the word
that the project's other registers have all gestured at. The mathematical
gauge proves theorems; the theorems describe truths. The physical gauge
measures phenomena; the phenomena are what is the case. The algorithmic
gauge computes outputs; the outputs are what the program said. The
reference trace records the compiler's response; the compiler's response
is what Lean returned. Each of those registers can be told as a story
that ends at a captured truth.

\texttt{TRUTH} appeared at section 2.28, in the middle of the procession,
as one obligation among many. The procession kept moving after
\texttt{TRUTH}, through \texttt{WITNESSED}, \texttt{REAL},
\texttt{LOCAL}, \texttt{UNIVERSAL}, \texttt{LOGICAL}, \texttt{HALTED},
\texttt{MEASURED}, \texttt{COMPILED}, and \texttt{INFERRED}. None of
those was a softening of truth. Each was the next obligation that
truth must satisfy to remain trustworthy. By the time the procession
reached \texttt{INFERRED}, truth had already been disciplined into a
record-permitted sentence.

\section{Why \texttt{TRUTH} Is Not The Ending}
\label{se:why-truth-is-not-the-ending}

\texttt{TRUTH} appears in the procession, at section 2.28. It is one
name among many. It does not arrive last and it does not arrive alone.
That placement is a deliberate refusal of a familiar move.

The familiar move is to end at truth. A reader is led through a sequence
of admissions, observations, and tests, and the sequence closes by
saying that the disciplined process has reached truth. The sequence's
authority is then transferred to the closing word. The word inherits
the discipline of the procession that produced it.

The transfer is a trick. The discipline of the procession belongs to
the procession. It does not transfer cleanly to a single closing word.
A word that pretends to inherit the procession's authority becomes a
banner under which less disciplined claims travel. The banner is then
carried by people who never walked the procession. The banner becomes
propaganda.

A reader who hears the buyer is worth buying as the truth about the
car has stripped the conditional form away. The same words then travel
into contexts where the record has not been done. A neighbor hears the
truth about the car. The neighbor passes the claim to another buyer
for a different car. The other buyer, hearing the claim, treats it as
evidence about cars in general. The procession has been stripped at
every step. By the time the claim reaches the next buyer, it carries
social force but no record.

Truth appears, does its work as one of the carried obligations in the
procession, and hands off to \texttt{WITNESSED}, \texttt{REAL},
\texttt{LOCAL}, and \texttt{UNIVERSAL}. None of those is a softening
of truth. Each is the next obligation that truth must satisfy in order
to remain trustworthy. By the time the procession reaches
\texttt{INFERRED}, truth has already been disciplined into a record-
permitted sentence.

Truth is present as pressure throughout the procession. It is absent
as possession. The class name \texttt{TRUTH} does its work in section
2.28: it demands that the sentence carry a carriage history. The
carriage history is what allows the sentence to be checked, revised,
or refused. Without the carriage history, the sentence is unmoored.
With it, the sentence is a public artifact other people can engage
with.

The buyer's sentence the car is worth buying is dangerous if it
pretends to own the car's future. The car has a future the inspection
cannot see. The weather will change. The road salt will work on the
body. The buyer's commute will change with a new job. The transmission
may fail at sixty thousand miles despite the inspection's clean
compression numbers. The neighbor will buy a similar car and ask the
buyer for advice; the advice will travel beyond the buyer's specific
procession. The inspection's truth was the truth of this morning,
under these instruments, against these comparables, for this buyer's
plans. The truth does not extend to next year by itself.

The sentence becomes honest when it carries its record. Under this
inspection, this price, this buyer, this repair risk, the buyer may
infer worth. The sentence is less grand than the unhedged version. It
is more trustworthy. The trustworthiness comes from the willingness to
name the limits of the inferred sentence inside the sentence itself. A
reader who hears the hedged version is being told two things at once:
what the buyer concluded, and what the conclusion depends on. The
reader is then equipped to act on the conclusion or to ask for its
revision if the dependencies change.

A procession that ended at \texttt{TRUTH} would have made the buyer's
sentence unhedged. The unhedged sentence would have been louder. It
would also have been less defensible. A buyer who paid the offered
price and then discovered the car had a problem the inspection did not
catch would have nowhere to stand. The buyer would have to either
insist that the truth held despite the contrary evidence, refusing the
discipline the procession had been carrying, or admit that the truth
was overstated, admitting that the ending was not what the procession
had earned. Neither move is acceptable. The hedged sentence avoids the
bind by naming the scope at the start.

With the hedged sentence, the buyer stands on the inspection's actual
scope: I bought what the inspection said I was buying. The next
problem, if it arrives, is the next problem. The inspection is not a
warranty. The inspection's discipline covered what it covered. Problems
that emerge from outside that scope are not failures of the
inspection; they are events the inspection did not promise to predict.
The buyer's defense, if the buyer needs one, is that the inspection's
scope was clearly stated and that the buyer acted within that scope.
The defense is available only because the procession preserved the
scope through every step.

\texttt{INFERRED} is the right ending because inference is what
disciplined records produce. Possession is what they refuse to produce.
The procession ends at inference because the procession's discipline
ends at inference. To end elsewhere would have been to break the
discipline the procession had been carrying since the first
\texttt{Fact}.

A reader who finds the ending unsatisfying may be looking for a
different kind of artifact than the procession produces. A procession
that ends at \texttt{TRUTH} would be promising more than the
procession can deliver. The honesty is in the delivery. The procession
produces inferences. Inferences are what the reader gets. A reader who
wanted ownership of truth has not been given that.

\section{The Question Remains}
\label{se:the-question-remains}

The final artifact is not a trophy. It is a disciplined question.

What can be distinguished. What can be admitted. What can be counted,
encoded, compared, represented, observed, measured, compiled, and
inferred. Each verb is a question the record has answered for the
present moment. None of those questions is closed forever.

The question remains because the system is honest. A dishonest rhetoric
would turn the procession into conquest. The class names would be
presented as the steps by which a project conquered uncertainty and
reached mastery. The reader would then be invited to believe that the
project has done something final.

The procession turns into permission instead. The class names license
a public sentence the speaker may speak. They do not license a claim
that the speaker has captured the field. The field remains. The
question remains.

The difference between conquest and permission is operationally
significant. A reader who hears the procession as conquest will treat
the buyer's sentence as a final verdict. A reader who hears the
procession as permission will treat the buyer's sentence as a licensed
conclusion that may be revised. The first reader will be surprised by
failure; the second reader will expect to revisit the conclusion as
conditions change. Honest argument prefers readers who expect revision.

For the car, the question remains in a specific and pragmatic way. The
buyer may infer worth this morning. By next month, the car will have
new miles, new weather exposure, new repair history. The question may
need to be asked again. The earlier inference was honest because it
was limited. The new inference, if it comes, will also be honest
because it will also be limited. The procession does not finish the
car. The procession finishes this morning's sentence. Tomorrow's car
will be a different car; tomorrow's sentence will be a different
sentence; tomorrow's procession will run from \texttt{Fact} through
\texttt{INFERRED} again, with new inputs.

The honest public form is not we solved the car. It is this is what
can be inferred now. The shift from solved to inferred is small in
print and large in implication. A solved car is final; an inferred
car is current. A buyer who tells the neighbor I solved the car has
overstated. A buyer who tells the neighbor I inferred that the car is
worth buying this morning has been honest.

A gauge does not own the thing it reads. A speedometer does not own
speed. It produces a reading that, under its calibration, the driver
may use. The reading is good for the moment. The next moment may need
a new reading. The instrument continues, the world continues, the
gauge keeps producing readings until the world or the gauge stops. A
driver who treats the speedometer's display as the truth about speed
has not understood the speedometer. A driver who treats the display
as the current reading under the speedometer's calibration has
understood both the display and the speedometer.

The procession's job is the same. It produces a sentence the speaker
may use. The sentence is good for the inspected record. The next
inspected record may produce a different sentence. The procession
continues, the records continue, the names keep doing their work as
long as the speakers keep speaking under them. The procession's
authority is its continuing application. A procession that has been
forgotten or abandoned no longer produces inferences. The discipline
must be carried forward, not stored in past honors.

A reader who closes the book and asks whether the buyer should have
bought the car has missed the point. The question is not should this
specific buyer have bought this specific car. The question is what
procession does the buyer's sentence rest on, and is that procession
one a reader is willing to act under. Different readers will reach
different conclusions about which processions are worth acting under.
The procession does not adjudicate. It walks one procession and shows
what that procession licenses.

The question of which processions are valid is itself a public
question. A community of readers can debate whether a particular
procession captures the obligations that matter for a particular kind
of decision. The debate is open. The procession is not eternal. The
names may be revised, the order may be reconsidered, the obligations
may be adjusted. The debate continues because the question remains.

\section{Inference Without Ownership}
\label{se:inference-without-ownership}

\texttt{INFERRED} is weaker than ownership and stronger than opinion.

It is weaker than ownership because the speaker does not claim to
possess the thing inferred. The buyer does not own the car's
mechanical future. The inspector does not own the road salt. The
procession does not own next year. The speaker has produced a sentence
about the present record. The future will produce its own records and
its own inferences.

It is stronger than opinion because the sentence is licensed by a
public procession. The speaker is not voicing a personal preference.
The speaker is reading aloud what the disciplined record permits to
be said. Another disciplined reader, with access to the same record,
would produce a similar sentence. The sentence has the kind of public
weight that opinion lacks.

The space between ownership and opinion is where inference lives. The
space is harder to maintain than either of the alternatives. Ownership
offers the comfort of finality: the matter is settled, the speaker
has authority, no further work is required. Opinion offers the comfort
of personal license: the speaker says what the speaker thinks, and no
one can demand a procession behind the saying. Inference offers neither
comfort. The speaker must have done the procession, refusing the
opinion comfort, and must acknowledge that the procession does not
capture everything, refusing the ownership comfort. The discipline is
demanding.

The demand is the point. Public arguments that move people toward
action need to be neither personal preference nor captured truth.
They need to be conditional, supported, and revisable. The class-name
procession is one offer of that form. The procession produces
inferences. The inferences are what the public can act on. The action
remains the speaker's responsibility, even when the inference
licenses it.

People who read the inferred sentence do not need to be told that
Truth has been captured for them. They need to be shown what a record
permits, what it does not permit, and what must remain open. The
shown record carries the same weight whether the reader believed the
speaker or not. The reader can re-inspect the record. The reader can
disagree with the inference and have a footing to stand on while
doing so.

A reader who disagrees with the buyer's worth buying inference can
challenge specific links in the procession. The reader can question
whether the comparable set was correctly defined. The reader can
question whether the repair-risk weighting matches the buyer's actual
use case. The reader can question whether the threshold for walk-away
was reasonable. Each challenge points at a specific link. Each
challenge is itself a procession move: the reader is applying the
same discipline to the buyer's inference that the buyer applied to
the seller's claims. The discipline is portable across speakers.

The absence of Truth as a possessed object is not a failure. It is
the condition of honest inquiry in the presence of the question.
Honest inquiry produces inferences; it does not produce ownership of
facts. A field that thinks it has produced ownership has stopped
inquiring. The field's discipline depends on the continuing question.
The class names' discipline depends on the continuing procession.

The buyer can walk away from the car without claiming the car is
cursed. The buyer can buy the car without claiming the car will never
fail. Both moves are inference without ownership. Each is a public
action licensed by the record and bounded by the question. A buyer
who later finds a problem the inspection missed has not been betrayed
by the procession. The procession was always honest about its scope.
A buyer who finds the inspected items in good order years later has
not been vindicated by truth. The procession was always honest about
its limits.

Inference without ownership is not a weaker version of having the
truth. It is the form discipline takes when discipline refuses to
lie. The class names do not give the speaker the world. They give the
speaker permission to speak under what the world has let into the
record. The speaking is not small. It is what public argument actually
is when public argument is being honest about its scope.

A reader who walks away from the procession carries the class-name
vocabulary into other settings. The vocabulary may travel as the
procession's actual structure, if the reader applies the discipline
to other decisions, or as a polished slogan, if the reader strips the
procession and quotes the names. The procession cannot prevent the
stripping. The procession can only show the discipline in its honest
form. Whether the reader carries the discipline or the slogan is the
reader's choice.
